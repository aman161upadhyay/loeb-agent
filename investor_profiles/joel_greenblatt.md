# Joel Greenblatt — special situations and systematic good-business bargains

Comparative profile, 2026-10-25. Keep three implementations separate: early concentrated Gotham special situations; the retail “Magic Formula”; and later diversified institutional long/short strategies. Their portfolio rules are not interchangeable. [1–3]

## Philosophy, sourcing and valuation

“In the stock market, knowing where to ‘bang’ is the secret to your fortune.” The publisher's excerpt from *You Can Be a Stock Market Genius* makes the informational point: profitable work can consist of knowing where mispricing is likely, not predicting the entire economy. Corporate events produce forced or indifferent sellers, neglected securities and accounting complexity. Research the actual documents, incentives and capital structure. [1]

Search spinoffs, restructurings, rights offerings, merger securities, recapitalizations, bankruptcies and selected arbitrages. Establish enterprise value, normalized earnings and incentives; identify why an institution might sell regardless of value. A spinoff is a search category, not an automatic bargain. The later Magic Formula combines a high earnings yield (EBIT relative to enterprise value) and high return on capital (EBIT relative to tangible capital employed). It aims to buy good businesses cheaply, not merely stocks with low price/book. Financials and utilities require special treatment and are excluded in the standard book implementation. [1–2]

## Construction, duration, exits and avoidance

The early book explains why detailed knowledge can support a relatively small portfolio, while acknowledging market-wide risk remains even with thousands of stocks. The Magic Formula instead relies on a diversified basket, staggered purchases and approximately annual rebalancing, maintained through multi-year underperformance. Later Gotham portfolios are much broader. Never attach the systematic basket's annual sale rule to every discretionary special situation. [1–3]

For a special situation, sell when value is realized, the event no longer offers attractive risk/reward, or the thesis fails. For the formula, follow the chosen implementation rather than cherry-pick names or stop after a bad year. Avoid dependence on macro prediction, superficial screens, leverage hidden in a post-spinoff balance sheet and event labels without incentives/valuation work. [1–2]

## Famous decisions / teaching cases and stated reasoning

The books teach **cases**, not a complete audited record of his fund's ownership; where personal transaction detail is absent, these are not represented as proven purchases.

| Case | Analytical reason |
|---|---|
| Marriott separation, announced 1992 | Separate the management/franchise economics from heavily indebted hotel assets; understand the capital structure and insider incentives rather than dismiss an ugly-looking spinoff. [1,4] |
| Sears / Dean Witter / Allstate separations, early 1990s | Use independently observable subsidiary values to assess the implied price of the remaining retail business. Teaching accounts emphasize simple sum-of-the-parts arithmetic rather than a heroic retail forecast. [1,5] |
| American Express / Lehman separation, 1994 | Institutional selling and different businesses can create a research opportunity; value the distributed security independently. Not every security received by a large shareholder suits that shareholder's mandate. [1] |
| Publishing the Magic Formula, 2005 | Translate good-business/cheap-price principles into a reproducible basket for people unable to perform intensive special-situation analysis. This is a strategy-design decision, not a stock pick. [2] |

## Mistakes and what changed

Greenblatt's Florida Cypress Gardens merger-arbitrage loss is a concrete example. In an interview with William Green, he recalls buying a substantial position for the deal spread; before closing, a sinkhole damaged the property. The acquisition was recut at a lower price and he lost money. The lesson is that apparently straightforward event spreads retain unforeseen downside and size must allow survival. The interview does not supply the original date, price, exact loss or a newly adopted numerical size cap; leave those unknown. [6]

The later broad approach is a documented change in implementation, but do not assert that this one loss “caused” it. In a 2016 interview Greenblatt warns that investors often abandon an approach after poor performance, making an otherwise sound strategy unworkable. His books acknowledge that value can take years to appear in prices and formula baskets include losers. Nor should a third-party backtest be relabeled Gotham's realized return: concentrated historical returns, book simulations and later live funds measure different things. [1–3]

## How an agent must be prompted differently

**Proposed instruction:** “Choose discretionary special situations or systematic basket mode before analyzing. In discretionary mode, inspect event documents, forced sellers, insider incentives, enterprise value and downside liabilities. In systematic mode, apply the specified rankings and rebalance without narrative cherry-picking. State why the market participant selling need not be irrational.” Relative to Ackman, do not require a public campaign, a large company or a permanent franchise; structural neglect can supply the edge. [1–3; prompt is synthesis]
