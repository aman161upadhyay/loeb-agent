# Benjamin Graham — valuation discipline before prediction

Comparative profile, 2026-10-25. Graham's defensive-investor rules, enterprising-investor strategies and actual partnership decisions are related but not identical.

## Philosophy, sourcing and valuation

“Margin of safety” is the organizing principle of chapter 20 of *The Intelligent Investor*. An investment requires analysis, protection of principal and an adequate return; speculation should be labeled and segregated rather than disguised. Mr. Market supplies optional transaction prices, not instructions about business value. Graham's defensive investor does not need to forecast the economy or identify the next exceptional company. [1–2]

Search balance sheets, security terms, neglected filings, liquidation opportunities, arbitrages and unusually cheap earnings. *Security Analysis* separates asset backing, normalized earning power and senior-security coverage; the appropriate method depends on the claim being purchased. Net-current-asset value deducts **all liabilities** from current assets; a discount to book equity is not the same test. Treat accounting values skeptically and distinguish temporary distress from permanent impairment. Defensive screens in the revised book emphasize size, financial condition, earnings stability, dividends, growth and moderate valuation, not a universal single low-P/E cutoff. [1–2]

## Construction, duration, exits and avoidance

Diversification protects against errors in individual bargains; the defensive common-stock discussion recommends a meaningful basket rather than all capital in one attractive statistic. Portfolio stock/bond allocation varies within explicit bounds, with a neutral balance available to investors unable to judge extremes. Enterprising operations can include negotiated or activist situations. Graham did not require every investment to be a passive minority stock. [1]

Hold until value is realized, the analytical gap closes or the investment basis changes; do not confuse this with Buffett's later preference to retain exceptional compounders indefinitely. Graham's late-life interview favors simple, tested group approaches and acknowledges competition in detailed security analysis. Avoid leverage that makes survival depend on markets, unsupported growth expectations, inadequate senior-security coverage and paying a speculative price while claiming investment safety. [1–3]

## Decisions and stated reasoning

| Decision | Reasoning and evidentiary status |
|---|---|
| Northern Pipeline, 1920s | Research into separately disclosed bond assets exposed excess capital; Graham pressed for distribution and used shareholder governance. This is an early activist example, not simply buying low P/E. Account in his memoir; archival narrative in Gramm. [4–5] |
| GEICO, 1948 | Graham-Newman bought a substantial interest in an unusual direct insurer; the *Intelligent Investor* postscript describes a concentrated success that exceeded gains from many other operations. A deliberately exceptional case, not evidence his ordinary diversification rules never mattered. [1,6] |
| A&P, late 1930s example | Chapter 8 uses depressed market prices relative to liquid resources and earning power to show why quotations can disconnect from enterprise value. Treat it as Graham's analytical case study; the book is not a complete dated transaction ledger. [1] |

## Mistakes and what changed

Graham's late interview recalls owing money through 1929–32 and explicitly calls that a mistake he did not repeat. Cheap securities did not immunize leveraged capital against a collapse. This supports his later stress on survivability; avoid inventing a precise loss percentage from inconsistent popular accounts. [3]

GEICO exposes a productive tension: an outlier quality investment made an extraordinary contribution to an investor known for diversified statistical bargains. The postscript itself recognizes the exceptional outcome and the mixture of judgment and favorable fortune. It does not justify retroactively attributing a Buffett-like “quality at any price” philosophy to Graham. Nor should regulatory distribution of GEICO shares be confused with a discretionary sale based on deteriorating economics. [1,6]

## How an agent must be prompted differently

**Proposed instruction:** “First classify investment versus speculation and defensive versus enterprising mandate. Establish verifiable asset backing, earnings coverage and normalized profits; require a substantial price cushion. Build a basket when the edge is statistical. Do not require activism, but examine realizability of surplus assets. Do not substitute a long growth narrative for evidence.” Against Ackman, reduce dependence on a handful of durable large-cap franchises and personally engineered catalysts; against Buffett, make explicit when buying an ordinary asset cheaply is sufficient. [1–5; prompt is synthesis]
