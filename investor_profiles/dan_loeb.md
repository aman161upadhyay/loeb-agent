# Dan Loeb — event-driven value with flexible implementation

Comparative profile, 2026-10-25. Separate Third Point's activist equity cases from its broader credit, private and non-activist investments.

## Philosophy, sourcing and valuation

Third Point's 2011 Yahoo letter identifies a value-oriented approach and hidden asset value that better governance could unlock. “It is time for new leadership at Yahoo.” That is a proposed mechanism of realization, not merely a declaration that the stock is cheap. Third Point's broader description includes opportunistic equity, corporate/structured/private credit and venture strategies; Loeb should not be reduced to an activist-only template. [1–2]

Search corporate events, mismanaged assets, capital-allocation opportunities, misunderstood growth and stressed securities. Valuation is situation-specific: sum-of-the-parts for conglomerates, normalized operating earnings and margin comparisons for companies with execution gaps, recovery/contract analysis for credit. Yahoo's cash and Asian holdings, Sony's disparate businesses and Nestlé's portfolio illustrate different routes to the gap. A catalyst may be an asset sale, leadership change, buyback or restructuring rather than a permanent operating transformation. [1,3–4]

## Construction, duration, exits and avoidance

Construction is flexible across strategies rather than a single public-equity count. Activist holdings can be important positions while credit and other exposures change gross/net risk; a 13F is incomplete. Holding periods depend on catalyst, value realization and changing facts. Sony's 2014 exit after rejection of the entertainment proposal and later 2019 re-entry demonstrate that the company name alone does not define one timeless thesis. There is no verified universal numerical stop or holding-period rule in the cited public material. [2–3,5]

Avoid paying for value that cannot be unlocked, treating corporate complexity as an automatic bargain, or persisting with a restructuring after superior evidence emerges. These are **process implications** from the cases, not a quoted complete Loeb exclusion checklist. Unlike Ackman's later emphasis on predictable cash flows and a small portfolio, Third Point's disclosed platform leaves greater scope for event, sector and capital-structure flexibility. [1–5]

## Decisions and stated reasoning

| Decision | Contemporary rationale |
|---|---|
| Yahoo, September 2011 | Cash, Alibaba/Yahoo Japan interests and core operations supported hidden value; board/leadership failures justified governance changes. The letter separates asset valuation from management critique. [1] |
| Nestlé, June 2017 | Improve margins, sharpen portfolio, repurchase shares and monetize the L'Oréal interest. This combines operating and financial activism; reported requests are not all equivalent to adopted actions. [4] |
| Sony, June 2019 | Third Point proposed separating semiconductors and simplifying the portfolio. Sony's September response disputed the recommendation and defended strategic integration—a primary counterthesis, not just campaign publicity. [3] |
| Disney, October 2020 | Redirect dividend resources into Disney+ content to accelerate streaming scale. This is investment in growth rather than a stereotypical demand for immediate payout. [6] |
| Disney, August–September 2022 | Proposed ESPN separation and other changes; soon withdrew the ESPN demand after stating a better understanding of its strategic potential. Do not collapse this into the distinct 2020 thesis. [7] |

## Mistakes and what changed

The ESPN reversal is a documented analytical revision, not necessarily proof of a realized investment loss. Loeb's willingness to abandon the spinoff demand is useful evidence against prompting an activist to defend every public proposal permanently. Conversely, an unsuccessful demand does not prove the entire investment failed: Sony did not accept the 2013 entertainment proposal and Loeb exited in 2014, but a campaign outcome and security return are different metrics. [5,7]

The record supports flexibility and updating; it does **not** establish a newly imposed universal portfolio rule following these episodes. A fuller mistakes audit should use dated Third Point annual/quarterly letters and distinguish exposure losses from campaign reversals. Avoid inventing specific growth-stock losses from venture holdings without the actual investor letter. [2,5,7; explicit research boundary]

## How an agent must be prompted differently

**Proposed instruction:** “Search broadly across events and capital structures. Identify the realizable valuation gap and best catalyst; compare a growth investment, financial restructuring and operating intervention. Model the company's strongest rebuttal. Update or withdraw individual demands without treating retreat as personal failure.” Relative to Ackman, do not require a durable large-cap franchise, CEO installation or decade-long ownership; a finite catalyst-driven trade can suffice. [1–7; prompt is synthesis]
