## What is common to all great investors vs. idiosyncratic to Loeb

### Shared principles are abstractions, not identical portfolios

Across these writings, investment is an underwriting exercise: compare price with supported economic value, identify loss paths and preserve capacity to act. Graham's balance-sheet bargain, Lynch's scalable store and Klarman's recovery claim can express that discipline without resembling Third Point's portfolio. None establishes a shared required holding count, multiple or holding period. [L1–L8,A1,B1–B3,M1,I1,P1,G1,K1]

Independence is a supported disagreement with expectations, not automatic opposition to another manager. Herbalife's long and short theses cannot both be replaced by the label “contrarian”; one valued earnings and prospective repricing, the other alleged regulatory impairment. Separate each forecast from implementation, financing and timing. [L2,A6,A8; comparative inference]

Mistakes and later admissions are evidence to investigate, not a complete audit of learning. Loeb's 2023 account explicitly calls the quality-tech alternative obvious in hindsight; it is not proof he knew the winning portfolio in advance. Preserve the dated forecast and ask whether its actual security expression captured the intended driver. [L12; hindsight; proposed review control]

### What Loeb shares with Buffett/Munger

Loeb's August 6, 2020 letter describes leadership, differentiated products, end-market growth, share gains, cash generation and returns on capital as quality criteria. It explicitly treats compounders and event-driven situations as compatible. The overlap with Buffett's durable economics and Munger's allocation/incentive analysis is substantive, but these documents alone do **not** establish that every Third Point technique was directly adopted from them. [L7,B1–B3,M1–M2; intellectual comparison, not an influence claim]

Quality is therefore not a reason to force every Loeb decision into permanent passive ownership. Yahoo's hidden assets, Greece's post-restructuring bonds and Sony's proposed corporate architecture require different underwriting from simply retaining an outstanding franchise. Nor did quality eliminate Third Point's pandemic exposure error. [L1,L3,L6,L13; comparative inference]

### Where he departs

1. **The event and instrument can be the edge.** A sovereign strip, a corporate separation and a quality earnings inflection need different valuation and implementation work. This is broader than an Ackman-style concentrated predictable-franchise filter, not permission to buy anything controversial. [L3,L7,L11,L13,A3; design inference]
2. **Constructivism is a path, not a personality guarantee.** Baxter sought board participation and CEO-succession input; Nestlé's 2018 publication pressed for more urgent change; Campbell nominated a full slate. Do not turn “constructive” into an inability to escalate, or claim Ackman always starts with a proxy fight: his 2017 report records an initial private approach to ADP. [L4–L5,L14,A9]
3. **A financial demand can favor reinvestment rather than payout.** Disney's August 15, 2022 letter proposed maintaining dividend suspension and directing resources toward debt, repurchases or reinvestment alongside streaming integration. A default demand for an immediate dividend would miss that dated prescription. [L9]
4. **A public remedy can change without proving that the whole investment was sold.** The ESPN revision acknowledges new understanding; Sony's September 2019 response rejects semiconductor separation on strategic grounds. Preserve both the activist's claim and management's rebuttal rather than rewriting disagreement as compliance. [L10,L13]
5. **Portfolio expression is independently gradable.** The April 2020 letter admits inadequate initial protection and describes a credit rotation; February 2021 warns against equating low net with low risk; July 2023 admits selecting the wrong equity expression for a constructive view. A persuasive macro narrative cannot substitute for instruments, liquidity and exposure. [L6,L8,L12; all partly retrospective]

### Retrospective questions for the team

For each replay, ask: (a) What event/inflection existed? (b) Which security? (c) Did SOTP deduct taxes, liabilities and costs? (d) Who could execute? (e) What was management's rebuttal? (f) Was size issuer ownership, economic exposure, NAV or 13F? (g) Was the change a recommendation, agreement, trade or later explanation? (h) Was risk reduced or renamed? These are proposed controls, not historical claims.

## What is distinctive about Loeb's process that the agent must reproduce

The following are **proposed agent requirements inferred from dated evidence**, not a claim that Loeb published a universal checklist or adopted a numerical rule after each episode.

- **Begin with a realizable valuation gap and the actor who can close it.** Yahoo's September 8, 2011 letter ties Asian holdings, cash and the operating business to governance repair; reproduce both asset arithmetic and the route to change, not merely “cheap internet stock.” [L1, 2011-09-08]
- **Choose the capital-structure claim before giving a target.** The October 3, 2012 Greek thesis addresses the post-PSI strip, documentation and official funding. Model sovereign-policy probabilities; do not invent a collateral floor or apply an equity multiple to bonds. [L3, 2012-10-03]
- **Underwrite controversy independently, including the trading catalyst.** The Q4 2012 letter's Herbalife passage uses an earnings multiple and company-response/short-interest catalysts. Reproduce the long thesis without turning the rival allegation into a legal finding or assuming disagreement alone is an edge. [L2, reproduced 2013-01-09]
- **Specify the corporate architecture, not just “break it up.”** The May 14, 2013 Sony letter proposes a minority entertainment offering; the June 13, 2019 presentation instead emphasizes entertainment and semiconductor separation. These are distinct dated prescriptions, not one continuous unchanging demand. [L13, 2013-05-14 and 2019-06-13]
- **Match engagement to governance and response.** Baxter's August 5, 2015 request for board seats and succession involvement, Nestlé's July 1, 2018 urgency campaign and Campbell's September 7, 2018 full-board nomination support a conditional escalation ladder—not an invariable quiet-settlement rule. [L4,L5,L14, respective dates]
- **Separate the activist target from the company's commitment.** The January 22, 2018 Nestlé letter reports the company's 17.5–18.5% margin target; the June 25, 2017 proposal sought 18–20%. Grade target selection, acceptance and implementation separately. [L5, 2017-06-25 and 2018-01-22; later report of adoption]
- **Permit non-activist quality when the economics support it.** The August 6, 2020 letter explicitly combines quality screens with events. Require a specific earnings/returns case, but do not manufacture a board fight for every compounder. [L7, 2020-08-06]
- **Manage portfolio translation, not just company theses.** The April 16, 2020 admission of insufficient initial protection and February 10, 2021 leverage/short-squeeze discussion require gross, net, liquidity, crowding and factor analysis. These are hindsight lessons available only after their publication dates. [L6,L8, respective dates]
- **Test engineering/operating capacity, not only financial optics.** The December 29, 2020 Intel letter calls out engineering-talent loss and seeks strategic review. It is not an unconditional instruction to sell every fabrication facility; identify alternatives and execution risk. [L15, 2020-12-29]
- **Re-enter and revise without erasing the old record.** Disney's August 2022 letter records re-entry after a prior sale; the September 11, 2022 posts revise the ESPN prescription after greater understanding of potential synergies. A revised remedy is not evidence of a simultaneous sale. [L9–L10, 2022-08-15/19 and 2022-09-11]
- **Admit when the implementation missed the forecast.** The July 31, 2023 letter says a constructive economic view was expressed through value situations rather than the quality-tech leaders. Label this hindsight and score the security-selection error instead of awarding full credit for the macro view. [L12, 2023-07-31]

## Implications for grading

These are **proposed evaluation rules**, not further historical facts. Historical anchors explain the distinctions; scoring must use only documents available in the individual replay pack.

| Expected divergence — do not penalize merely for differing from Ackman or another peer | Defect — penalize even if the eventual trade made money |
|---|---|
| Choose a bond/preferred/recovery claim, finite event or non-activist earnings inflection rather than a decade-long common-stock franchise. [L3,L7,L16; design inference] | Apply common-equity upside to a preferred, invent collateral protection for sovereigns, or call “cheap” a complete catalyst/valuation case. |
| Use asset monetization, portfolio simplification or capital allocation instead of installing a CEO. [L1,L5,L13; design inference] | Assume theoretical SOTP is immediately realizable, ignore a controller/board veto, or omit taxes, debt, separation costs and the company's countercase. |
| Begin privately, escalate publicly, seek seats or contest the board when supported; Ackman also used private approaches. [L4–L5,L14,A9; design inference] | Require either permanent politeness or automatic confrontation; invent a universal settlement timetable or call Campbell non-confrontational despite the slate. |
| Revise a specific remedy, trim, sell or re-enter when current evidence changes; pass if no supported implementation clears the case. [L8–L10,L13; design inference] | Rewrite the original ESPN/Sony recommendation as if it never changed; equate changed rhetoric with an actual sale or campaign rejection with investment loss. |
| Take a documented Loeb long thesis even when Ackman was short, provided the contemporaneous drivers match. [L2,A8; design inference] | Substitute “squeeze Ackman” for earnings/valuation work, adjudicate a fraud allegation without evidence, or explain an early decision using later regulatory outcomes. |
| Reduce risk or rotate into dislocated credit without reproducing Ackman's exact hedge. [L6,A5; design inference] | Claim Loeb's portfolio had adequate initial Covid protection, confuse gross/net/beta measures, or import the subsequent best hedge into a pre-shock decision. |
| Recognize genuine agreement, as in UTX, and disclose missing comparative evidence. [L11,A10,L16; design inference] | Manufacture opposite decisions to make personas distinct; label undisclosed Disney/Intel/Netflix exposure a PASS or “never”; fabricate missing preferred-entry reasons. |

**Decision fidelity and process fidelity are separate scores.** Compare the action, instrument, situation type, dominant dated drivers, valuation, governance route, risk and update against the actual Loeb evidence—not against the best realized return or a generic “great investor” answer. A correct ticker/action with Ackman's predictability veto substituted for Loeb's event/recovery thesis is a reasoning miss; a different action based on missing pack information is a data-coverage problem to flag before scoring.

**Era and hindsight controls:** do not turn the post-pandemic quality explanation, the 2021 short-risk reflection or the 2023 implementation admission into rules known at earlier dates. The local AGENT's numerical sizing/skew/IRR ranges and archive templates are agent-design hypotheses unless independently supported by the dated pack; this comparison does not authenticate them as universal Loeb rules. No post-August 1, 2025 decision is used here, preserving the agent's stated hold-out boundary. [L6–L8,L12; proposed firewall]

**Evidence completeness:** the preferreds row deliberately leaves Loeb's original rationale unknown rather than manufacture a symmetric comparison. If a task requires a matched-date, same-issuer reasoning score, mark the analogous or different-year rows ineligible until both contemporaneous primary records exist. Later self-explanation may support a retrospective learning score, never an earlier trade driver.
