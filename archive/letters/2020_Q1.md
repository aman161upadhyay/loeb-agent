# L12 — Q1 2020: concentrated equities were not adequately protected

**Date:** 2020-04-16. Era C. [Primary letter](https://d2gr5kl7dt2z3t.cloudfront.net/blog/wp-content/uploads/securepdfs/2020/04/20095042/Third-Point-Q1-2020-Investor-Letter.pdf?mod=article_inline).

**Failure/evidence:** Offshore lost about 16% during Q1. Top twenty positions represented roughly 85% of NAV entering the crisis; aerospace exposure was inadequately hedged against coronavirus losses. Concentrated quality/event theses did not immunize the portfolio to a discontinuous demand shock.

**Action/sizing:** equity net falls from 70% to 58%, beta-adjusted 65% to 48%; corporate/sovereign credit rises from 9% to 20% and structured credit from 15% to 27%. Campbell is exited. Private exposure is reduced below 10% of assets. These snapshots are not interchangeable with gross exposure.

**Credit valuation/catalyst:** spread normalization over 12–18 months is the general bond-evaluation template. Liquidity disruption, government relief and eventual stabilization create prospective returns; payment deferrals/defaults remain risks.

**Quote:** the strategy can “toggle between asset classes, sectors, and geographies.”

**Exit/disconfirmation:** failure of liquidity support, persistent cash-flow impairment or inadequate collateral can defeat a normalization thesis. The letter's willingness to act in crisis should be read together with its admission of inadequate initial protection, not used to erase the drawdown.
