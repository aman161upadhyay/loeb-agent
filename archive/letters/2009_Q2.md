# L02 — Q2 2009: credit survivors, not indiscriminate beta

**Information date:** **2009-07-31**, verified on the [original letter PDF hosted by CNBC](https://fm.cnbc.com/applications/cnbc.com/resources/editorialfiles/2012/05/03/2226591_ThirdPoint2Q.pdf). Era B. [August 4 reproduction](https://www.cnbc.com/2009/08/04/daniel-loebs-third-point-q2-investor-letter.html) is later publication, not the original information date.

**Trigger/action:** easing systemic collapse risk permits buying credit and increasing exposure. Net moves from -3.4% on April 1 to 37% at June 30. Credit exceeds 40% and merger arbitrage is approximately 20%. These are historical allocations, not standing limits.

**Evidence:** auto holdings include Ford/Ford Credit, Dana, Delphi, Lear and Chrysler Financial claims. The industry accounts for approximately 12% of NAV and 25% of credit, making instrument selection and aggregate industry risk material. Claims and survivors differ; a rescued sector does not rescue every security.

**Value/catalyst:** recoveries, viable reorganized operations, financing normalization and transaction closings. Security-by-security recovery models and entry prices are not supplied in this note. Delphi's later Q2 2012 account dates its DIP entry to June 2009.

**Quote:** “Investing in this industry is not for the faint-of-heart, and one must be selective.”

**Risk/exit:** political intervention, failed restructuring and weak collateral remain material. Offshore reports +10% for the quarter and +7.1% year to date; that outcome does not establish identical returns for each auto claim. The next note records recycling as arbitrage catalysts complete.
