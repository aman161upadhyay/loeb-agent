# L05 — Q4 2012: Greece and the need for a variant view

**Information date:** reproduction 2013-01-09; original signature date not asserted. Era B. [Letter reproduction](https://www.distressed-debt-investing.com/2013/01/third-point-4q-2012-letter.html). [Reuters reporting, 2012-11-28](https://www.cnbc.com/2012/11/28/greek-debt-deal-turns-hedge-fund-trade-to-gold.html).

**Greek trigger/evidence:** political and economic research supports a variant view despite frightening sovereign headlines. Reuters attributes the 17-cent purchase reference to the October 3 Q3 letter; the later buyback discussion occurs at much higher prices. Other funds' cost bases must not be assigned to Third Point.

**Value/catalyst:** legal/political survival, European support and debt repurchase make expected recovery differ from panic market price. An asserted sovereign recovery is not equivalent to an enforceable corporate collateral floor. Exact account-level sizing and all transaction terms are unknown here.

**Quote:** “We dug deep beyond the frightening headlines and conducted in-depth research.”

**Herbalife:** the letter offers a long valuation around 10–12x EPS and a $55–68 target range, with roughly 40–70% potential upside and an analyst-day/short-interest catalyst. These are contemporary projections, not achieved returns or proof against regulatory allegations.

**Risk/exit:** failed political support or weaker recoveries would invalidate Greece; business/regulatory evidence and squeeze dynamics can change Herbalife's long thesis. Do not infer an actual exit date or P&L from the target. The contrast shows both sovereign-distress and equity-controversy applications, not a transferable “buy controversial assets” rule.
