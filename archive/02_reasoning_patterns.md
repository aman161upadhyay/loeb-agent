# Reusable reasoning patterns, with dated counterevidence

Cutoff **2026-10-25**. These are archive-derived templates, **not verbatim Loeb rules**. L/P/D IDs resolve to dated URLs in [letters/README.md](letters/README.md). “Exit test” is an analyst inference unless explicitly described as an actual sale. Unknown current sizing constraints are not filled with historical ranges.

## 01 — Hidden asset plus broken governance

- **Trigger/evidence:** quoted parent value fails to reflect a valuable subsidiary/asset; management incentives prevent realization. Separate asset valuation from holding-company leakage.
- **Valuation:** sum of parts less taxes, debt, costs and realistic discount; probability-weight realization.
- **Sizing:** disclosed issuer stake is not NAV. Economic exposure can include swaps.
- **Catalyst:** board change, asset monetization, allocation committee.
- **Exit test:** realized asset value or evidence that allocation/governance cannot be repaired.
- **Dated examples:** Yahoo (P01, 2011-09-08); Nestlé/L'Oréal (L09, 2018-01-22); SK Square/Hynix (L23, 2025-10-31). **Failure:** CoStar's valuable core did not prevent exit after inadequate governance response (L25, 2026-04-10).

## 02 — Post-reorganization business is not the old security

- **Trigger/evidence:** bankruptcy removes legacy liabilities/costs; market retains old-company stigma. Analyze claim priority and new ownership.
- **Valuation:** normalized cash flow with post-reorganization debt, pension and dilution.
- **Sizing:** aggregate industry/claim exposure; Delphi core 2–5% in 2012 is dated.
- **Catalyst:** financing, emergence, listing, capital returns.
- **Exit test:** realized recovery, lower prospective spread, or operating relapse.
- **Dated examples:** auto credit deployment (L02, 2009-08-04 reproduction); Delphi/Dana gains (L03, 2009-12-01); Delphi's cleaner earnings and buybacks (L04, 2012-07-31). **Failure risk:** the 2009 letter explicitly warns that there will be winners and losers; no invented failed claim attribution.

## 03 — Forced seller creates a security-specific dislocation

- **Trigger/evidence:** forced liquidation or mandate constraints rather than deteriorating ultimate cash flow.
- **Valuation:** compare stressed collateral/recovery and cash carry with price; do not treat peak price as value.
- **Sizing:** reserve capital and inspect redemption/liquidity mismatch.
- **Catalyst:** forced selling exhausts, refinancing or stabilization.
- **Exit test:** normalization removes excess return, or collateral impairment disproves the premise.
- **Dated examples:** European iTraxx tranche (L04, 2012-07-31, actual normalization exit); pandemic credit rotation (L12, 2020-04-16); CCC/CLO selling opportunities (L26, 2026-07-31). **Negative:** tight broad spreads do not justify indiscriminate purchases in the last case.

## 04 — Political survival is part of recovery value

- **Trigger/evidence:** policy pessimism prices a binary collapse; legal, fiscal and political research suggests a different distribution.
- **Valuation:** probability-weight recoveries, dilution and intervention; no corporate-style collateral floor for sovereigns.
- **Sizing:** policy-correlated risks must be aggregated; exact Greek NAV allocation unknown here.
- **Catalyst:** support program, buyback, regulatory/capital restructuring.
- **Exit test:** resolution or changed political feasibility.
- **Dated examples:** Greece thesis quoted from 2012-10-03 letter and reviewed 2013-01-09 (L05); Japan governance engagement discussed 2016-09-21 (I05); GSE preferreds/housing affordability (L25, 2026-04-10). **Failure boundary:** election optimism before tariffs must not be retroactively validated (L21).

## 05 — Under-earning is different from secular decline

- **Trigger/evidence:** operating margins/returns lag achievable economics; identify a credible restoration bridge.
- **Valuation:** normalized earnings rather than one depressed quarter; sensitivity to cycle and costs.
- **Sizing:** conviction depends on recoverable earnings, not distance below old price.
- **Catalyst:** operations, leadership, mix and capacity discipline.
- **Exit test:** normalized value realized or structural deterioration replaces cyclical explanation.
- **Dated examples:** Dow (L06, 2014-01-21); Nestlé (L09, 2018-01-22); Siemens Energy (L20, 2025-02-04). **Negative:** missing Lumen's rerating because of secular-decline concern (L28, 2024-10-16) exposes classification risk without proving all decliners attractive.

## 06 — Conglomerate architecture suppresses business-specific value

- **Trigger/evidence:** unlike businesses share a multiple, capital budget or management system.
- **Valuation:** business-specific peer multiples less separation costs and dis-synergies.
- **Sizing:** implementation probability and correlated cycle exposures govern conviction; exact NAV unknown.
- **Catalyst:** spinoff, listing, portfolio simplification.
- **Exit test:** separation completed, valuation closes or separation economics deteriorate.
- **Dated examples:** UTX three-way case (L10, 2018-05-07 reproduction); Dell/VMware and Prudential/Jackson (L27, 2021-10-27); Flex CPI (L26, 2026-07-31). **Failure/countercase:** Sony's 2013 rejection argues retained entertainment integration creates more value (P03, 2013-08-06).

## 07 — Contradictory stakeholders can create an allocation trap

- **Trigger/evidence:** one corporate entity is asked to maximize incompatible financial/transition/strategic objectives.
- **Valuation:** estimate economics under coherent mandates, but include dis-synergies and stakeholder vetoes.
- **Sizing:** a plausible argument is not authority to force implementation.
- **Catalyst:** structural redesign or leadership that clarifies priorities.
- **Exit test:** credible alignment or persistent inability to change.
- **Dated examples:** Shell (L27, 2021-10-27); Sony entertainment focus versus chip separation (P08, 2019-06-13); Nestlé portfolio focus (P09, 2018-07-01). **Negative:** Shell's own 89%-supported strategy and Sony's resistance constrain activist probability.

## 08 — Quality plus an event can be more attractive than either alone

- **Trigger/evidence:** durable franchise at a margin/ROIC/allocation inflection.
- **Valuation:** forward cash flows and reinvestment returns; avoid paying any multiple for quality.
- **Sizing:** duration and multiple-compression risks remain.
- **Catalyst:** capital returns, operating improvement, separation or product monetization.
- **Exit test:** deteriorating moat, allocation or price-implied return.
- **Dated examples:** Constellation/Roper (L08, 2015-08-03); explicit quality framework (L13, 2020-08-06); Corpay/ICE (L19, 2024-08-23). **Negative:** 2023 admission of missing quality-tech leadership (L17) shows knowing the category does not ensure owning the winners.

## 09 — Good company, bad incremental allocation

- **Trigger/evidence:** profitable core funds questionable new expansion, acquisitions or incentives.
- **Valuation:** split core value from new-project burn and required financing; sunk cost is not future value.
- **Sizing:** require incremental capital discipline, not just a brand moat.
- **Catalyst:** compensation, board oversight, spending limits or disposals.
- **Exit test:** actual economic response; exit if governance cannot correct allocation.
- **Dated examples:** Yahoo leadership repair (P01, 2011-09-08); Campbell complexity/M&A critique (P06, 2018-09-07); CoStar engagement (L21, 2025-04-30) then **actual exit** (L25, 2026-04-10). The last pair is an explicit failed-intervention case.

## 10 — Constructive first, escalate when evidence warrants

- **Trigger/evidence:** private engagement produces inadequate speed/scope; assess board responsiveness.
- **Valuation:** activist costs, probability, time and operating erosion reduce theoretical upside.
- **Sizing:** issuer stake and influence do not equal control.
- **Catalyst:** board nominees, proxy contest, public presentation or settlement.
- **Exit test:** implemented improvement, acceptable compromise or exhausted ability to change.
- **Dated examples:** Baxter constructive request (P05, 2015-08-05); Nestlé escalation (P09, 2018-07-01); Campbell full-board nomination (P06, 2018-09-07). **Negative:** CoStar demonstrates that winning a governance mechanism need not win the economics (L25).

## 11 — Management quality is observable allocation and execution

- **Trigger/evidence:** repeated disciplined decisions, returns-focused systems and a credible successor.
- **Valuation:** sustainable reinvestment returns/earnings, not personal charisma.
- **Sizing:** judgment uncertainty and key-person risk require a loss case.
- **Catalyst:** CEO succession, changed incentives or operating system diffusion.
- **Exit test:** repeated deviations from claimed discipline.
- **Dated examples:** Baxter succession (P05, 2015-08-05); DSV systems/integration (L28, 2024-10-16); Casey's difficult-but-disciplined decisions (L22, 2025-07-30). **Counterexample:** Intel engineering decline (P07, 2020-12-29) shows financial optics can conceal organizational damage.

## 12 — Completed catalysts should release capital

- **Trigger/evidence:** expected deal/reorganization closes, spread disappears or target valuation arrives.
- **Valuation:** reassess residual prospective return, not historical profit.
- **Sizing:** trim/redeploy according to the new opportunity set; no mandatory all-or-nothing sale.
- **Catalyst:** closing, tender, spread compression, maturity/cash amortization.
- **Exit test:** actual closure or diminished excess return.
- **Dated examples:** Pfizer/Wyeth and Merck/Schering arbitrage run-off (L03, 2009-12-01); Progress Energy Resources bid monetization (L04, 2012-07-31); mortgage sales inside 5% yield (L25, 2026-04-10). **Failure risk:** premature trading exits are acknowledged separately in L07, 2015-02-09.

## 13 — Capital-structure instrument selection is part of the thesis

- **Trigger/evidence:** preferred/common/debt claims price different recovery, conversion or liquidity assumptions.
- **Valuation:** claim waterfall, conversion ratio, accrued income, dilution and legal rights.
- **Sizing:** common-equivalent exposure and credit risk need separate denominators.
- **Catalyst:** recapitalization, conversion, emergence or policy resolution.
- **Exit test:** convergence or impairment of specific claim rights.
- **Dated examples:** Popular preferred conversion (L03, 2009-12-01); Delphi DIP/reorganized equity (L04, 2012-07-31); GSE preferreds (L25, 2026-04-10). **Negative:** broad enterprise survival does not guarantee every junior instrument survives.

## 14 — Net exposure alone is a poor risk summary

- **Trigger/evidence:** high gross, illiquid/crowded shorts or correlated longs can overwhelm low nominal net.
- **Valuation:** add funding/borrow/squeeze scenarios to investment downside.
- **Sizing:** gross/net, beta, liquidity and stress loss all matter.
- **Catalyst:** regime change or risk-budget pressure, not necessarily business deterioration.
- **Exit test:** reduce intolerable implementation/correlation risk.
- **Dated examples:** pandemic concentration and inadequate protection (L12, 2020-04-16); explicit post-squeeze leverage warning (L14, 2021-02-10); July 2026 drawdown despite Q2 average net near 45% (L26, 2026-07-31). These are failures/cautions, not proofs that a particular hedge would always work.

## 15 — Short thesis and short trade are separate

- **Trigger/evidence:** overvaluation plus leverage, cash-flow deterioration, accounting concerns or destructive allocation.
- **Valuation:** fundamental downside must exceed borrow, squeeze and takeover risks.
- **Sizing:** historical D01 top-short 2–4% is not a present rule; liquidity/crowding can require less.
- **Catalyst:** financing stress, earnings evidence, allocation mistake.
- **Exit test:** quick loss control when asymmetry/implementation becomes intolerable; no verified fixed percentage.
- **Dated examples:** investigative shorts (D01, January 2011); high-short-interest avoidance (L14, 2021-02-10); shift toward baskets/index (L17, 2023-07-31); AI-displaced/misallocating businesses (L25, 2026-04-10). Meme squeeze risk supplies the explicit negative case.

## 16 — Re-enter when price and evidence change

- **Trigger/evidence:** a previous sale does not freeze the company's expected return forever.
- **Valuation:** refresh estimates and current price; do not anchor on former ownership.
- **Sizing:** fund from the current opportunity set, not a desire to recover old gains.
- **Catalyst:** strategic change or repricing of growth/risks.
- **Exit test:** revised thesis fails, or valuation again removes expected return.
- **Dated examples:** Disney sold late-2021/early-2022 then re-entered (L16, August 2022); Nvidia re-initiation (L22, 2025-07-30); Sony's substantially changed 2019 architecture (P08, 2019-06-13). **Negative:** changed Sony prescriptions invite credibility/dis-synergy critique, not automatic praise for flexibility.

## 17 — Revision must preserve the original claim

- **Trigger/evidence:** new evidence contradicts an earlier specific recommendation.
- **Valuation:** compare economics of the revised structure, not just tone.
- **Sizing:** disclosed opinion is not proof of a simultaneous fund trade.
- **Catalyst:** direct engagement or operational information changes expected synergies.
- **Exit test:** publicly update the thesis; separately record actual sales if known.
- **Dated examples:** Sony minority entertainment offering (P02, 2013-05-14) versus entertainment-focused semiconductor separation (P08, 2019-06-13); ESPN standalone proposal revised toward retained synergies (X, 2022-09-11); CoStar engagement-to-exit (L21/L25, 2025-04-30/2026-04-10). Failures remain visible rather than overwritten.

## 18 — AI adoption and economic capture are different questions

- **Trigger/evidence:** new usage creates demand, but identify who has distribution, manufacturing, data or pricing power.
- **Valuation:** revenue/profit bridge and capital needs; not an “AI” multiple alone.
- **Sizing:** shared capex/customer/factor exposures can correlate apparently diverse holdings.
- **Catalyst:** monetization, upgrades, capacity utilization or workflow adoption.
- **Exit test:** weaker unit economics, substitution or unsustainable capex.
- **Dated examples:** hyperscaler/semiconductor thesis (L17, 2023-07-31); Alphabet/TSMC/Vistra (L18, 2024-04-30); Apple distribution (L19, 2024-08-23); HBM/packaging (L23, 2025-10-31). **Negative:** July 2026 selloff tests the correlated trade, not necessarily all operating forecasts.

## 19 — New technology can destroy yesterday's quality

- **Trigger/evidence:** historical high margins/capital-light economics become contestable.
- **Valuation:** revise terminal margins, reinvestment requirements and customer retention.
- **Sizing:** hedge/short selection must be security-specific.
- **Catalyst:** product substitution, pricing pressure, AI adoption.
- **Exit test:** erosion becomes structural, or market overprices the threat.
- **Dated examples:** quality framework (L13, 2020-08-06) provides the baseline; tech credit underweight for AI transition (L22, 2025-07-30); software/information-service risk (L24, 2026-02-06); AI-loser shorts (L25, 2026-04-10). **Countercase:** L19's ICE/Corpay thesis argues incumbents may benefit rather than disappear.

## 20 — Nested discount needs a governance bridge

- **Trigger/evidence:** attractive underlying asset plus parent discount; check ownership and monetization rights.
- **Valuation:** mark assets, deduct liabilities/taxes, distinguish current discount dates from forward earnings.
- **Sizing:** indirect exposure can add liquidity, control and jurisdiction risks.
- **Catalyst:** buyback/cancellation, disposals, transparency or capital allocation.
- **Exit test:** discount narrows, underlying becomes expensive or governance never improves.
- **Dated examples:** Yahoo/Alibaba (P01, 2011-09-08); Nestlé/L'Oréal monetization argument (L09, 2018-01-22); SK Square (L23, 2025-10-31 and L24, 2026-02-06). **Negative:** discounts can persist; January 2026's ~47% observation is not a quarter-end realized exit.

## 21 — Diagnose the thesis-to-portfolio translation error

- **Trigger/evidence:** broad economic forecast is right but actual holdings lose or miss leadership.
- **Valuation:** evaluate selected securities' sensitivity, duration and catalysts rather than claiming forecast credit.
- **Sizing:** reallocate only after separating factor, research and trading mistakes.
- **Catalyst:** new evidence and a revised portfolio expression.
- **Exit test:** remove positions that no longer represent the intended risk/reward.
- **Dated examples:** trading/premature exits (L07, 2015-02-09); 2018 process critique (L11, 2018-11-09); value instead of quality-tech (L17, 2023-07-31). These are admitted errors, not hypothetical counterexamples.

## 22 — Credit carry and duration are distinct from collateral safety

- **Trigger/evidence:** high yield may reflect rates, defaults, illiquidity or structure; separate each.
- **Valuation:** collateral cash flows, loss severity, amortization and spread/rate sensitivities.
- **Sizing:** fund liquidity and rate hedges independently of loss reserves.
- **Catalyst:** cash paydown, refinancing or spread normalization.
- **Exit test:** realized yield compresses or collateral degrades.
- **Dated examples:** pandemic structured-credit allocation (L12, 2020-04-16); rate-hedged mortgage exposure (L16, August 2022); structured swaps and selective risk (L24, 2026-02-06); mortgages sold inside 5% yield (L25, 2026-04-10). **Negative:** a hedge against rates is not protection against borrower default.

## 23 — Governance/credit documents matter when sponsors are adversarial

- **Trigger/evidence:** liability-management maneuvers transfer collateral or alter lender priority.
- **Valuation:** contractual rights, cooperation thresholds and recovery waterfall, not just reported EBITDA.
- **Sizing:** legal uncertainty and concentrated creditors can dominate nominal spread.
- **Catalyst:** creditor cooperation, refinancing, settlement or restructuring.
- **Exit test:** rights protected/value realized, or document weakness impairs recoveries.
- **Dated examples:** CLO/cooperation discussion (L28, 2024-10-16); >$700m current/anticipated LME situations (L20, 2025-02-04); selective distress after credit disappointments (L22, 2025-07-30). **Negative:** 2025 corporate-credit disappointment (L24) rejects a claim of universal structural edge.

## 24 — Observe information dates, especially subsequent events

- **Trigger/evidence:** a quarterly letter is written weeks later and includes newer trades/events.
- **Valuation:** forward estimates and as-of marks must carry dates.
- **Sizing:** never assign a later exposure to quarter-end automatically.
- **Catalyst:** separate expected from announced/completed events.
- **Exit test:** preserve a new dated state rather than editing history.
- **Dated examples:** October 2018 rout in November Q3 letter (L11); January 2021 squeeze/Gelsinger in Q4 letter (L14); April tariffs in Q1 2025 letter (L21); July 2026 loss in Q2 letter (L26). **Failure prevented:** look-ahead leakage in calibration or backtests.
