# Dated investment principles

As of **2026-10-25**. Source IDs Lxx refer to [the letter register](letters/README.md); Pxx and Ixx are registered there and in [interviews](interviews/README.md). Rules below are empirical interpretations, not instructions attributed verbatim to Loeb. Unknown sizing/exit rules remain unknown.

## 1. Enterprise value must be realizable, not merely arithmetically cheap

**A/B evidence:** The January 2011 institutional diligence report describes value orientation plus an identifiable catalyst and modeling of the whole business. Yahoo's 2011-09-08 letter locates hidden value in assets damaged by governance (P01). Q4 2013 focuses on businesses “under-earning relative to normalized earnings power” (L06, 2014-01-21).

**C evolution:** Nestlé's brands are not sufficient without margin, portfolio and allocation changes (L09, 2018-01-22). A compounder still needs an attractive entry price (L13, 2020-08-06).

**E test:** CoStar's attractive underlying franchise did not justify indefinitely funding an unreformed allocation policy; L25 (2026-04-10) reports the exit. Avoid turning a sum-of-parts spreadsheet into an unconditional hold.

## 2. A catalyst can be embedded, negotiated or contested

The 2009 credit book relies on bankruptcy and financing normalization (L02–03); Yahoo needs governance intervention (P01); Progress Energy Resources monetizes through an external bid (L04). UTX separation (L10) and Intel leadership repair (P07/L14) require different implementation paths.

Campaign escalation is contingent, not the default. Baxter 2015 seeks representation constructively; Campbell 2018 nominates an entire board (P06). Nestlé 2018 escalates after dissatisfaction with incremental progress (I03). Sony management rejects the 2013 IPO proposal (P03). Catalyst probability, management authority and stakeholder resistance belong in valuation.

No evidence supports treating every Third Point equity holding as an activist campaign.

## 3. Normalize earnings and cash flow before applying a multiple

**B:** Delphi's cleaner post-bankruptcy cost base, geography, balance sheet and buybacks matter more than its association with the old US auto industry (L04, 2012-07-31). Dow's hidden earnings power depends on petrochemical strategy and operating improvement (L06).

**C:** UTX uses separate peer multiples for Otis, climate and aerospace rather than one conglomerate multiple (L10, 2018-05-07 reproduction). Roper/Constellation in 2015 already combine operating progress with capital allocation (L08).

**E:** SK Hynix's HBM profit mix is offered as evidence against a commodity-memory multiple (L23, 2025-10-31). This is a thesis about future earnings durability, not independently proven immunity to cycles.

Valuation output should state earnings year, bridge to that earnings level, peer selection, net debt, holding-company leakage and implementation costs. Do not compare a forward P/E to an unrelated historical EV/EBITDA.

## 4. Quality is operational and financial, not a label

L13 (2020-08-06) says “today, quality is also an essential screen” and identifies leadership, unique products, growing end-markets, share gains, earnings/free-cash-flow growth and high returns on invested capital. It immediately warns that most such firms trade at high multiples and entry price matters.

L08 demonstrates the theme by 2015. L09's specialist-team discussion records an organizational shift dating to 2009. Therefore neither 2016 nor 2020 is a clean birth date for quality investing; 2020 is an unusually explicit articulation.

**Negative evidence:** L17 (2023-07-31) admits that a constructive economic view was expressed through value situations instead of the quality-tech winners. L24 (2026-02-06) questions how durable previously prized capital-light software economics remain under AI. A moat must be re-underwritten when technology changes.

## 5. Capital allocation can destroy a good business or unlock a mediocre valuation

Yahoo's board, Sony's portfolio, Nestlé's L'Oréal stake, Baxter's CEO succession and CoStar's Homes.com investment all express this distinction. The mechanism must be specific: stop negative-return spend, return genuinely excess capital, separate conflicting businesses, acquire sensibly, or improve incentives.

**C/E contrast:** CoStar's Q1 2025 engagement seeks a Capital Allocation Committee and compensation review (L21). Q1 2026 exits after judging the board response inadequate (L25). Board access is not equivalent to successful economic change.

**Countercase:** Sony's 2013 company response argues that full entertainment ownership creates strategic benefits (P03). An activist's transaction multiple must be weighed against actual integration benefits, taxes and lost optionality.

## 6. Distress is a cash-flow, legal and ownership problem

Auto credits in Q2 2009 (L02) require selecting survivors, claims and recoveries, not buying every cyclical equity. Delphi's DIP entry was **June 2009**, as recounted in L04. Greece's thesis is developed in Q3 2012 and revisited in L05; it is not in the Q2 2012 letter.

L12 (2020-04-16) shifts toward credit during the pandemic, with a stated general template of spread normalization over 12–18 months. L16 (2022-08 reproduction) warns credit cycles move slowly, then suddenly.

E-era credit analysis includes CLO eligibility, forced selling, liability-management documents, collateral and maturity walls. L20 (2025-02-04) discusses creditor cooperation against sponsor maneuvers; L26 (2026-07-31) prefers selective opportunities despite tight broad spreads.

Do not equate a low bond price with a high expected recovery. Analyze lien priority, liquidity runway, sponsor incentives, documentation, dilution and actual collateral.

## 7. The opportunity set determines asset allocation

Equities, corporate/sovereign credit, structured products, private investments and merger arbitrage compete for capital. L03 (2009-12-01 reporting) records risk arbitrage shrinking after major deals close. L12 increases credit as pandemic liquidation changes relative returns. L16 reduces gross/net and later covers shorts as markets change. L22 documents merger arbitrage plus Nvidia re-entry.

An open mind is not an exemption from a decision record. Store the instrument, price/valuation reference, event horizon, liquidity, loss case and funding source. A reduction in common stock does not prove a bearish enterprise thesis if credit or derivatives replace it.

## 8. Downside and liquidity govern sizing; historical numbers are not permanent rules

The [January 2011 Cliffwater report](https://data.treasury.ri.gov/dataset/96dcb86f-e97e-4b05-8ce2-a40289e477a6/resource/af87093d-d2a1-4d7d-a1e5-57294dc43a28/download/third-point-cliffwater-investment-due-diligence-reportredacted.pdf) says positions use probability-weighted upside/downside estimates. It reports top longs generally 5–7%, top shorts 2–4%, and approximately 85% liquidatable within ten days at that time. Its position-downside numerical guideline is **redacted**; do not fill that document's omission by inference.

A separately recovered [New Jersey memorandum, 2011-03-18](https://nj.gov/treasury/doinvest/pdf/AlternativeInvestments/HedgeFund/ThirdPoint-PershingSquare.pdf) (D02) does disclose a **general guideline limiting probability-weighted position downside to 2%**. This is an authentic, separately attributed historical observation—not a guaranteed stop-loss, a 2% position cap, or proof of a present-day rule. The memorandum also reports Partners' 2008 annual loss of 32.84%; do not confuse this vehicle with Offshore or Ultra.

L12 describes roughly 85% of NAV in the top twenty positions entering the pandemic and acknowledges inadequate aerospace protection. It reports net equity reduced from 70% to 58%, with beta-adjusted exposure from 65% to 48%. These are dated portfolio states, not recommended limits.

L14 (2021-02-10) explicitly distinguishes leverage from net exposure: low net can hide dangerous gross and short squeeze exposure. L25 cuts gross/net when target prices and factor rotations reduce reward-to-risk. L26's June-quarter average net near 45% does not describe July's realized risk perfectly.

## 9. Shorts require a separate asymmetry and implementation discipline

The 2011 report emphasizes leverage, potential liquidity failure and investigative accounting/fraud work. L09 explains single-name shorts replacing index hedges; L10 adds fundamental and quantitative baskets. L14 warns against illiquid, crowded high-short-interest positions after early-2021 squeezes and advocates cutting losses promptly. L17 reduces single-name shorts and uses baskets/index hedges after repeated squeeze risk.

L25 (2026-04-10) identifies shorts where deteriorating businesses compound their problem through poor capital allocation and AI-displaced business models. The statement “Handicapping the AI losers appears easier than picking the winners” is contextual, not a license to short every software company.

There is no verified permanent stop-loss percentage. Borrow availability, recall, squeeze exposure, crowding, liquidity and takeover risk must be considered independently of fundamental value.

## 10. Separate technological adoption from who captures the economics

Disney 2020 focuses on content designed for DTC rather than merely repurposed releases; the 2022 ESPN spin argument is publicly revised in September (X archive). Shell 2021 asks whether conflicting legacy and transition mandates belong in one corporate structure.

L17's AI analysis starts with hyperscalers and semiconductor infrastructure. L18 (2024-04-30) argues incumbents have financial/intellectual advantages; TSMC's discount and Alphabet's Gemini-related sentiment provide entry valuation. L19 adds Apple's distribution position, not just an AI buzzword.

L23 argues reasoning/post-training demand offsets efficiency-led compute fears; L24/L25 simultaneously identify possible software losers. L26 adds power/cooling integration through Flex and existing-distribution monetization through Block. Each layer has distinct capital intensity, replacement cycles, pricing power and risk.

## 11. Political analysis must be dispassionate and second-order

Japan's governance changes support Fanuc/Sony/Seven & i engagement; Greece requires political survival analysis. L20 (2025-02-04) calls for “second-order thinking” and an “unemotional response, independent of one’s political views.”

This principle is tested, not validated, by his public political opinions. L21 cuts exposure after tariff shocks, notwithstanding earlier optimism about deregulation and corporate activity (L20/L19–20). L25's GSE preferred thesis depends partly on housing affordability policy. Do not transform social-media ideology into a forecast.

## 12. Error correction is a process input, not retrospective victory narration

L07 (2015-02-09) discusses poor trading/premature exits. L11 (2018-11-09) says underperformance demands examining process and warns of hindsight's “perfect storm.” L12 admits pandemic underprotection; L15 records 2022's difficult rate/crypto environment; L17 explicitly admits missing the quality-tech expression of an otherwise constructive economic view.

The correct archive stores both original thesis and later revision. Sony 2013 and 2019 prescribe different portfolio architectures. ESPN September 2022 reverses the spin-off preference. CoStar 2026 exits after 2025 optimism. July 2026's drawdown follows—not precedes—Q2's gains.

**What not to infer:** that every loser was a research failure; that every winner validates the original causal thesis; that a silent 13F omission proves a fundamental exit reason; or that a manager's later lesson was known at initial purchase.

## 13. Economic cash flow must charge for employee equity

On [2022-10-25](https://x.com/DanielSLoeb1/status/1584776507146514432), [2022-11-05](https://x.com/DanielSLoeb1/status/1589015730380427264) and [2022-12-10](https://x.com/DanielSLoeb1/status/1601675220666179585), Loeb distinguishes economic cash generation from stock issuance in lieu of wages. This is an accounting/valuation criticism, not a moral objection to employee compensation. A software FCF bridge should reconcile SBC, dilution and repurchases; favorable reported FCF is not enough if maintaining ownership requires offsetting share issuance.

This principle is compatible with buying technology businesses: the [2022-06-10 post](https://x.com/DanielSLoeb1/status/1535197242038050816) applies the same cash-flow discipline across sectors. No universal SBC threshold or valuation haircut was disclosed.

## 14. Defend duration without defending a broken public commitment

The [2022-09-03 post](https://x.com/DanielSLoeb1/status/1566073765842374662) identifies commitment bias as activism's greatest risk; September 11's ESPN reversal is observable contrary evidence to rigid public-position defense. On [2024-10-16](https://x.com/DanielSLoeb1/status/1846373903779713191), Loeb explicitly differentiates single-manager advantages from platforms: longer investment duration, concentration of best ideas, greater drawdown tolerance, higher net and lower gross.

These two observations must be used together. A price decline or crowded-factor selloff need not falsify a durable thesis, but “we can tolerate drawdowns” cannot excuse deteriorating fundamental evidence. CoStar's Q1 2026 complete exit is an explicit thesis break, not a mere mark-to-market fluctuation. The [2026-03-16 post](https://x.com/DanielSLoeb1/status/2033513631044821374) also questions how technological change affects valuation three to five years out, even with current cash flow and an apparent moat. Duration is an advantage only when durability is underwritten.

## Operational evidence checklist

For a new case record: information date; era; security/instrument; source rank; trigger; enterprise and claim value; operating evidence; expected catalyst/control; valuation bridge and probability cases; loss and liquidity cases; disclosed sizing versus unknown; hedge/correlation; decision horizon; observable disconfirmation; actual dated changes. Keep action and interpretation separate.
