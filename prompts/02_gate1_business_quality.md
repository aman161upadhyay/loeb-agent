Situation types:
E1 activist_created_event — good assets whose value is obscured by a fixable problem Third Point can name and press: a margin
   gap vs peers, a conglomerate/sum-of-parts discount with separable units, capital allocation (buybacks, non-core stakes), or a
   management/board problem; needs a governance route (nomination window, settlement precedent, receptive holders) and a
   stake size that gets a hearing
E2 announced_or_structural_event — spin-off, post-reorg listing, merger arbitrage, index/listing change, restructuring, forced
   selling, or a crowded and publicised short position that creates a squeeze path for a long in a cash-generative business
   (a technical event: the exit rule is to sell into strength, not to win the argument); needs the document or data that
   creates the event and the mispricing it causes
E3 credit_distressed_sovereign — DIP/post-reorg equity, fulcrum securities, secured loans, structured credit, sovereign bonds
   with an official-sector path; needs a waterfall/recovery analysis and a process timetable
E4 quality_earnings_inflection (AS_OF_DATE >= 2016) — durable business entering accelerating earnings or cash return the
   market has not priced; needs the inflection mechanism in numbers; no activism required
E5 macro_thematic — a regime view expressed through securities with asymmetric payoff; needs the policy/supply-demand argument
E6 private_venture_spac (AS_OF_DATE >= 2016) — pre-IPO/SPAC; small; cut when valuation discipline returns
E7 short — accounting, balance-sheet or business-model problem with a catalyst, or a factor hedge for the long book
NONE — no event, no inflection, no plan

Score each with one sentence of evidence [Dnn]:
1 event_identified — PASS if a type E1-E7 fits with evidence; FAIL if only "cheap"
2 edge — PASS if Third Point brings something (plan, process, capital-structure expertise, size, timing, contrarian position
  vs a crowded trade); FAIL if a crowded consensus trade with no differentiated view
3 governance_route_or_process — PASS if the event can be forced or is already in motion (nomination window, settlement precedent,
  court/official process, announced deal); FAIL if a controller/government blocks it and the mispricing is not extreme
  (Japan after 2013 = PASS if the plan is polite and specific)
4 downside_floor — PASS if there is an asset, cash, recurring-FCF or seniority floor under the bear case; FAIL if structurally
  declining with leverage and no floor
5 size_and_liquidity — PASS if Third Point can build a position that matters (Era A: 5-10% of a small cap; Era B/C: $300M-$3.5B)
  and exit within a reasonable time
6 management_credibility — PASS if management/board can execute or can be replaced; FAIL if the thesis depends on an unproven
  management executing a long roadmap (post-2022 Intel rule) unless the position is sized as a trade

Scoring rules by situation type (apply before the hard-fail test):
- E1 in Era A/B (AS_OF_DATE < 2012): the plan is Third Point's to write, not the pack's to supply. Score "edge" PASS when the
  pack shows a value gap with a nameable, fixable cause — a management discount after repeated operating disappointments
  (Massey 2005), entrenched or self-dealing insiders, idle cash or NOLs, a conglomerate with separable units, an accounting
  delinquency over real assets (Ligand 2005) — because Third Point's edge in that era was the willingness to say it publicly,
  file the 13D letter and run or threaten a proxy contest that other holders would not. Score "governance_route" PASS when
  directors stand for election, a 5-10% stake is buildable and the base is restless (institutional holders, prior shareholder
  criticism); a settlement precedent or an open nomination window is not required. Score "downside_floor" on asset value at the
  management discount (reserves, IP and royalties, real estate, NOLs, net cash), not on current FCF; a loss-making year does not
  remove an asset floor. "management_credibility" asks whether management can be REPLACED or pressured, not whether it is good —
  a bad, entrenched management is the opportunity in E1, so score PASS when the board is electable and FAIL only for a
  controller who cannot be moved.
- E3 credit/distressed: score the CLAIM. "downside_floor" = collateral or seniority coverage of the price paid (first liens in
  the 50s covered by a ring-fenced finance company's receivables: Chrysler Financial 2009; Delphi DIP and post-reorg 2009);
  "size_and_liquidity" = whether a $100M-$1B claim can be bought from forced sellers (there is no equity denominator and no
  daily volume — that is normal, not a FAIL); "management_credibility" = the process (court, government deadline, agent bank),
  not the management; "edge" = the recovery analysis itself when claims trade below coverage and the holders are forced
  sellers. Missing delinquency detail lowers conviction and size, it does not fail the gate.
- E2 crowded-short squeeze long (a cash-generative business under a publicised short campaign): the type is E2, not E7 —
  Third Point is on the LONG side of someone else's short. "governance_route_or_process" is the technical path (short interest
  as % of float, days to cover, a scheduled rebuttal or analyst day, buyback capacity); "downside_floor" is the business itself
  at a single-digit multiple with free cash flow and a buyback (the regulatory or legal tail is sized through a smaller
  position and a defined exit, it is not gated); "edge" is the contrarian position plus independent diligence on the
  short-seller's claim. The exit is written at entry: sell into the repricing (Herbalife 2013).
- E4 quality/earnings inflection (2016+) and E5 thematic: no governance route is needed — score "governance_route_or_process"
  PASS when the inflection or theme is already in motion in the numbers (segment margins turning, capital return announced,
  demand step-change visible in orders and capex) and "edge" on being early and specific about the mechanism, not on
  non-public insight: Third Point's 2023 pivot to the AI beneficiaries (hyperscalers, foundry and memory, software ARPU) was a
  consensus-to-be trade entered before the consensus, sized as a basket and cut when the valuation discipline said so.
  "Crowded" is a FAIL only when the pack shows positioning already extreme (sentiment surveys, flows, multiples at the top of
  their own history) at AS_OF_DATE.
- E1 in Era C/E large caps (2012+; Nestlé 2017, Campbell 2018, Colgate 2022, Disney 2022): the route is a private letter and,
  if ignored, a public deck; the register is index-heavy and receptive by default, so "governance_route" is PASS unless a
  controller or a dual-class structure blocks it; "edge" is the under-earning or hidden-asset arithmetic against named peers
  (the pack's segment data or peer margins), not a nomination window.
- E2/E3 post-reorganisation or overhang situations (PG&E 2020, Delphi 2009, Chrysler Financial 2009): the forced seller or the
  trust/creditor overhang IS the reason the price is available; with a regulated or asset-backed floor the edge is the
  willingness to hold through the distribution, so score "edge" PASS and leave the overhang to sizing and timetable.
- Missing data is UNSURE, not FAIL: a criterion may FAIL only on evidence in the pack that the thing is absent or adverse.

Discipline tests (apply to every E1/E4 long before the hard-fail test; these are why Third Point's 13Fs do not contain most
"cheap and under-earning" names):
- Melting ice cube: if the core business is in secular decline — share lost to a structural competitor, negative organic growth
  for three or more years, margins compressing without a cyclical cause — then "downside_floor" = FAIL: a low multiple on
  falling earnings is not a floor (big-box retail under Amazon 2012; daily-deals and coupon models; leveraged consumer-brand
  roll-ups). Exception only when the thesis is a whole-company sale or liquidation at asset value with credible, named
  buyers, or a separable asset whose value does not depend on the declining unit (Sony's entertainment and insurance 2013;
  Yahoo's Alibaba stake 2011; Campbell's snacks and a named strategic buyer 2018).
- Someone else's fight: if another activist already holds board seats or has published the same asks (a Trian director on the
  board; Starboard or Icahn in a proxy fight), "edge" = FAIL — Third Point leads or stays out; the easy asks are already in
  the price.
- Unknowable liabilities: legacy liabilities that cannot be bounded from the pack — long-term-care or run-off insurance
  reserves, product-safety or criminal process under regulatory review, a deposit run — make "downside_floor" FAIL for the
  equity; the right instrument, if any, is the credit (E3).
- Leverage on a shrinking base: net debt above ~4x EBITDA in a business with falling organic sales → "downside_floor" FAIL
  unless the thesis is the credit or an announced deleveraging sale with proceeds in hand.
- Adjusted-earnings roll-up: a serial acquirer whose "adjusted" EPS is positive while GAAP EPS is deeply negative, with
  negative tangible equity, acquisition debt and litigation or settlement reserves (specialty-pharma roll-ups 2014-16),
  belongs to the E7 short template, not to E1; "downside_floor" = FAIL for the equity — divestiture cash owed to creditors is
  not a floor for shareholders.
- Multi-year operational turnaround as the only lever: when there is no separable asset, no capital-return capacity and no
  sale route — only "new management executes" over several years under regulatory or safety oversight — the position is a
  trade (≤ 3% NAV) or a PASS, in every era (the 2022 Intel rule stated generally).

Hard fail rule: FAIL on 1 or 4, or FAIL on both 2 and 3, or FAIL on three or more criteria → hard_fail = true. UNSURE never
counts toward a hard fail.
