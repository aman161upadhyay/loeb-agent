## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2006 relationship / 2007 public campaign.** The April 11, 2007 letter says discussions had lasted more than a year; exact first acquisition is unknown, so the 2006 filename denotes the earlier relationship rather than a verified first-fill date. PDL combined valuable antibody-humanization royalties, commercial operations and development spending. Loeb argued the royalty stream’s value was obscured by a costly operating strategy and management resistance. [1]

A proper model must separate finite-life patent royalties, commercial assets and uncertain R&D rather than mechanically apply a perpetual earnings multiple. July allegations that $32–34 buyer discussions had been withheld were Third Point’s allegations, not a signed offer or established legal finding. [2]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“the Company should examine splitting into two publicly traded entities” — April 11, 2007 letter. [1]

The same letter demanded CEO change and board additions. Those demands belong with the royalty/capital-allocation argument, not a claim that all research spending lacks value. [1]

## What Third Point did
March 5 public letter; April 11 increased stake to 9.7% and rejected management’s process. May letters proposed independent director Jean-Jacques Bienaimé; July escalated criticism of Mark McDade, headquarters costs and the reported acquisition interest. Director nominations and sale proposals were tools to release royalty value; not evidence of a Third Point takeover. [1,2]

## What happened after
PDL’s 2008 annual report records March commercial/manufacturing sales, a $4.25/share May cash dividend and December separation of R&D into Facet. The 8-K confirms one Facet share per five PDL shares distributed December 18, 2008, with Queen et al. patents and royalty assets retained at PDL. These corporate actions broadly match the separation thesis. Total return requires both securities, cash payments and Third Point’s actual ownership dates; that P&L remains unknown. Outcome win means structural catalyst, not certified fund profit. [4,5]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Royalty cash flows obscured by mixed business:** Separating royalty assets from development spend exposed a different cash-flow profile. [1,4]
- **Reduce spending and return capital:** Cash distributions were a preferred alternative to funding management’s existing plan. [1,5]
- **Leadership and independent board:** CEO change and named nominees were proposed to execute the plan. [1,2]
- **Strategic-buyer optionality:** Reported interest motivated calls for a credible review, not guaranteed sale proceeds. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: an asset-and-incentive campaign whose structural result must not be scored from the unadjusted parent share price after distributions. Its limits include patent duration, clinical asset value and contested leadership criticism. [1–5]

## Public discussion
Lawler’s April 13, 2007 article disclosed a long PDL position and agreed some costs were excessive while arguing Loeb cherry-picked a three-year return window: under McDade’s full tenure he cited 148% versus 46% for Nasdaq biotech. He also defended collaboration/R&D value. This is a useful contemporaneous rebuttal rather than automatic endorsement of the activist. [3]
