## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**April 2003 campaign snapshot; first purchase unknown.** Potlatch was a timber/paper-products company after three losing years; its reported first-quarter loss was $8.9m. Its voting regime gave more votes to shares held at least four years, with an 80% shareholder threshold needed to eliminate the provision. The $21.95 quotation and loss were reported April 17, after the initial letter; do not backdate them to purchase. [1]

Contemporaneous coverage records Loeb questioning investment in a new $66m Las Vegas towel factory while consumer-products pricing suffered oversupply. Management argued industry cycles had been considered and the long-term project remained sound. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“We find it ironic that shareholders must endure four years of your destructive stewardship in order to be entitled to a full vote” — April 2003 letter, quoted by the April 17 Tribune. This is a verified press reproduction, not a recovered full primary exhibit. [1]

The original April 7 letter locator survives, but its PRNewswire page now returns 404. Full asset valuation, pension calculations and exact CEO-removal wording remain primary-source gaps. [2]

## What Third Point did
Loeb publicly challenged the time-phased voting system and management’s capital use in April 2003. The CEO said the board would discuss the voting regime, potentially at the May 12 meeting; any elimination proposal would not reach shareholders before May 2004 under the reported timetable. Do not record a promised discussion as an actual rule change or Third Point board appointment. [1]
