## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**August 2015 disclosure:** Baxter had separated Baxalta; Third Point identified an under-earning, now more focused medical-products company facing CEO succession. The August disclosure was initially about 7%, while the Q3 investor letter subsequently described 9.9%. These are different observation dates, not conflicting measurements. The spin was already a corporate action, not something first invented by Loeb after buying. [1–2]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“break-ups and spinoffs are classic opportunities and Baxter fits this mold” — Q3 2015 letter. It sought to “re-invigorate the more focused, post-spin company and drive it toward industry-leading operational performance.” The letter credits Jose “Joe” Almeida's Covidien operating record. [1]

## What Third Point did
| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| August 5, 2015 | SEC-filed letter disclosed investment and engagement on CEO succession/board representation. [2] |
| Q3 2015 | Stake reached 9.9%; Munib Islam joined board; agreement provided another independent director, subsequently Michael Mahoney. [1] |
| Late 2015 | Supported Almeida as new CEO. [1] |
| Q4 2018 | Reported common shares declined from 36 million to 28 million. [4] |
| 2019 | Additional trims, but still 17.5 million shares at December 31. [4] |
| Q1/Q2/Q3 2020 | Holdings fell to 11.58 million, then 4 million, then zero in compiled 13F history. [4] |

## What happened after
The Q4 2018 update reports 17.4% operating margins and room toward 23%, $4 billion returned to shareholders and $1 billion spent on business development since 2016. Those are an interim reported margin, a future potential and cumulative capital actions—not all original-entry facts. New pumps and injectable drugs were prospective revenue catalysts. [3]

**Exit correction:** the investment did not end with 2018's trim, or in 2019. The recovered common-stock history supports a Q3 2020 exit window; exact execution day and derivative exposure are unknown. No aggregate realized profit is asserted. [4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Focused under-earner:** post-spin margins lagged peers. [1]
- **Operator:** Almeida's prior record gave the turnaround a specific executor. [1]
- **Governance:** board access supported implementation. [1–2]
- **Capital allocation:** buybacks, disciplined business development and margins mattered together. [1,3]
- **Growth:** later product launches were an incremental thesis update, not the entire original case. [3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: textbook large-cap constructivism around a spin-off, transition in leadership and measurable earnings normalization. Keep Baxalta's subsequent corporate history separate from Third Point's Baxter ownership. [1–4]

## Public discussion
The August SEC letter and joint board arrangements are more reliable than headlines conflating Baxalta and Baxter. Filing-compilation portfolio weights refer to disclosed US holdings, not total fund NAV. Authentic contemporary forum/X discussion remains missing. [2,4]
