## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**September 2005 information set.** Massey’s Appalachian coal assets were attractive to Third Point while the shares traded at what Loeb called a management/“Blankenship discount.” He cited operating disappointments and an analyst’s approximately $50m cash-flow impact from diesel hedging failures. Those are campaign claims and estimates, not court findings. [1]

Loeb’s prospective 2006 EBITDA range of $800–900m exceeded an approximately $750m consensus, versus $300–350m capex. This supported a free-cash-flow and leverage/repurchase argument; a coal-price reversal or continuing operational miss would undermine it. Do not leak the 2010 mine disaster into this 2005 entry information set. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
The exact contemporaneous framing was a “Blankenship discount” — September 19, 2005. [1]

The proposal tied the alleged discount to returning capital and strengthening governance. The numerical earnings estimates belong to Loeb’s forecast, not realized 2006 performance. [1]

## What Third Point did
September 2005: disclosed 5.9% and proposed leveraged repurchases, including a Dutch tender, with stronger oversight. March 2006: escalated into a proxy contest. The July 21 executed settlement records that Third Point nominated Loeb and Swanson at the May 16 meeting, a subsequent dispute over Swanson’s election, and resolution of the litigation. It lists both men as directors and provides $750,000 reimbursement of Third Point’s legal expenses. [1–3]

## What happened after
Board representation is a verified governance result. The settlement did not remove Blankenship: he remains on its director list. No full transaction-level return, exit date, actual forecast reconciliation or causal attribution of later corporate events was recovered. Outcome win is specifically a board-access result, not a claim of realized investment profit. [3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Management discount to valuable coal assets:** The investment separated attractive assets from distrust of stewardship. [1]
- **Fix operating execution:** Missed operating performance and diesel costs were specific critiques. [1]
- **Return excess prospective cash flow:** Forecast EBITDA/capex economics supported leverage and a tender. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: asset underwriting plus capital-allocation pressure, escalating to actual board participation. A governance win can coexist with an unresolved economic result and continuing incumbent leadership. [1–3]
