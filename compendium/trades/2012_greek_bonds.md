## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Q3 2012, after the PSI haircut.** New Greek government debt was a strip of 20 bonds, roughly 20 years average maturity. After March’s €175bn official package and private-creditor haircut to around 25%, elections and delayed implementation drove strip prices from about 25 to 14 cents. Third Point believed those prices nearly assumed euro exit. Draghi’s late-July commitment to the euro changed its assessment of redenomination probability. [1]

At roughly 17 cents entry, Loeb expected incremental funding alone to support low-20s pricing; larger upside required fiscal compliance, recovery and debt sustainability. UK-law documentation and pari-passu provisions were his arguments against another private-only haircut, not a guarantee against sovereign or political losses. Entry was Q3; precise first-fill day/month is unknown. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“We initiated a position in the GGB Strip at ~17 cents in the Third Quarter” — October 3, 2012. [1]

“the probability of a Greek default was overstated by the market” — same letter. This is a differentiated probability judgment, not a claim Greece was solvent at face value. [1]

## What Third Point did
Bought the new post-PSI strip during Q3, sent a European credit analyst to Athens and tested officials’ incentives to implement the Troika plan rather than leave the euro. October scenarios priced the strip around 30 at 15% yield or 40 at 12.5% a year later. In December Third Point participated substantially in the sovereign buyback, according to FT/press. [1–3]

## What happened after
December 19 reporting attributes roughly $500m profit to the buyback, while later FT reporting describes the bet and tender. This is secondary-source reported trade profit, not a fund net return. The October low-20s mark and 30/40 yield scenarios were predictions at the time; December monetization came through a specific policy transaction rather than a year of coupon carry. Exact purchased face, average tender price, residual holdings and audited realized P&L remain unresolved. [1–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Redenomination probability overstated:** Prices appeared to treat euro exit as much more likely than Third Point’s policy analysis did. [1]
- **Official funding and policy resolve:** Incremental Troika support/Draghi commitment created a concrete near-term catalyst. [1]
- **Document-level creditor protections:** UK law and ranking clauses mattered to selective-restructuring risk. [1]
- **Recovery-to-yield repricing:** A switch from default assumptions to yield valuation produced asymmetric upside scenarios. [1]
- **Field research on implementation incentives:** Athens meetings tested officials’ willingness and budget progress. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: sovereign investment with macro inputs but security-level legal and cash-price discipline. It is not a generic leveraged euro bet. Preserve the distinction between a reported $500m trade profit and portfolio exposure denominators. [1–3]

## Public discussion
FT/CNN public reporting highlighted a hedge-fund windfall from a crisis buyback, a distributionally controversial outcome. The investment letter supplied a policy/contractual thesis before that payoff. Authentic period Reddit/VIC/COBF discussion was not recovered. [1–3]
