## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2018 campaign:** Campbell faced debate over operating performance, strategy and balance-sheet repair. Third Point's initially confrontational campaign sought major board change and consideration of a sale. The incumbent publicly challenged Third Point's plan and argued its own strategic process was appropriate. Exact initial purchase/disclosure dates and stake cost remain unresolved rather than reverse-engineered from later 13Fs. [1–2]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
**Settlement, November 26:** “Third Point looks forward to working collaboratively with Campbell to improve value for all shareholders at this important time for the Company.” Loeb emphasized finding a “world-class CEO.” [2]

**Q4 2018 update:** “repair the balance sheet, execute an operational turnaround of the business, and explore all options to create long-term value for shareholders.” This describes the post-settlement implementation thesis, not an assertion that the original demand for a sale was met. [5]

## What Third Point did
| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| 2018 | Public proxy contest; campaign ultimately reduced to a settlement. [1–2] |
| November 26, 2018 | Agreed Sarah Hofstetter and Kurt Schmidt would join board after annual meeting, plus consultation over a third director; withdrew slate and litigation, accepted 12-month standstill. [2] |
| Late 2018 | Participated in CEO search; later letter credits recruitment of Mark Clouse. [2,5] |
| 2019 year-end | Reduced below 5% following stock appreciation, according to annual letter. [3] |
| Q1 2020 | Exited disclosed common holding. [4] |

## What happened after
The 2019 letter reports Campbell shares gained 55% for the year and frames the turnaround as a major winner. This is issuer calendar-year performance, not Third Point's full-period return. The settlement gave two immediate board nominees and input on an additional appointment/CEO search; it did **not** hand Third Point unilateral control or complete the sale of the company. [2–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Accountability:** governance changes were a way to change operating decisions. [1–2]
- **Alternatives:** sale was an option, not the only viable terminal outcome. [1,5]
- **Leadership:** recruiting an operator became central to the settlement. [2,5]
- **Balance sheet:** reduce financial strain. [5]
- **Operations:** portfolio focus and execution could create value without selling the whole company. [3,5]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: public confrontation transitioned to negotiated governance and operating collaboration. The result breaks a simplistic label of either “failed sale campaign” or “full proxy takeover.” [2–3]
