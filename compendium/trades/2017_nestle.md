## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**June 25, 2017:** Third Point disclosed roughly 40 million shares and over $3.5 billion invested **including options**, through funds and a dedicated SPV. Nestlé's approximately CHF90 billion 2016 sales and brands in coffee, infant formula, pet food and water offered attractive category/geographic exposure. The letter contrasted this quality with five years of stalled EPS, lower margins and long-term peer underperformance. Outside CEO Mark Schneider's arrival created a credible change window. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“It is rare to find a business of Nestlé's quality with so many avenues for improvement.” The letter proposed an “18-20% by 2020” formal margin range, leverage of at least 2x and monetization of the remaining 23% L'Oréal stake, then valued above $25 billion. These were recommendations, not company commitments. Accent rendering is normalized from the reproduced letter. [1]

**July 1, 2018 escalation:** “This is a call for urgency – rather than incrementalism”. The deck/letter urged Nestlé to become “sharper,” “bolder” and “faster.” [3]

## What Third Point did
| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| June 25, 2017 | Announced position and four-part plan; retained consumer operator Jan Bennink as adviser. [1] |
| Q4 2017 letter | Welcomed company target of 17.5–18.5% margin by 2020, CHF20 billion buyback, portfolio adjustments and new outside directors. **Company target differed from Third Point's 18–20%.** [2] |
| July 1, 2018 | Published 34-page #NestléNOW deck and renewed letter; proposed divesting up to 15% of sales and selling L'Oréal holding. [3] |
| 2019 | Remained a positive contributor and core activist/constructive position. [4] |

## What happened after
The initial plan predicted CHF5–6 EPS by 2020 versus CHF3.40 in 2016; that remains a forecast in this archive, not a verified realized result. The 2018 letter said change had been too slow despite some actions. Later 2019 performance does not establish all proposals were adopted, particularly full L'Oréal monetization. [1,3–4]

**Requested trims:** dated trade-level evidence for Nestlé trims during 2017–2019 was not recovered. Do not infer them from US 13F absence, because Swiss ordinary shares/SPV/options complicate US-only reporting. Exact subsequent reductions, final exit and investment-level P&L remain open gaps. [1,4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Quality/under-earning:** excellent categories plus measurable peer gaps. [1]
- **Margin accountability:** explicit target rather than undefined “continuous improvement.” [1–2]
- **Balance sheet:** low leverage and buybacks could improve per-share economics. [1]
- **Portfolio:** prune assets inconsistent with growth priorities. [1,3]
- **L'Oréal:** non-strategic financial investment distinct from core food operations. [1,3]
- **Executor:** Schneider/Bennink operating experience made self-help more credible. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: the Baxter playbook transferred to consumer staples—quality assets, new leader, margins, capital and portfolio. A dedicated SPV changed sizing, not the principle that operating improvements must justify value. [1]

## Public discussion
The July 2018 publication is a public escalation from private dialogue, not an unrelated second investment. Its criticism that Nestlé divested less than 2% of sales was the activist's dated assessment. Contemporary authenticated social threads not recovered. [3]
