## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2011 credit entry; details supplied retrospectively.** Third Point bought unsecured debt and preferred securities when the market feared Ally would absorb excessive liabilities from mortgage subsidiary ResCap. The January 2014 letter explicitly dates initial investment to 2011 and distinguishes it from the later private equity accumulation. A precise 2011 cost or original claim-by-claim model was not recovered. [1,2]

Ally’s valuable auto-finance franchise and parent/subsidiary legal boundaries had to be distinguished from ResCap’s mortgage losses. The later $185bn asset size, $50bn deposit base and earnings plan are 2013/14 information and must not be backdated into the first credit purchase. [2]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“We invested initially in 2011 in Ally’s unsecured debt and preferred securities” — January 21, 2014 retrospective. [2]

“market estimates of potential liabilities related to the company’s wholly owned mortgage subsidiary, Rescap, were excessive.” — same source. The statement describes the original legal-liability thesis, not a 2014 IPO pitch. [2]

## What Third Point did
Started in debt/preferred in 2011. Ally stopped funding ResCap losses directly and ResCap entered Chapter 11 in 2012. The October 2012 letter lists Ally among top-five quarterly winners. In the six months before the January 2014 letter Third Point acquired about 9.5% of Ally common in private transactions; this is a later security/thesis phase, not a verified 2011 equity percentage. [2,3]

## What happened after
By the 2014 retrospective, Ally had settled mortgage-related claims for about $2bn after creditor negotiations and disposed of international operations with $30bn assets. Loeb said the settlement matched expectations and described debt above par. He then forecast funding-cost improvement, rising online deposits, TARP exit/IPO and equity worth more than book. Those latter forecasts were not 2011 known facts or already-realized returns. Total fund profit and final equity exit remain unknown. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Bound ResCap liability rather than extrapolate fear:** Parent exposure was the central 2011 mispricing argument. [2]
- **Underwrite the surviving auto-finance franchise:** A viable residual business supported value after mortgage separation. [2]
- **Use multiple capital-structure claims:** Unsecured/preferred entry preceded private common accumulation. [2]
- **Funding-cost and operational upside after cleanup:** This became the later equity thesis; it is not substituted for the original liability thesis. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: resembles Chrysler Financial in legal-entity discipline but has a multi-year transformation into an equity holding. Keep the 2011 credit decision and 2013 equity additions separate in training labels. [2,3]

## Public discussion
MarketFolly’s March 2012 summary supplies a period public locator. The detailed recovered evidence is Loeb’s 2014 retrospective; original 2011 full letter, contrary liability estimates and transaction-level realized returns remain gaps. [1,2]
