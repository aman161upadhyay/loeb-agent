## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**April 2006 snapshot.** Nabi had valuable product, development and biologics assets but, in Loeb’s view, a failed development strategy and cash burn of around $25m in the first quarter were eroding realizable shareholder value. The first 8.4% 13D was the week before the April 27 letter; first purchase and average basis remain unknown. [1]

Loeb argued that piecemeal partnerships could impair whole-company buyer interest and criticized retention/incentive arrangements as rewarding poor performance. Those were activist assessments. Management’s May 1 rebuttal defended its team, plan and strategic review. [1,4]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“these valuable assets should be monetized in the near-term for the benefit of the company’s owners” — April 27, 2006. [1]

The desired cash return was to shareholders rather than another round of speculative research expenditure; distinguish this liquidation/monetization argument from endorsement of an expensive new drug-development cycle. [1]

## What Third Point did
Third Point challenged Lehman’s role, urged fresh advisers and a genuine sale process, and pushed against proceeds being reinvested in the same strategy. August letter continued pressure. November 10 settlement added Jason Aryeh and Timothy Lynch and created a five-member strategic-action committee under the disclosed nomination arrangements. Do not substitute Loeb personally for the actual nominees. [1–3]

## What happened after
Board and strategic-committee concessions are verified. The recovered evidence does not establish total monetization proceeds, distributions received by Third Point, full exit or P&L. The settlement therefore is not sufficient to label a complete financial win. Ongoing means archival return unresolved, not asserted current ownership. [3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Monetize existing assets before cash erosion:** The cash-burn/asset-value comparison motivated urgency. [1]
- **Preserve whole-company optionality:** Exclusive or piecemeal partnerships could reduce a strategic buyer’s interest. [1]
- **Independent sale advice:** Loeb disputed the incumbent advisory process and wanted a fresh test. [1]
- **Governance and incentive reset:** Committee representation and opposition to retention rewards targeted execution. [1–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: another asset-versus-R&D case, but the evidence should not generalize to “all biotech research is waste.” The key was a specific credibility gap and threatened erosion of existing assets. [1,4]

## Public discussion
Read the May 1 issuer response alongside the activist letter: management insisted it had the right plan and team and disputed Loeb’s account. The disagreement is contemporaneous, not retrospective internet sentiment. Authenticated forum commentary remains missing. [4]
