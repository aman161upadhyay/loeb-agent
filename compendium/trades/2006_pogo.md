## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Late 2006 initiation, not first entry in 2007.** Pogo was an oil/gas producer whose acquisition strategy, in Third Point’s view, had failed to convert valuable assets into competitive shareholder returns. The December letter compared approximately 5.8% annualized ten-year performance with 11.7% for peers. Its $1.8bn Northrock acquisition in 2005 plus roughly $350m subsequent capex had accompanied falling production from about 30,000 to 27,000 boe/day, according to Loeb. [1]

These comparisons supported a test of realizable asset value via sale, not simply a commodity-price prediction. Management execution, tax leakage, transaction feasibility and oil/gas markets remained risks. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“we demand that the Board immediately initiate a process to sell the Company in whole or several parts to the highest bidder or bidders.” — December 1, 2006. [1]

The sale demand is the actionable thesis; retrospective descriptions that first entry followed a 2007 rumor misdate the initial decision. [1,3]

## What Third Point did
November 20, 2006 initial 13D; December public demand for a competitive full/parts sale with majority-board challenge if necessary. February 23, 2007 amendment reported 7.9% and escalated the proxy process. March 12 settlement appointed Daniel Loeb and Brad Radoff to the board and resolved that immediate contest. [1–3]

## What happened after
A board settlement is verified. Final asset sale, buyer consideration, shareholding at closing and full fund return were not recovered in this source set. Do not treat board entry as proof that every proposed sale or forecast asset valuation was realized. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Poor long-run returns versus peers:** The performance comparison made continuing the acquisition strategy hard to defend. [1]
- **Northrock acquisition disappointed:** Capital spent versus declining production supplied a specific case. [1]
- **Realize whole/parts asset value:** A competitive sale was the central catalyst, not a vague turnaround. [1]
- **Credible proxy escalation:** Board nominations made the demand enforceable through shareholder voting. [1–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: the Penn Virginia/Western Gas logic repeats—valuable resource assets can be better monetized than reinvested by an incumbent. Actual board access distinguishes an escalation from an empty public letter. [1–3]

## Public discussion
Seeking Alpha circulated the February proxy escalation contemporaneously. This is public documentation of Third Point’s argument, not independent agreement with its valuation. No verified forum-specific competing model was recovered. [4]
