## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**May 14, 2013:** Sony combined entertainment franchises, electronics, gaming and financial interests. Kazuo Hirai had become CEO in 2012. Loeb's letter disclosed approximately $700 million direct ownership and $440 million cash-settled swaps, rounded to $1.1 billion aggregate exposure. This was not a $1.1 billion all-cash common-stock purchase. Third Point estimated entertainment represented over 40% of enterprise value while earning below US peer margins. Those are investor estimates at disclosure, not audited segment valuations. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“For Sony to Change, Sony Must Focus.” — May 14 letter. More precisely, the proposal was to “Take Public a 15—20% Stake in Sony Entertainment,” not dispose of all entertainment. Third Point proposed subscription rights for existing shareholders and offered a $1.5–2 billion backstop without fees. Its scenario of up to 60% share-price upside was a forecast. [1]

**Hindsight, October 2014:** “While, regrettably, the Company rejected our partial spin-out suggestion, they made some changes that were consistent with our goals.” Original PDF extraction normalizes spacing/hyphens; the wording is in the Q3 letter. [3]

## What Third Point did
| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| May 14, 2013 | Public letter; partial entertainment listing, debt reduction, electronics focus and willingness to join board. [1] |
| August 6, 2013 | Sony board rejected partial entertainment listing after review. [2] |
| October 21, 2014 letter | Reported exiting and nearly 20% return; exact final sale date unknown. [3] |

## What happened after
Sony rejected the proposed listing but cut entertainment costs, improved investor dialogue and made management changes. Third Point credited the electronics team with leaving PCs and improving television economics, while acknowledging repeated profit warnings and worse-than-expected electronics news. The letter reports nearly 20% investment return; it does not establish an annualized or fund-net return. [3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Hidden assets:** separate earnings streams obscured one another's value. [1]
- **Margin benchmark:** entertainment peer margins implied substantial potential EBITDA uplift. [1]
- **Financing/incentives:** minority equity and rights could finance electronics while retaining parent control. [1]
- **Focus/product cycle:** remove weak commoditized product lines; recognize strong sensors, gaming and cameras. [1]
- **Independent downside protection:** October's retrospective explicitly says activist change should not be the only source of safety. [3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: foundational large-cap constructivism in Era B. A profitable trade can coexist with rejection of the headline proposal. Sony II in 2019 is a separate decision with a different proposed separation and must not be merged into this return history. [1–3]

## Public discussion
Sony's August response is the essential contemporary counterargument: it maintained wholly owned entertainment better supported group strategy and promised operating improvements. This is stronger opposing evidence than an unsourced “Japan rejected activism” generalization. No authenticated social-media thread recovered. [2]
