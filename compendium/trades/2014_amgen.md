## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**October 21, 2014 disclosure:** Amgen's mature cash-generating drugs and new-product pipeline were valued together amid skepticism about R&D returns, cost structure and capital allocation. Third Point's scientific/medical advisers supported diligence. The roughly $249/share two-year separation scenario in the letter was an activist forecast, not fair value established by an actual transaction. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
Third Point called Amgen a “hidden value situation” and explored “MatureCo” and “GrowthCo.” The exact original PDF has spacing defects in the recovered mirror; quotation is limited to these unambiguous phrases. [1]

**February 2015 update:** “We also remain focused on Amgen's R&D productivity, given the company's disappointing returns on its $30 billion in R&D spending over the past decade.” This is an updated assessment, not proof that all spending lacked value. [2]

## What Third Point did
In 2014 Third Point accumulated a large stake, engaged management and advocated focused R&D, longer-term margin guidance and shareholder returns. It added corporate separation as a way to make the distinct cash-flow/growth profiles visible. The next letter welcomed transformation commitments but continued to press R&D decision-making and more granular disclosure. Exact purchase dates, lot sizes and eventual exit remain unknown. [1–2]

## What happened after
The Q4 2014 letter reports a targeted operating-margin move from 38% to 52% by 2018, a 30% dividend increase and accelerated repurchases. It reports shares rose nearly 45% in calendar 2014. Those are respectively a **target**, announced capital actions and an **issuer stock return**; none establishes Third Point's realized P&L or that the proposed corporate split occurred. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Two earnings profiles:** mature products and pipeline had different investor constituencies. [1]
- **R&D returns:** better project selection mattered as much as a bigger research budget. [1–2]
- **Margins:** quantify the cost-structure opportunity over several years. [1–2]
- **Capital:** dividends/buybacks could return surplus cash. [1–2]
- **Optional separation:** recognize value without assuming the board would accept a split. [1]
