## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**September–October 2014:** Alibaba had listed publicly, allowing direct exposure distinct from earlier SoftBank look-through ownership. Third Point's Q3 letter emphasized commerce growth, margin leverage and assets outside the core marketplace. Buying following the IPO does not establish an allocation at the IPO offer price. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
The letter identifies “Aliyun,” “China Smart Logistics” and “AliPay” as sources of hidden value. Longer verbatim quotation is withheld because the recovered PDF-text mirror collapses spaces. The authenticated substance is a commerce growth/platform and hidden-asset thesis, not a demand for board change. [1]

## What Third Point did
Established a significant direct position following the IPO and disclosed the rationale October 21. Filing-history compilation lists Alibaba among Q4 2016 exits. An exact final execution date, derivative residuals and cost basis are not recovered. The **2017 re-entry is separate** in `2017_alibaba_reentry.md`. [1–2]

## What happened after
No defensible realized dollar return recovered. A 13F exit establishes absence of that reported US security at quarter-end, not absence of every possible economic exposure. Do not infer profit from subsequent Alibaba share performance or combine two separate ownership episodes. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Growth:** expanding e-commerce penetration was the base earnings engine. [1]
- **Leverage:** platform growth could improve earnings faster than sales. [1]
- **Hidden assets:** cloud, logistics and payments supplied additional option value. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: early evidence of the quality/platform toolkit alongside event-driven positions, not exclusively activism. [1]

## Public discussion
The Q3 letter's analysis is the investor's case, not proof that hidden assets would be realized for ADR owners. Authenticated contemporary opposing forums/X posts remain unlocated. [1]
