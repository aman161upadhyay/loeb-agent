## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2013 entry, described retrospectively in May 2015:** Third Point bought for civil aircraft engines and automotive turbochargers. The later Q1 2014 thesis added valuable real estate and an undeserved conglomerate discount. Exact entry-date financial statements and price are unknown; the 2014 ¥1,000-plus valuation cannot be backdated to the purchase. [1–2]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“We see the intrinsic value of IHI at more than ¥1000 per share, well above where the stock trades today.” — Q1 2014 letter. **May 2015 update:** “We expect that at the AGM in June, investors in IHI will demand answers from management about why the company continues to spend shareholder capital so irresponsibly”. [1–2]

## What Third Point did
Invested in 2013, outlined the discount/franchise case in 2014 and publicly criticized non-core investments in 2015. No verified board seat or final exit recovered. [1–2]

## What happened after
Aerospace profits improved, but E&C and a Brazilian shipyard investment undermined the case. The 2015 letter cited a ¥29 billion extraordinary loss and over ¥17 billion in debt guarantees. These are adverse thesis updates, not facts available at the initial purchase. Full-period fund P&L unknown. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Aerospace:** attractive technology franchise supported core value. [1–2]
- **Turbochargers:** fuel-efficiency demand provided another growth engine. [1–2]
- **Discount:** unrelated activities and real estate obscured value. [1]
- **Allocation:** good assets did not protect against poor peripheral investments. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a useful negative constructivist case—hidden assets and growth do not automatically prevent capital leakage. [1–2]

## Public discussion
The AGM challenge made capital allocation a public issue. Independent contemporary forum/X sources not recovered; the critical assessment is Third Point's own. [2]
