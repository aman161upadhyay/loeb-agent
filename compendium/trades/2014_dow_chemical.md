## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**January 21, 2014:** Third Point's largest investment was Dow. The letter compared ten-year total return of 46% with 199% for the S&P chemicals index and 101% for the S&P 500, using January 10 data. It argued cheap North American feedstocks had not translated into appropriate profits because downstream migration, subsidies between segments and poor disclosure obscured under-earning. The letter appeared in January but is titled Q4 2013: exact first purchase is unknown, not automatically January 2014. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“The review should explicitly explore whether separating Dow's petrochemical businesses via a spin-off would drive greater stakeholder value.” The analysis contemplated **keeping upstream/downstream petrochemicals together**, while separating specialty activities. Forecast standalone petrochemical EBITDA above $9 billion and specialty EBITDA $4–5 billion were modeled future earnings, not achieved 2013 results. [1]

## What Third Point did
| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| January 21, 2014 | Requested objective strategic review and petrochemical/specialty separation. [1] |
| November 21, 2014 | Agreement to add four independent directors: Mark Loughridge, Ray Milchovich, Steve Miller and Richard Davis. First three to join January 2015, Davis May 2015. [2] |
| December 2015, discussed Q1 2016 | Dow/DuPont merger and subsequent break-up became a new catalyst sequence. [3] |

## What happened after
Four seats in the settlement were **not four Third Point employees**; Loeb himself did not receive a seat in this agreement. The one-year standstill traded public campaign freedom for governance changes. The later merger and break-up are treated in `2017_dowdupont.md` as a continuation/catalyst phase, not an independently additive P&L trade. Verified original-lot realized profit and ultimate exit remain unknown. [2–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Under-earning:** feedstock advantages versus realized petrochemical returns. [1]
- **Separation:** commodity and specialty businesses deserved distinct strategies and valuation. [1]
- **Self-help:** several billion dollars of potential annual EBITDA improvement was the activist estimate. [1]
- **Disclosure:** opaque internal transfer pricing blocked measurement and accountability. [1]
- **Capital allocation:** buybacks and balance-sheet flexibility mattered alongside operations. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: major Era B constructivist campaign that combines earnings normalization with a corporate event. It was not merely a macro bet on ethylene prices; nor did the original plan require separating the integrated petrochemical chain internally. [1]

## Public discussion
The joint settlement emphasizes amicable resolution; it does not endorse every element of Third Point's valuation. Dow's integration defense is addressed explicitly in the original letter. No authenticated social/forum discussion recovered. [1–2]
