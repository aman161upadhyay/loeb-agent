## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2007 initiation, explained in July 2008.** Loeb explicitly says EXCO was entered in 2007 because production and a large asset base made it cheap. The detailed recovered discussion is later: excellent management, Haynesville and Marcellus shale exposure, and a discount to peers. Neither exact first-fill month nor a 2007 valuation model is supplied. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“When we entered Exco in 2007, we believed it was cheap based on its production levels and huge asset base.” — July 25, 2008. [1]

## What Third Point did
Retained through Q2 2008, when EXCO became the largest quarterly contributor. Expected a multi-billion-dollar joint venture selling close to half the shale assets, with debt repayment, shareholder dividend and accelerated exploration. Those were anticipated catalysts, not already closed transactions in this letter. [1]

## What happened after
The quarterly table gives an +86.6% security move and names EXCO the biggest winner; it does not supply a dollar contribution or exit return. The same letter describes energy reversals and portfolio losses in July. Final ownership duration and economic result remain unknown, so win refers to the disclosed interim period only. [1]

- **Cheap reserves/production asset base:** The letter explicitly attributes initial 2007 entry to this valuation gap. [1]
- **Management and shale opportunity:** Later conviction rested on execution quality and resource optionality. [1]
