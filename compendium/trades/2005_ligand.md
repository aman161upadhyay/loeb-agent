## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**September 2005 campaign snapshot.** Loeb distinguished valuable scientific assets, intellectual property and tax attributes from management’s commercial execution and accounting/restatement troubles. The September 30 follow-up raised his estimated downside-case value to $17 from $14, citing approximately $600m NOLs, real estate and reported acquirer interest. Those are investor assumptions about monetizable value, not a binding buyer offer. [1,2]

A 10% poison pill and delayed shareholder meeting constrained escalation. The governance plan sought to preserve assets while resolving restatements and testing strategic alternatives, rather than funding the status quo indefinitely. [2]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“we have increased our $14 worst-case valuation of your shares to $17 per share.” — September 30, 2005. [2]

This is a downside **estimate**, not a guarantee against mark-to-market loss. The original-entry question was who could best monetize the scientific/IP assets. [1,2]

## What Third Point did
September letters demanded an independent strategic process, a three-person committee including two Third Point representatives, accounting repairs, an annual meeting and an increase in or removal of the pill threshold. The December 2006 issuer agreement describes the December 2, 2005 arrangement that added Daniel Loeb, Jeffrey Perry and Brigette Roberts to the board. Third Point still held 7.375m shares in December 2006. [1–3]

## What happened after
Issuer results dated February 20, 2008 confirm oncology products sold in October 2006 and AVINZA sold in February 2007. Operating expenses from continuing operations fell from $85.5m in 2006 to $75m in 2007, but continuing operations still lost $34.8m in 2007. Thus asset monetization occurred without a whole-company sale or instant disappearance of operating losses. Exact special distributions, split-adjusted cost, final exit and Third Point P&L remain unknown here. [4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Separate IP value from operating failures:** Scientific assets and management competence were treated as distinct underwriting questions. [1,2]
- **Test strategic buyers:** An independent special committee was the proposed monetization mechanism. [2]
- **Preserve tax and property value:** NOLs and real estate raised Loeb’s downside estimate, subject to realizability. [2]
- **Change governance and remove defenses:** Board representation and a less restrictive pill enabled an alternative strategy. [2,3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a royalty/IP asset case with an activist mechanism, not a clean acquisition-arbitrage win. Recovered evidence supports restructuring but not a confident profit classification. Ongoing here means archive outcome unresolved, not current ownership. [1–4]

## Public discussion
The issuer’s later financial release supplies counterweight to a victory-only activist narrative: continuing losses persisted after major product disposals. Authentic contemporary forum reactions and a complete economic return calculation were not recovered. [4]
