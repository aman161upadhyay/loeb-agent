## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**September 8, 2011, after Bartz’s dismissal.** The original letter disclosed 65m shares including exercisable options / 5.1%, Third Point’s third-largest outside-owner position. Exact first purchase date remains incompletely verified; August entry is approximate. Yahoo’s depressed core internet business was entangled with valuable Alibaba and Yahoo Japan stakes and strained partner relationships. [1]

At $13.61, Loeb deducted $2.49/share tax-adjusted net cash, $3.10 Japan and $5.24 Alibaba after-tax value, leaving core at $2.78 or 2.2x 2012 estimated EBITDA. A 7x core rerating implied over $19; tax-efficient Asian monetization could add $3–4 and Alibaba growth another $5 over the medium term. These are scenario valuations, not sums already realized or a prediction that Alibaba would IPO immediately. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“merely replacing the Company’s CEO – yet again – will not be enough” — September 8, 2011. [1]

“a reconstituted Board with new Directors who will bring fresh eyes, relevant industry expertise and increased investor alignment” — same letter. The resume controversy came later; it was not the initial investment thesis. [1]

## What Third Point did
September 2011: demanded Bostock and other director departures, offered candidates, sought partner repair, business review and capital optimization, and threatened proxy nominations. May 2012: disclosed Thompson’s inaccurate computer-science credential and escalated demands for accountability. A May 13 settlement subsequently gave Loeb, Harry Wilson and Michael Wolf board positions, as the 2013 issuer filing confirms. [1–4]

July 22, 2013: agreed to sell 40m shares at $29.11, with three director resignations effective July 31. **Date precision:** the agreement scheduled July 24 closing, but the issuer’s final July 25 8-K says purchase actually completed July 25. Use the completed event, not the earlier schedule. [4,5]

## What happened after
The issuer paid **$1,164,400,000 gross consideration**, not net investment profit. Reuters reported around 20m shares retained, so July 2013 ended the board campaign but was not full portfolio liquidation. Original purchase cost, options, later adds/trims and residual realization are required for complete P&L and IRR and remain unknown here. The sale price was far above the original $13.61 quotation, but a simple price ratio is not Third Point’s weighted-average return. [1,4–6]

The governance catalyst and monetization opportunity succeeded broadly; assigning all subsequent appreciation to operational repair would omit the original Asian-asset thesis and market repricing. [1,6]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Hidden Asian-asset sum of parts:** After-tax Alibaba and Japan stakes explained much of the apparent valuation gap. [1]
- **Core business not irreparably damaged:** Loeb argued management/governance, not absence of franchise assets, depressed core value. [1]
- **Replace board as well as CEO:** Repeat CEO changes had not fixed incentives and strategic execution. [1]
- **Restore partners and optimize capital:** Asian relationships, tax structure and repurchases were explicit catalysts. [1]
- **Escalate with verifiable governance evidence:** The 2012 biography issue supplied leverage after, not before, the economic thesis. [2,3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: fuller, institutionally scaled version of early asset-rich/poorly governed campaigns. A concrete valuation model came first; the later governance scandal accelerated access. Selling much of a winner while retaining upside distinguishes an activist campaign endpoint from the investment endpoint. [1–6]

## Public discussion
Reuters’s July 23 report explains the sale, Asian asset repricing and retained stake. The 2013 8-K says resignations were consistent with ownership falling below 2%, not a management dispute. No authenticated period Twitter/Reddit/VIC/COBF model was recovered. Neither governance drama nor a $1bn proceeds headline substitutes for net P&L. [4,6]
