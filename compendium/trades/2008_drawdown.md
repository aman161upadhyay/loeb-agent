## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**July 25, 2008 letter, before the worst phase.** Offshore was +6.2% in Q2 and +1.6% YTD; long energy and short financials had driven gains. EXCO, MasterCard, an anonymous financial short, Plains and Questar were quarterly winners. Deutsche Boerse, Daimler, Yahoo, Huntsman and Phoenix were losers. These are position price/contribution disclosures from that date, not a complete attribution of the later annual drawdown. [1]

Loeb still liked shale/resource monetization but described Europe/India exits and recognized July’s energy reversal and financial-short squeeze. The crisis could make ostensibly independent positions correlate; the recovered midyear letter is not proof he had de-risked sufficiently before September. [1]

**July loss mechanism actually disclosed:** the largest exposure was energy; some stocks fell over 30% in a week while financial shorts rallied. Loeb reported high-single-digit July losses by the July 25 letter and said energy exposure had been cut despite conviction in valuation. This is direct evidence of adverse factor co-movement before the later crisis, not a complete full-year attribution. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“the best opportunities in upstream companies that benefit from high oil and gas prices” — July 25, 2008. This records contemporaneous exposure, not hindsight. [1]

“capital we would be very pleased to lose should such protection prove unnecessary” — Q4 2009 letter reproduced March 8, 2010, on planned tail-hedging expenditure around 2% per annum. This is a later risk response, not a verified 2008 trading rule. [5]

## What Third Point did
Q4 2008 13F coverage records exit of Plains, Leap Wireless, Time Warner, Tibco, BUD and other longs, reductions in multiple energy/credit-sensitive equities, and a new GLD position. A decline in the reported 13F book from about $1.7bn to $799m combines sales, marks and disclosure coverage; it is not a fund loss or redemption figure. [3]

By April 1, 2009 long/short net was -3.4%; by June 30 it was 37%, credit over 40% and arbitrage over 20%. He explicitly shifted as public intervention reduced perceived systemic-collapse risk. In July 2009 he changed redemption terms while stressing liability/portfolio-liquidity matching. [4]

## What happened after
NJ’s March 18, 2011 allocator memorandum reports **Partners -32.84% in 2008**, followed by +38.14% in 2009 and +41.79% in 2010. The same review records two risk-reporting staff, weekly risk committee, RiskPoint, and a general sizing guideline limiting estimated downside to 2% at the position level. It does not establish when each control was introduced, that loss could never exceed 2%, or a hard 2% stop-loss. [2]

**Primary gap:** the complete original Q4 2008 letter was not recovered. Frequently surfaced Scribd document 27903456 is Q4 **2009**, not 2008; do not train invented “rules adopted immediately after Lehman” from that mislabel. Annual drawdown attribution by security, gross leverage and exact peak-to-trough NAV remain unknown.

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Energy longs / financial shorts had worked:** Midyear realized success supported positions subsequently exposed to regime change. [1]
- **Distinguish fundamental value from liquidity risk:** The year-end book contraction and later liquidity matching illustrate the issue; precise causal attribution remains incomplete. [3,4]
- **Update systemic-risk assessment:** 2009 intervention changed net exposure and credit allocations materially. [4]
- **Explicit downside and tail protection:** Later sources verify probability-weighted position sizing and a proposed protection budget, not a guaranteed loss cap. [2,5]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: essential losing counterweight to activist success stories. Correctly shorting troubled financials did not prevent portfolio-level losses. The evidenced evolution is a later explicit framework for downside, liquidity and tail events, with implementation dates left unknown rather than invented. [1–5]

## Public discussion
MarketFolly’s April 2009 analysis exposes broad reductions but covers only reportable long US securities, not shorts, sovereign/credit positions or total fund exposure. NJ is an allocator due-diligence source, not a Loeb quote. Public forum threads and a complete original Q4 2008 letter remain gaps. [2,3]
