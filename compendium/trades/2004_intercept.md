## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**May 27, 2004 campaign snapshot.** InterCept supplied technology to banks but had diverted capital into iBill and EPX, acquired for about $155m and sold for about $51m, a $104m loss in Loeb’s accounting. He attacked a proposed management buyout, related-party dealings, corporate aircraft and family employment as evidence that shareholders needed an independent process. These governance accusations are attributed to his letter, not adjudicated findings. [1]

Sprout’s roughly $10m preferred investment came with influence over a sale; Loeb objected to the attempt to reduce its conversion price from $13.97 to $10.50. That conflict mattered to the distribution of acquisition value, not just operating efficiency. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“Third Point will support Jana’s efforts to control the Company’s Board and effect a sale of the Company to the highest bidder.” — May 27, 2004 (apostrophe normalized). [1]

## What Third Point did
Third Point disclosed 7.1% and supported JANA’s effort to replace the board rather than demanding its own operating role. The company’s June 14 8-K reported a resumed sale process, Jefferies engagement and settlement with JANA; distinguish the independent activist’s settlement from Third Point receiving board seats. [1,2]

## What happened after
A formal sale-process response is verified. This case does not certify final buyer, closing consideration, Third Point sale date or realized return: those terminal facts remain unknown in this source set. The governance concession is not itself a measured investment win. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Stop unrelated value destruction:** The iBill/EPX acquisition-and-disposal arithmetic was the central capital-allocation example. [1]
- **Eliminate management-buyout conflicts:** An independent highest-bidder process was the proposed remedy. [1]
- **Constrain preferred-holder veto economics:** Sprout’s sale rights and repricing proposal could divert common-holder value. [1]
- **Support another activist’s board slate:** JANA supplied the governance mechanism; collaboration was pragmatic. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: activism need not mean personal board seats. A small portfolio position could support a larger holder’s control contest when the sale mechanism was credible. Loeb explicitly distinguished a $22m position from $1.1bn firm assets. [1]

## Public discussion
The SEC-filed issuer settlement documents the practical response. Loeb’s allegations about personal conduct should not substitute for the economic argument. No authenticated contemporaneous forum counter-thesis was recovered. [1,2]
