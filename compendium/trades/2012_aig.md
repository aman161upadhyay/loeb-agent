## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**March 2012 entry.** Treasury’s desire to sell AIG shares created a non-economic seller, in Loeb’s view anchored to its $29 cost basis and political need to exit. Company buybacks in those offerings supplied capital return and downside support; a larger free float implied index-sensitive buying. The $29 number was Treasury’s basis, not Third Point’s purchase price. [1]

The October letter’s roughly 10x 2013 consensus earnings and half of $65 pro forma tangible book describe the later accumulation/retention decision, not a verified March entry multiple. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“‘forced’ (or non-economically-motivated) selling” — October 3, 2012, explaining the March entry. [1]

“our initial thesis for AIG was only the prologue” — same letter, explicitly marking the shift from technical trade toward a core turnaround position. [1]

## What Third Point did
Bought March 2012, added in second- and third-quarter Treasury offerings and open market. Re-underwrote as a cheap post-reorganization-like equity simplifying non-core operations and improving underwriting. No Third Point board campaign is established by these sources. [1]

## What happened after
At October 2012, expected catalysts included AIA lockup expiry enabling sale of a 13.7% stake worth about $6.1bn, ILFC sale/spin/listing yielding over $5bn excess capital, eventual Treasury exit and Chartis ROE improving toward 10–12% by 2015. These are contemporaneous estimates and forecasts, **not all completed actions**. Actual terminal return and final exit remain unknown in this recovery; do not score as a realized win merely because the government exited. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Non-economic Treasury seller:** Political/time constraints could create a price discount. [1]
- **Capital return plus index demand:** Buybacks and increasing free float were separate technical supports. [1]
- **Post-reorg-like complexity discount:** Non-core disposals and simplified structure broadened the thesis. [1]
- **Operational underwriting improvement:** Chartis pricing/mix and targeted ROE supplied fundamental upside beyond seller exhaustion. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: strong example of thesis evolution while maintaining a margin-of-safety framework. An initial technical catalyst can end without requiring exit if the reassessed operating value remains attractive; this must not be confused with moving a target after failure. [1]

## Public discussion
MarketFolly reproduced the letter contemporaneously. The potential counter-case is embedded in the source: regulatory approval for capital return, insurance turnaround execution and non-core sale proceeds were not assured. No authenticated period forum valuation debate was recovered. [1,2]
