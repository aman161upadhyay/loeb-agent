## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Q4 2012:** Third Point bought Morgan Stanley at $16.77 average, approximately 35% below tangible book. By January's update the discount was about 20%. The letter viewed a financial turnaround, not a distressed debt purchase. [1–2]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“We hope Morgan Stanley will show that its reinvention begins at the top and set an example for the company by quickly revising its board practices.” — investor letter quoted by NYT. The criticism concerned board compensation despite a broadly favorable management/franchise assessment. [2]

## What Third Point did
Accumulated approximately 7.8 million shares in Q4, criticized board compensation and sold the common position during Q1 2013. This file does not combine possible later MS re-entries. [1–2]

## What happened after
NYT inferred a likely profit; it reports MS rose 15% in Q1 to $21.98, but that period-end price is **not the fund's sale VWAP**. Exact realized gain unknown. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Turnaround:** corporate reinvention supported rerating. [1–2]
- **Price:** tangible-book discount supplied the valuation anchor. [1]
- **Governance:** directors' compensation should reflect shareholder outcomes. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: rapid monetization of a turnaround/valuation opportunity despite a broader long-term corporate narrative. [1–2]

## Public discussion
NYT documents the apparent contrast between praising management and challenging director pay. Authentic contemporary forum/X threads not recovered. [2]
