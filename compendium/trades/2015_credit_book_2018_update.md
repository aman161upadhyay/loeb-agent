## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Coverage note:** this is a multi-instrument strategy chronology, not a single fictional security or a substitute for named trade files. In the Q4 2015 letter Third Point said nearly half its profits since 2009 came from credit and its corporate credit team was then net short. This is historical strategy attribution, not a 2015 credit return or a permanent short-credit policy. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
The Q1 2016 letter says it shifted “from net short to net long by covering shorts and aggressively adding to our energy credit positions.” That willingness to reverse is the key driver, not a constant directional prediction. [2]

By Q2 2018, it had reduced the “net performing credit book to zero” because of a “dearth of attractive opportunities and rising interest rate risk.” Zero **net performing** credit does not mean no gross credit, no distressed credit or no structured products. [5]

## What happened after
The September 2016 account of the **Third Point Reinsurance portfolio** reports Q2 corporate-credit return on average exposure of 15.1%, structured-credit 2.6%, and Argentine government bonds 11.7% for the quarter/20.4% YTD. These are **strategy RoA figures for a specified account**, not fund NAV contributions, annualized returns or universal returns across all funds. [3]

The 2017 audited reinsurance tables establish an actual shift in ABS composition toward reperforming and marketplace loans; they do not independently disclose Loeb's instrument-specific reasoning or prove the change caused gains. The Q1 2018 letter reports structured products +6.9% on average exposure. It explicitly treats rich credit pricing and rates as reasons to reduce risk despite prior gains. A complete 2015–2017 monthly exposure/P&L bridge and the original Dell/Sprint instrument terms remain gaps; no aggregate credit-book gain is invented. [4,6,8]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Flexibility:** change direction when conditions change, even after a recent short thesis. [1–2]
- **Dislocation:** performing energy paper offered attractive rebound exposure without requiring broad distressed ownership. [3]
- **Collateral:** housing and consumer underwriting supported structured instruments, not an indiscriminate high-yield index bet. [4–5]
- **Relative value:** equities could be preferable when spreads were too tight and duration unattractive. [4–5]
- **Cycle discipline:** 2019 warning about leveraged loans/CLO collateral extends the cautious late-cycle stance, rather than proving a dated short position in every CLO. [7]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: the archive must represent Third Point as a cross-asset investor, not just an activist equity fund. Equity, performing credit, distressed credit, sovereigns and securitizations have different payoff/risk drivers; do not collapse them into one “cheap debt” template. [1–5]
