## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Ahead of April 29, 2009 shareholder vote.** Following Belgian/Dutch bank nationalizations, Fortis was left mainly an insurance business with an uncertain BNP transaction, lawsuits and political conflict. Traditional arbitrage holders faced constrained balance sheets after Lehman and short-selling restrictions that made convertible hedges difficult. This created forced sales of hybrids independently of remaining asset value. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“significant nearterm catalysts were likely to unlock this value” — July 31, 2009. [1]

“market assigned almost no probability” to payment of the 8.75% installment, as Loeb interpreted 8-cent mandatory-convertible pricing. This was his probability inference, not a literally observed option-implied model. [1]

## What Third Point did
Initiated common, selected convertibles and other hybrids ahead of the April 29 BNP deal vote. Underwrote contractual and practical reasons the coupon would be paid. After approval and coupon receipts, cost basis fell by more than 50%; retained core positions awaiting half-year financials and operating plan. [1]

## What happened after
Reported approximately 160% return on invested capital over four months. That is an interim position-level claim, not annual fund performance or a fully realized exit multiple. Coupons continued to be paid as of July; exact eventual disposition and weighted costs remain unknown. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Forced selling rather than only weak fundamentals:** Lehman balance-sheet constraints and hedge restrictions affected hybrid holders. [1]
- **Asset-rich residual insurer:** The surviving business mattered after the banks were separated. [1]
- **Contractual coupon mispricing:** 8-cent pricing offered a highly asymmetric coupon/recovery opportunity in Loeb’s view. [1]
- **Shareholder vote as catalyst:** April 29 approval could resolve transaction uncertainty. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a forced-seller case with document-level security selection. The common, hybrid and convertible exposures must not be collapsed into a single generic “bank preferred” price or a bailout bet. [1]

## Public discussion
The letter reports market fatigue and diminished research coverage, not an authenticated consensus survey. Litigation and political uncertainty were real contrary risks in the original account. No verified contemporaneous forum thread was recovered. [1]
