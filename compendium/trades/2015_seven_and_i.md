## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**October 2015 thesis:** Seven & i's convenience stores had pricing power, US sales momentum and international potential, but supermarket/general-merchandise operations obscured their value and consumed resources. Third Point had added through Q3 and described the balance sheet as underlevered. Exact first purchase, position size and entry multiple remain unknown. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“If Seven & i evolves into a global convenience store pure-play, shareholders would benefit from a meaningful rerating.” Also: “Shareholders have suffered from the subsidization of Ito Yokado for so long that Seven & i is emerging as one of the most crucial tests for the success of corporate governance reform in Japan.” — Q3 2015 letter. [1]

## What Third Point did
Added during Q3 2015 and argued for business focus/capital discipline. In March 2016 Loeb publicly opposed nepotism in selecting leadership. In April, the board rejected the chairman's proposal to remove Ryuichi Isaka and Toshifumi Suzuki announced his departure. The retrieved sources do not justify attributing every director's vote solely to Third Point. [1–3]

## What happened after
The succession dispute supplied a governance catalyst, not proof of a completed convenience-store pure-play transformation in 2016. Exit date, full implementation and Third Point realized return remain unknown. `open` refers to last verified campaign state, not current ownership. Later activists' Seven & i campaigns must not be retroactively assigned to Loeb. [1–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Franchise quality:** convenience-store economics anchored valuation. [1]
- **Cross-subsidy:** Ito Yokado was a specific source of value leakage. [1]
- **Focus:** a cleaner business mix could command a higher multiple. [1]
- **Capital:** underleverage provided room for more efficient allocation. [1]
- **Succession:** management selection became a concrete governance test. [2–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: quality franchise plus fixable allocation problem, with Japanese governance reform as an enabling condition. The willingness to challenge succession moved a constructive thesis into public confrontation. [1–3]

## Public discussion
March–April Japanese/Reuters reporting focuses on the chairman's succession proposal and the board's independence, offering a more specific debate than “foreign activist versus Japan.” Authenticated investor-forum/X sources not recovered. [2–3]
