## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Summer 2009.** Healthcare-reform uncertainty coincided with Health Net’s announced Tricare contract loss and Northeast asset sale to UnitedHealth. Loeb believed the contract decision was not final and the stock failed to reflect residual business value. The recovered letter does not establish the first-fill month. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“the prevailing price did not reflect the residual value in the business” — Q4 2009 letter reproduced March 8, 2010. [1]

## What Third Point did
Established common exposure in summer 2009; reported 3m shares at year-end. Retained for cost reduction, clarification of healthcare legislation and possible industry consolidation, praising CEO Jay Gellert’s ownership alignment. No proxy campaign or Third Point board seat is established. [1]

## What happened after
The letter forecast increased profitability over coming quarters, regulatory clarity and consolidation benefits. It does not supply complete realized return, sale date or proof that all catalysts occurred. Ongoing marks an unresolved archive outcome, not current ownership. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Residual value after headline loss:** Contract pessimism and asset disposal appeared to obscure surviving business value. [1]
- **Regulatory-overhang resolution:** Clarification of healthcare reform could reduce uncertainty. [1]
- **Cost reduction and scale consolidation:** Operational savings and larger-industry economics supplied prospective catalysts. [1]
- **Aligned management:** Loeb explicitly praised Gellert’s significant stock ownership. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: constructive long without a management fight; a familiar management-alignment screen applied to a business affected by policy uncertainty. Do not confuse this with the contemporaneous Citi/CIT credit trades. [1]
