## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**October 2, 2013 campaign date:** Third Point disclosed 9.3%, described itself as largest shareholder, and argued Sotheby's was losing competitive ground to Christie's despite its brand. Its letter compared expenses tracking near 2007 levels against revenue about 15% lower, criticized rebates/guarantees and identified modern/contemporary art, private sales, internet distribution and international expansion as missed opportunities. These are campaign assertions requiring the incumbent's response, not uncontested findings. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“Sotheby's is like an old master painting in desperate need of restoration.” The letter requested Ruprecht step down and sought directors bringing an “owners' perspective.” Its argument was operational and governance-based, not simply a bet on rising art prices. [1]

## What Third Point did
| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| October 2, 2013 | Public letter demanded leadership, governance, strategy and cost changes. [1] |
| 2014 campaign | Fought proxy contest and litigated rights plan. [2] |
| May 5, 2014 | Settlement appointed Dan Loeb, Olivier Reza and Harry Wilson; proxy contest ended. The three seats were secured by settlement, not a completed contested election victory. [2] |
| 2013–2019 | Worked on CEO search, strategy, expense discipline, balance sheet and capital allocation; Tad Smith became CEO. [3] |
| Q3 2019 letter | Reported exit through Patrick Drahi-controlled buyer's acquisition. [3] |

## What happened after
The 2019 letter presents the six-year involvement as an activist success and cites the buyer's 61% premium. An acquisition premium measures announcement price versus a reference market price; it is not six-year TSR or fund P&L. Aggregate cost, dividends, realized profit and exact final settlement date remain unknown here. Crucially, 2014 was the board-settlement year, **not the exit year**. [2–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Competitive gap:** Christie's comparison made the problem specific. [1]
- **Costs/margins:** lack of expense discipline despite lower revenues. [1]
- **Alignment:** CEO pay/perquisites and scant director ownership versus Third Point's capital at risk. [1]
- **Growth strategy:** modern art, online sales and new geographies rather than indiscriminate cuts. [1]
- **Governance mechanism:** three board seats and CEO search created implementation leverage. [1–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: poison-pen rhetoric served a later collaborative board process; the multi-year ownership disproves the idea that every campaign was a short-lived letter trade. Do not equate favorable settlement with proof every operational criticism was right. [1–3]

## Public discussion
The settlement quotes Ruprecht welcoming fresh voices and De Sole defending existing leadership. The rights-plan dispute is material counterevidence to a frictionless activism narrative. No independently authenticated contemporary VIC/COBF/Reddit/X discussion recovered. [2]
