## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Q1 2015:** Yum's China troubles had depressed confidence in a former growth story. Third Point saw early stabilization and a still-long store-growth runway, while the rest of the system generated franchise cash flows. Exact original valuation and stake unknown. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“This ‘open-ended China growth’ story once captivated Yum! investors and we believe it could again” — Q1 2015 letter. This was prospective, not evidence the recovery was already complete. [1]

## What Third Point did
Initiated in Q1, discussed management involvement, upgraded talent and productivity measures in May. The secondary Q4 holdings account reports roughly 99% sold, not necessarily a mathematically complete exit. [1–2]

## What happened after
Final residual, exit date and investment P&L remain unknown. Do not automatically assign later corporate separations to Third Point's originating intervention. [1–2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Recovery:** normalize China earnings rather than extrapolate distress. [1]
- **Runway:** unit expansion remained possible. [1]
- **Execution:** talent/productivity changes supported the turn. [1]
- **Cash:** franchise-heavy operations outside China supplied a separate value source. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: consumer turnaround with growth optionality and active exposure management; major reduction despite a long-run narrative. [1–2]

## Public discussion
The original letter addresses investor focus on China versus overlooked franchise cash flows. No verified contemporary social/forum thread recovered. [1]
