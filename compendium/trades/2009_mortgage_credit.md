## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**April 2009.** Deleveraging removed the former levered buyer base—banks, insurers and CDOs—from much of the approximately $2.2tn non-agency market. Ratings downgrades forced sales to unlevered capital. Third Point hired Keri Anisgarten and began buying again, after profitable subprime shorts in 2007. These are separate decisions, not an uninterrupted long-MBS position. [1]

Modeled purchase returns were 17–20% base case and 10% severe stress; the latter assumed 100% defaults and another 20% home-price decline. Extension and mark-to-market risk remained material even if modeled collateral ultimately covered the claim. Limited initial CMBS exposure reflected expected further commercial-pool deterioration. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“markets with this level of dislocation provide an irresistible opportunity” — July 31, 2009. [1]

“Mark-to-market and duration extension are the biggest risks” — same letter. The thesis was not that default losses had disappeared. [1]

## What Third Point did
Began deploying in April; by July invested about $160m and raised possible allocation from 5–10% to 10–15% of capital. By October 2012 portfolio was around 15% of invested capital, nearly half Re-REMICs with high-teens average current-yield/YTM metric, and much of the rest 2005 subprime mezzanine. Sold 100 bonds and bought 135 with better terms in 2012, adding about $325m exposure; maintained a trading/re-underwriting process rather than simply buying and forgetting. [1,2]

## What happened after
Reported more than $20m profits by July 2009 while explicitly saying four months was too early to declare victory. October 2012 letter reports about 15% YTD return on average exposure and positive returns across collateral categories. These figures are strategy/period-specific, not an investor net IRR or lifetime realized gain. [1,2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Forced deleveraging creates orphaned assets:** Loss of levered buyers and ratings-driven selling changed market-clearing prices. [1]
- **Stress collateral cash flows:** Returns were modeled under explicit defaults and home-price declines. [1]
- **Price liquidity and extension risk:** Ultimate coverage does not eliminate path-dependent marks or duration. [1,2]
- **Trade relative value as capital returns:** 2012 swaps and new shorts exploited renewed enthusiasm without abandoning collateral analysis. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: transition from public activism toward specialist underwriting while retaining catalyst/forced-seller logic. The 2012 letter warned of late-2011 capital flight and possible eminent-domain intervention, so the case is not “housing always recovers.” [1,2]

## Public discussion
The October 2012 letter explicitly contrasts contemporary media enthusiasm for mortgages with Third Point’s earlier entry and notes new short opportunities as pricing became effusive. Independent forum discussions were not recovered. [2]
