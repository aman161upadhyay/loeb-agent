## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2012 campaign snapshot.** Murphy combined E&P, refining and retail/marketing. Over the three years preceding Third Point’s investment the stock fell approximately 15% against roughly 49% gains for the SPDR oil/gas E&P index, according to the October letter. Management had delayed a retail separation and cited underperformance. These are Loeb’s comparisons at disclosure, not a verified original entry date. [1]

Retail generated $363m 2011 EBITDA across over 1,100 stations and had lower ongoing capital needs. Loeb valued it at $2.3–2.8bn; Canadian Montney acreage and Syncrude interests supplied additional sale candidates. Those comparable-transaction asset estimates had taxes and execution risk, not guaranteed liquidity. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“four easy steps to unlock the latent value” — October 3, 2012. [1]

The detailed plan, not the adjective “easy,” is the useful thesis: retail separation, Canadian gas disposal, Syncrude stake sale and UK refining exit. [1]

## What Third Point did
Shared the four-step proposal with management, disclosed a significant stake and filed for HSR approval to preserve capacity to add. CNBC’s just-under-5% stake estimate was sourced reporting, not a recovered 13D. HSR clearance is acquisition flexibility, not proof Third Point bought control or obtained a board seat. [1,2]

## What happened after
Loeb estimated $8.4–8.9bn pretax / $7.3–7.8bn after-tax proceeds from the proposed steps, leaving an E&P business capable of roughly $2.9bn 2013 EBITDA in his forecast. A 3.5x multiple yielded $91–94/share. These remain **proposal economics** here; completed corporate actions, actual tax leakage, Third Point exit and fund P&L were not recovered. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Conglomerate complexity discount:** Disparate operations obscured the E&P value. [1]
- **Retail multiple and cash-flow separation:** A stand-alone retail value exceeded the apparent mixed-company allocation. [1]
- **Sell non-core Canadian/UK exposures:** Comparable-transaction values supplied cash-release scenarios. [1]
- **Focus on remaining oily growth:** Capital and management focus would shift to a more concentrated production profile. [1]
- **Preserve escalation flexibility:** HSR filing supported potential further purchases if private dialogue failed. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: late Era A repeats Western Gas-style tax-aware separation and capital allocation on a larger asset base. A detailed sum-of-parts forecast should be graded separately from actual monetization success. [1]

## Public discussion
CNBC’s contemporaneous report framed Murphy as a new activist target. Its unnamed-source stake estimate must remain distinguishable from original company/13D ownership evidence. No verified period forum counter-model was recovered. [2]
