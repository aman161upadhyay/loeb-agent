## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Q2 2016:** Third Point invested in a late-stage Chinese ride-sharing platform. At the July letter, Didi led Uber in China and Apple had also invested. The later Uber combination should be treated as a subsequent risk update, not necessarily information known before purchase. [1–2]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“We look at this as sort of a bridge equity investment to an IPO which we expect to take place next year.” — reinsurance earnings-call excerpt published September 12, 2016. He explicitly distinguished a late-stage deal from a startup/Series A. [2]

## What Third Point did
Made the Q2 private investment, discussed it in the letter and later call, and declined to disclose position size. The later call viewed Uber's announced combination as reducing competition risk while recognizing China country risk. [1–2]

## What happened after
The call's “next year” IPO was a forecast, not a verified 2017 listing or a contractual liquidity guarantee. Ultimate exit, private preferred terms and realized return remain unknown. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Platform:** leading network position. [1–2]
- **Market:** enormous transportation opportunity. [2]
- **Stage:** expected liquidity differed from early-stage venture. [2]
- **Competition:** subsequent combination changed underwriting. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: venture/private exposure within an opportunistic mandate, with explicit sizing/liquidity awareness rather than a public activist template. [2]

## Public discussion
The call acknowledges country risk; the letter's comparison with Uber is not proof of permanent market dominance. No authenticated contemporary forum/X source recovered. [1–2]
