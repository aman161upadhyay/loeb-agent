## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**July 31, 2015 thesis update:** formerly Actavis, Allergan had agreed to sell its generics business at approximately 17x EBITDA. Third Point's case centered on a focused branded company, balance-sheet capacity and valuation below comparable franchises. This is a campaign-date update; original initiation, acquisition-related security exchanges and lot costs remain unknown. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
**Verbatim original Allergan passage not recovered.** Market Folly's contemporaneous summary says branded assets plus an unlevered balance sheet and a below-comparable valuation created opportunity. This is clearly a paraphrase, not a fabricated quotation. The 2015 letter's broader compounder discussion must not be substituted for a specific Allergan quote. [1]

## What Third Point did
Held and discussed Allergan in the Q2 letter; the Q4 2015 secondary holdings summary reports an approximately 50% increase. Q4 2016 reporting records a full common-stock exit. Neither an exact final execution day nor reason for the final sale has been independently recovered. [1–3]

## What happened after
The available exit evidence verifies direction, not net P&L. It would be hindsight contamination to teach a July 2015 model that a subsequent Pfizer merger/tax-policy outcome was already known. This file does not classify the whole position as a loss simply from a later merger failure or as a win from the generics sale. [1,3]
