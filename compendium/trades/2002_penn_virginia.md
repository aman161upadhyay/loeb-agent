## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**February 27, 2002, not a new 2004 initiation.** Penn Virginia combined oil/gas assets with a large interest in separately traded Penn Virginia Resource Partners (coal/timber) and the GP incentive rights. Loeb valued the PVR units around $175m against roughly $300m parent market value, leaving about $125m for approximately 250 Bcfe reserves, before crediting the GP. This was a sum-of-parts hypothesis, not a valuation established by a sale. [1]

The $112m Synergy acquisition had suffered a $35m writedown within four months, according to the letter. Loeb contrasted 2001 finding costs $1.56/Mcfe and planned 2002 costs $1.22 with buying back reserves through shares at about $0.50/Mcfe. Commodity prices and reserve economics could still change. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“Do not confuse our investment as a vote of confidence in management’s ill-conceived acquisition and exploration strategy.” — February 27, 2002 (apostrophe normalized typographically). [1]

“We believe the Company’s shares are worth at least $50.00” — same letter. [1]

“I firmly believe that it is essential that managers have a stake in the company in the form of shares, not just options.” — March 11, 2003 follow-up. [2]

## What Third Point did
February 2002: disclosed 5.6%, demanded no further dilutive acquisitions, lower drilling, possible oil/gas asset sales, buybacks and realization of GP value. August 2002 BP Capital’s letter recorded Third Point’s separate acquisition proposal and opposed the poison pill. March 2003 Third Point held 880,000 shares and challenged Dearlove’s sales and incentive asymmetry. May 10, 2004 amendment reported 770,000 shares; this is continuation evidence, not proof of a fresh 2004 trade. [1–4]

## What happened after
The retrieved filings do not establish a completed sale, terminal liquidation date or fund P&L. The later insider-selling dispute is evidence of continued dissatisfaction, not proof the original asset values were realized. The 2004 13D amendment supports continued ownership. Outcome class ongoing means **unresolved in the recovered archive**, not a claim that a position remains open today. [2,3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Sum-of-parts discount:** Public PVR units exposed a residual value gap in oil/gas assets. [1]
- **Repurchase cheaper reserves:** Buybacks were cheaper than drilling on Loeb’s stated cost comparison. [1]
- **Stop destructive acquisitions:** Synergy was his concrete example of bad timing and weak returns. [1]
- **Align management with downside:** The 2003 follow-up distinguished outright stock from options. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: cheap assets **despite**, not because of, management. Follow-up shows the ownership/incentive issue can persist after the first letter; this is not a completed “write letter, stock sold” template. [1,2]

## Public discussion
BP Capital’s August 2002 letter independently documents the contested strategic direction and management’s rejection of its $40 bid and recapitalization proposal. These were Pickens proposals, not Loeb purchase prices or achieved exit values. No authenticated Mr. Pink post was recovered. [4]
