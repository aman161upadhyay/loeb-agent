## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**January 9, 2013 disclosure information set:** Ackman's public short presentation had depressed the nutritional-products distributor's stock. Third Point's disclosed long was 8.24% as of January 3. This dates the holding, not necessarily the first purchase: a January disclosure must not be converted into a fabricated January initiation. The contemporaneous letter framed Herbalife as a compounder at a controversy-induced discount. Exact original cost, then-public financial statement snapshot and independent distributor-economics diligence are not recovered. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“Applying a modest 10-12x earnings multiple suggests Herbalife's shares are worth $55-68, offering 40-70% upside from here and making the company a compelling long investment” — Q4 2012 letter, excerpt published January 9, 2013. These are targets, not realized proceeds. [1]

The same excerpt points to the next day's analyst meeting and “the significant short interest.” The claim that Third Point found no pyramid-scheme evidence is a reported characterization of its research, not a verified legal conclusion or an independently authenticated Loeb quotation. [1]

## What happened after
The available April account reports a successful Q1 investment; May's filing coverage confirms the sale. Neither justifies an invented dollar profit or holding-period IRR. The rapid exit is important: this was not a commitment to hold through all subsequent regulatory proceedings. Later Herbalife operating/regulatory history and Ackman's eventual result must not be substituted for Third Point's own short-duration outcome. [2–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Independent controversy underwriting:** taking the opposite side required company-specific research, not merely disagreeing with another manager. [1]
- **Compounder at a discount:** the business-quality assessment preceded the proposed rerating. [1]
- **Explicit valuation:** 10–12x and $55–68 were the disclosed valuation bridge. [1]
- **Near-term catalysts:** company rebuttal and crowded short positioning could accelerate recognition. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: Era B opportunism and asymmetric repricing rather than an activist governance campaign. The exit breaks any simplistic “compounder means permanent holding” rule. Do not infer Loeb shorted Herbalife, joined Ackman's campaign, or stayed for Icahn's entire investment. [1–2]
