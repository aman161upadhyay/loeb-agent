## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Early 2014 purchase:** DSM's profit warning offered a dislocation during a transition away from commodity chemicals toward life sciences/nutrition. July's letter described approximately $12 billion sales and $1.7 billion EBITDA; those are the letter's snapshot, not newly verified financial statements. Nutrition peer valuations and commodity operations made a blended multiple misleading. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“DSM offers compelling risk-reward as its portfolio continues to shift toward that of a high-quality, higher multiple life sciences company.” — July 23, 2014 letter. [1]

## What Third Point did
Built a position earlier in 2014 and advocated portfolio simplification, including potential caprolactam separation. No verified board-control campaign, exact ownership percentage or final sale recovered. [1]

## What happened after
No transaction-level realized outcome established. The 8.5x Performance Materials scenario and 11–13x nutrition peer framework were valuation assumptions, not announced acquisition prices. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Dislocation:** profit warning depressed expectations. [1]
- **Quality:** nutrition mix merited different valuation. [1]
- **Focus:** non-core disposals could reveal the remaining earnings. [1]
- **SOTP:** blended market value obscured distinct assets. [1]
