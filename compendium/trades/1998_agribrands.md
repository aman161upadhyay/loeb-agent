## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Separate entry and intervention dates.** Third Point said it had owned Agribrands since the March 1998 Ralston Purina spin-off. The detailed valuation below is the September 8, 2000 campaign information set, not a reconstructed 1998 model. Stiritz was also Ralcorp chairman; the proposed related-party merger offered a $39 cash option for up to 20% of shares. Loeb cited $160 million cash, $90 million annual EBITDA and $14/share cash net of debt and unfunded pensions. These are his then-current figures and estimates, not independently audited conclusions. [1]

Loeb described animal-feed peers and potential financial buyers, low capital needs and steady cash flows. His thesis was not that the announced Ralcorp terms were safe arbitrage profits: it was that they transferred control too cheaply without a competitive auction. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“Our recent purchases that have pushed us over the 5% threshold are not a vote of support for the Ralcorp merger.” — September 8, 2000. [1]

“Such restructuring transactions include, but are not limited to, a leveraged recapitalization, Dutch tender offer, or special dividend.” — same letter, stating alternatives to the conflicted deal. [1]

## What Third Point did
| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| March 1998 | Established ownership at spin-off, according to later letter. [1] |
| September 2000 | Added roughly 1% of outstanding shares specifically to cross 5% and publicize opposition in a securities filing. [1,2] |
| September 8 / 15, 2000 | Sent / filed detailed letter challenging valuation, sale process and option compensation. [1,2] |
| April 17, 2001 | Reported selling below 5%; publicly praised Cargill outcome. [3] |

## What happened after
Cargill agreed to buy all shares for $54.50 cash on December 4, 2000. Loeb’s April 17, 2001 letter endorsed that alternative and said Third Point had sold below 5%; this does not establish the price or date of liquidation of every remaining share. No fund-level IRR or dollar gain is verified. [3,4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Asset-value discount:** The letter supplies explicit multiples and net-cash/earnings arithmetic. [1]
- **Reject related-party underpricing:** Stiritz’s roles and missing competitive approaches were the process objection; misconduct allegations remain Loeb’s allegations. [1]
- **Monetize cash and steady cash flow:** Auction, recapitalization, tender or dividend were competing means to unlock value. [1]
- **Use 13D threshold deliberately:** The incremental purchase was designed to make opposition public, not to endorse the announced merger. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: early Era A already combines sum-of-parts underwriting, incentive scrutiny and a concrete corporate catalyst. The decisive feature is the alternative buyer/recapitalization path, not the tone of the letter. [1,3]

## Public discussion
The original filing is the strongest contemporaneous public argument. Loeb’s contacting potential competitors is reported by Loeb, not independent verification of formal bids. Authenticated message-board posts under “Mr. Pink,” VIC, COBF and contemporaneous forum rebuttals were not recovered; do not fabricate them. [1]
