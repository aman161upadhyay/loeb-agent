## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**November 2013:** Third Point participated around an offering in a US wireless operator with improved relative operating performance. Merger speculation was optionality, not the sole disclosed reason. Exact purchase price and stake size remain unknown. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“The offering represented a favorable relative valuation versus peers, enhanced by recently improved relative operating performance and an attractive EBITDA growth trajectory.” The letter also emphasized a “dynamic management team.” — January 21, 2014. [1]

## What Third Point did
Bought in November 2013 and treated T-Mobile as a core TMT investment in the January letter. A separately authored holdings summary accompanying the Q4 2015 letter reports a full sale; final execution day and residual instruments unknown. [1–2]

## What happened after
No verified realized return recovered. Keep this operating/valuation thesis distinct from the contingent Sprint/T-Mobile synergy scenario within the contemporaneous SoftBank thesis. [1–2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Momentum:** improved execution made the forward earnings bridge credible. [1]
- **Growth:** EBITDA expansion was explicit. [1]
- **Leadership:** management was part of underwriting. [1]
- **Price/optionality:** peer-relative valuation came before a hypothetical consolidation gain. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: capital-markets/event entry into an improving business, without a public board campaign. [1]

## Public discussion
The January letter situates this alongside Sony and SoftBank, but those are separate securities and catalysts. Authentic contemporary forum/X discussion remains missing. [1]
