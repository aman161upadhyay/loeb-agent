## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Cutoff: early March 2020.** The subsequent April letter is retrospective, not evidence Loeb foresaw the shutdown. Its account says the top 20 investments represented about 85% of NAV; aerospace, airline and auto exposures about 10% of exposure produced over one-third of the quarter’s losses. These are manager hindsight measurements. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words

April 16, **hindsight**: “We did not ascribe adequate probability to a full-blown global pandemic in our scenario analysis around COVID-19”. He explained that shutdown sensitivity and concentration amplified the loss; credit offered more asymmetric returns under extreme uncertainty. [1]

## What Third Point did

Reduced equity exposure by approximately 15% shortly after the selloff; deployed $2.2B into structured/corporate credit in mid-March, nearly doubling credit exposure. Q2 corporate credit was reduced from about 23% to 17% of Offshore NAV by harvesting late-Q1 purchases. Loeb resumed sole-CIO responsibility and added a quality/compounder lens rather than relying only on events. [1,2]

## What happened after

Offshore -16% Q1; Q2 +10.8%; YTD -3.7% through July 31, 2020. These are fund returns, not credit-trade P&L. [1,2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern

A loss case, not a fabricated perfectly hedged crisis win. The process correction was scenario breadth, liquidity and capital-structure flexibility; later quality investing followed a failure of the prior mix. [1,2]
