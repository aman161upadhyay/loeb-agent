## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Q3 2013 purchase:** Nokia had agreed to sell businesses to Microsoft. Third Point underwrote the remaining assets and prospective cash rather than a recovery of the old handset franchise. The letter's approximately €8 billion net cash was expected **at closing**, not necessarily cash already on the balance sheet at entry. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“At our purchase price, we seized an opportunity to create new Nokia at a substantial discount to target value.” It expected excess cash distributions and said: “Either a buyback or a special dividend is possible”. — Q3 letter quoted October 22. [1]

## What Third Point did
Initiated during Q3 and disclosed the post-disposal thesis in October. Exact stake, shareholder engagement and final exit were not recovered. [1]

## What happened after
The retrieved source establishes the thesis and forecast catalyst, not actual distributions received or realized return. Do not substitute Nokia's later network-industry history for this trade's result. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Corporate event:** removal of handset activities changed the business being valued. [1]
- **Cash:** pro forma balance sheet supported downside analysis. [1]
- **Return catalyst:** distributions could bring additional investors and close the gap. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a passive event-driven sum-of-parts opportunity; Third Point did not have to originate the asset sale. [1]

## Public discussion
Market Folly explicitly describes this as the type of event-driven play Third Point favored. No authenticated contemporary opposing forum/X discussion recovered. [1]
