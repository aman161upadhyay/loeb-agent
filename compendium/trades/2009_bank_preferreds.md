## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Q2 2009 capital-repair setting.** Government intervention created preferred/common exchange opportunities at stressed banks. BAC was entered through preferred securities averaging 57 cents on the dollar, then exchanged into common in mid-May. The company’s $11.66 tangible book at June 30 and Loeb’s $3 normalized EPS model were later retention-date information, not necessarily public at first purchase. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“We initiated this position in BAC Preferred” — July 31, 2009. [1]

“the usurious rates charged to borrow the stock” — same letter, explaining why the Citigroup preferred/equity exchange disappointed. [1]

## What Third Point did
Bought discounted BAC preferred, exchanged for common worth $10, then retained common because of a separate normalized-earnings/revaluation thesis. This was not simply a perpetual preferred coupon trade. Loeb also attempted the Citigroup exchange and reported disappointing economics because stock-borrow cost consumed the apparent arbitrage attraction. [1]

## What happened after
BAC common had appreciated over 40% since the exchange at the July letter date. Loeb viewed 4.3x normalized $3/share earnings and a possible 1.5x tangible-book valuation as attractive; the nearly 50% further upside was his forecast. BAC total realized P&L and Citigroup dollar loss are unknown. Class win describes disclosed interim BAC appreciation, not the entire combined strategy. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Buy discounted capital claims:** Preferred entry at 57 cents preceded conversion to common. [1]
- **Exchange catalyst:** Capital restructuring supplied a timed transition rather than waiting for an indefinite repricing. [1]
- **Normalized earnings exceed depressed valuation:** The retained common thesis used a $3 EPS scenario. [1]
- **Implementation costs can defeat spread:** Citigroup borrow costs make nominal exchange economics insufficient. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: capital-structure flexibility plus explicit willingness to hold common after a technical catalyst. Citigroup is the necessary negative execution counterexample: correct direction does not guarantee net arbitrage profit. [1]

## Public discussion
Loeb’s original letter discloses both BAC success and Citigroup disappointment. No verified external thread or borrow-rate series was recovered; do not replace the documented cost problem with a generic bank-insolvency story. [1]
