## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**April 2005 campaign-date snapshot.** Integrated exploration/production and midstream gathering assets obscured value; Loeb estimated the midstream business at $1.6–2.2bn and favored separation or an MLP structure retaining GP economics. He compared $3.46/Mcfe exploration spending with $0.67 development costs and wanted about $100m projected 2005 midstream free cash flow used intelligently. These were investor estimates, not realized proceeds. [1]

**Source-date conflict:** the initial exhibit heading reads April 25, 2004, but refers to a March 22, 2005 HSR filing and 2005 guidance and is contained in a 2005 accession. This record uses 2005, explicitly preserving the discrepancy rather than manufacturing a 2004 initiation. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“Please do not misconstrue our recommendations as a condemnation of management or the Company’s Board of Directors.” — initial letter. [1]

“initiate a share repurchase program of $300--$500 million” — August 15, 2005 follow-up, after better permits and gas prices led Loeb to raise his valuation. [2]

## What Third Point did
Initial 5.9% ownership and HSR clearance allowed a potentially larger investment. Recommended after-tax asset separation/MLP or sale, curtailed costly exploration and redeployed cash into development or repurchases. August follow-up proposed using borrowing capacity to buy 10–15% of shares. February 2006 filing reported 3.6m shares, demonstrating that not all campaign shares were necessarily held through acquisition. [1–3]

## What happened after
Anadarko’s August 23, 2006 release confirms a completed $61 cash/share acquisition. This validates a realizable strategic value above the initial quotation but is **not** Third Point’s weighted-average return: purchases, trims and ownership at close require their own ledger. Later press said Third Point had reached 8.6%. [4,5]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Integrated-asset complexity discount:** Separate midstream and E&P valuations were the core explanation for mispricing. [1]
- **Tax-aware monetization:** MLP, spin or sale choices depended on leakage and retained GP economics. [1]
- **Improve drilling capital allocation:** Development and repurchases compared favorably with risky exploration. [1]
- **Update thesis with improving facts:** August permits, throughput and prices prompted a higher valuation and larger buyback proposal. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: constructive capital-allocation activism, not automatic CEO removal. The letter explicitly praises management while demanding a structural catalyst. Updating the valuation also demonstrates a thesis responsive to new operating facts. [1,2]

## Public discussion
NGI’s December 2006 Pogo coverage placed Western Gas in the activist’s track record. Its later percentage is secondary evidence; the SEC filings remain preferred for date-specific ownership. Forum discussion remains unverified. [3,5]
