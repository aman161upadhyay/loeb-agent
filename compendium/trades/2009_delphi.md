## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**June 2009 credit entry.** Third Point’s July 2009 letter lists Delphi within its auto credit opportunity set as production stabilized at depressed levels; the later Q2 2012 letter identifies the actual first instrument as a June 2009 DIP facility. The 2012 business-quality claims must not be relabeled as verbatim June 2009 due diligence. [1,2]

In July 2009 Loeb cited US SAAR around 9.5–10m since October 2008, retail roughly 8m, used-car prices up about 20% from December lows and cash-for-clunkers as support for stabilization. These industry observations supplemented bottom-up security selection, not a claim every bankrupt supplier would recover. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“each investment is generated through bottoms-up analysis” — July 31, 2009, on the auto book including Delphi. [1]

“a best-in-class supplier which still trades at the valuation of more commoditized and disadvantaged comparable companies” — July 31, 2012 letter quoted August 1. This is the later equity-retention thesis. [2]

## What Third Point did
June 2009 bought DIP financing; retained exposure through restructuring and later IPO, according to the 2012 letter. By 2012 the thesis emphasized premium businesses/customer geography, low need for further deleveraging, limited North American union labor and smaller pension liabilities than peers. Expected buybacks/dividend and shareholder-base diversification were new equity catalysts. [2]

## What happened after
The October 3, 2012 letter lists Delphi among the top five quarterly winners. The July $35–40 target and anticipated return of free cash flow equal to about 25% of market cap by end-2013 were forecasts, not proceeds already distributed. “Largest winner ever” and a precise total profit were not verified in recovered primary evidence; do not invent either. [2,3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Senior restructuring entry:** DIP financing, not speculative old common, was the entry instrument. [2]
- **Selective auto normalization:** The July 2009 letter described stabilization but explicitly stressed selectivity. [1]
- **Post-reorg quality discount:** Later equity value depended on superior business mix and reduced legacy liabilities. [2]
- **Capital return and ownership normalization:** 2012 retention catalysts differed from the original DIP catalyst. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: follow the security and thesis through time. A DIP-to-equity winner can become a differentiated operating-business holding, but the later quality story must not contaminate the original entry snapshot. [1–3]

## Public discussion
MarketFolly noted a hedge-fund-heavy ownership list and recent director sales; Loeb himself recognized concentrated ownership as a volatility/overhang risk. That is a useful contrary technical consideration, not proof of weak operations. No verified contemporary forum model was recovered. [2]
