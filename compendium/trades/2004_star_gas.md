## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Distressed entry after collapse, not an 80%-loss round trip.** The February 14, 2005 letter recalls the October 2004 distribution suspension and price collapse from $21.60 to $4.32. Loeb expressly said Third Point bought around the depressed prevailing levels and had already taken profits on about 500,000 units near $7. Exact first-fill date is unknown; the 2004 entry label is an approximate campaign chronology. [1]

Heating-oil operations had volumes down 15%, unit margins down 10%, fixed costs up 8%, and first-quarter EBITDA near zero versus $26m previously. Covenant/liquidity problems and propane-sale tax allocations created economic pain beyond the quoted unit price. Those are contemporaneous letter figures, not evidence that a change of CEO alone could fix commodity demand. [1]

## Loeb's thesis in his own words
“we purchased our stake around these levels and took profits on about 500,000 shares near the $7.00 per unit level.” — February 14, 2005. This direct statement is the crucial correction to the popular collapse narrative. [1]

The letter demanded operating accountability, liquidity clarity and leadership change; those are paraphrases of the full primary letter, not reconstructed quotations. [1]

## What Third Point did
Third Point accumulated distressed units, partially monetized a recovery, held 6.0% in February 2005 and requested a credible operating plan, better investor communication, independent oversight and Sevin’s departure. The personal attacks in the letter are not the underwriting thesis. [1]

March 7: company announced Irik Sevin’s resignation as CEO/chairman, Audrey Sevin’s departure from the board, Joseph Cavanaugh as CEO and Daniel Donovan as COO. Irik remained a director/consultant under the disclosed arrangements; this was not an unconditional severance of every tie. [2]

## What happened after
The named leadership catalyst occurred. No complete purchase ledger, distributions, tax allocations or final liquidation proceeds were recovered, so classify this as a **governance/catalyst win**, not a verified fund-return multiple. Loeb’s own statement establishes partial profit-taking but not total profit. [1,2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Recover value in already-distressed units:** Entry was after the collapse rather than before it. [1]
- **Fix operating execution:** Volume, margin and cost deterioration required a measurable turnaround. [1]
- **Replace conflicted leadership:** An independent operator and board oversight were specific remedies. [1]
- **Demand credible liquidity communication:** Covenant risks and tax/distribution consequences required clear reporting. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: famous rhetoric obscures a distressed entry, earlier profit-taking and a precise management catalyst. Train the timing distinction, not the insult as an investment signal. [1]

## Public discussion
GlobalCapital reproduced the letter on February 18, helping explain its public notoriety. The issuer’s March announcement is the necessary corrective to simplified claims that Loeb instantly fired Sevin and completely expelled him. No authenticated Mr. Pink-era board discussion was recovered. [2,3]
