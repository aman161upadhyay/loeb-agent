# Fifteen additional decisions: manual forecast of the frozen chain

## Protocol and boundaries

These are **manual chain walks, not new LLM outputs**. The selection is outside the 47 calibration decisions, ten controls and eleven holdouts, checked against their IDs and company/action windows. Existing Danaher 2020 ownership is not the summer-2015 initiation examined here. No 2025–26 holdout thesis is reused.

“Untuned” means absent from the scored decision sets, **not absent from the corpus or model pretraining**. The compendium, archival lessons and possibly AGENT may already teach these names. I read the dossiers including their outcomes; consequently this is an informed stress forecast, not a blinded historical prediction. Dates below are decision windows/disclosure checkpoints where exact execution is unknown. No new cutoff pack or unverified daily price has been manufactured. Later letter valuation models are identified as such.

G1 columns use **E**vent, **X** edge, **R** governance/process, **F**loor, **S**ize/liquidity and **M**anagement/process credibility; P/U/F mean PASS/UNSURE/FAIL. The deterministic veto is event FAIL, floor FAIL, edge and route both FAIL, or at least three FAILs. Missing evidence should be UNSURE under the written prompt, although the saved runs sometimes violate that rule.

For existing positions, the shipped staged runner **bypasses Gates 1–5** and calls prompt 08 directly. G1–G4 assessments in those cases are diagnostic counterfactuals; the actual predicted action comes from 08. Similarly, a gold exposure review could be routed to 09 if the evaluator chooses portfolio mode; that taxonomy must be fixed in advance.

The primary action forecasts imply approximately **eight substantive action agreements out of fifteen** if escalation is scored as campaign behavior and E.ON's disclosed ownership is treated as a purchase proxy. That is **not a valid strict 8/15 measured hit rate**: InterCept routing, Agribrands/Penn dual actions and missing original dates make several labels non-comparable. For a genuinely new, balanced and fully specified pack set, my preregisterable expectation would be **45–65% exact-action accuracy and 20–40% full mechanism matches**, with materially worse exits than new longs. Those are subjective ranges, not confidence intervals. I would expect perhaps four to six of these fifteen to reproduce most stated mechanisms. Do not combine this exercise with the existing scoreboard.

## 1. Agribrands: buy enough to make opposition public

**Source:** `compendium/trades/1998_agribrands.md`, September 8, 2000 SEC letter and September 15 13D. Although the filename says 1998, the evaluated decision is the **September 2000 intervention**, not the original spin-off purchase.

Pre-decision economic facts described by the letter: approximately $41 stock, $160m cash, $90m annual EBITDA, $14/share cash net of debt and pensions; related-party Ralcorp deal offered $39 cash for up to 20% of shares. These are contemporary activist calculations, not independently verified pack inputs.

- **G1 E/X/R/F/S/M = P/P/P/P/P/U.** E1 conflicted control transaction; edge is an auction/recap alternative; deliberate crossing of 5% creates a public route; cash and earnings offer support; buildability demonstrated; controller response uncertain.
- **Discipline:** not a melting business or debt-funded roll-up. Another activist is not the central mechanism. Cash must not be counted twice in EV.
- **G2:** conflicted process and undervaluation of surplus cash; a generic “cheap agricultural stock” rationale would miss the mechanism.
- **G3:** letter's 2.9x EV/EBITDA and $14 net cash support investigation, but $14 versus $41 is a roughly 66% cash-only drawdown, not a tight floor. **Do not use the later $54.50 Cargill offer as a pre-decision target.**
- **G4:** P2 public opposition, with auction, tender/recap or dividend asks; not P0 complacent merger arbitrage.
- **Prediction:** existing mode ESCALATE, high confidence in campaign direction; no justifiable NAV size from issuer ownership. Actual action included an ADD, so one scalar action loses information.

## 2. BindView: a direct counterexample to “lead or stay out”

**Source:** `compendium/trades/2001_bindview.md`, March 22, 2001 SEC letter.

At $2.25, approximately $120m equity value and $60m cash with virtually no debt, the letter calculates ~$60m EV versus $103m low-end sales guidance. Products and technical staff remained valuable despite cash burn, sales-execution and accounting-control complaints. Chapman was a collaborator, not an economic disqualifier.

- **G1 E/X/R/F/S/M = P/F/P/U/P/U.** E1 sale; early-era edge would be PASS, but the universal “same asks” collaboration rule overwrites it to FAIL. Route through allied engagement survives; product value/cash less burn is not a complete appraisal.
- **Discipline:** someone-else's-fight applies literally; accounting issues require diligence but do not prove zero IP value. No positive evidence of structurally impossible recovery.
- **G2:** likely NO_EDGE because another activist already advances the sale; this can produce PASS without a G1 hard fail.
- **G3:** Loeb's proposed $5–7 at 2–3x sales implies 122–211% upside from $2.25. Cash alone ~$1.125/share implies 50% downside before burn: apparent 2.4–4.2x upside/downside, **conditional on a strategic revenue valuation**, not an independently earned target.
- **G4:** P2 collaboration/sale pressure rather than exclusive campaign leadership.
- **Prediction:** PASS, medium confidence; alternative BUY if early-era exception wins. Actual accumulation exceeds two million shares. This is a clean economic reason to remove a universal collaboration veto, not add a BindView exception.

## 3. Penn Virginia: persistent incentive problem

**Source:** `compendium/trades/2002_penn_virginia.md`, February 2002 and March 11, 2003 letters. The evaluated action is the **March 2003 follow-up**.

Prior thesis: ~$175m listed PVR units within ~$300m parent equity value, leaving ~$125m for 250 Bcfe reserves before GP rights; buyback-reserve cost ~$0.50/Mcfe versus stated drilling cost $1.22–1.56. By March 2003 ownership reached 880,000 shares, nearly 10%, and insider selling aggravated incentive concerns.

- **G1 P/P/P/U/P/F** diagnostically: real separable assets, quantified cause and electable governance; stale February 2002 valuation is not a current floor; management's behavior is adverse but replacement/pressure remains possible.
- **Discipline:** oil/gas cyclicality is not secular obsolescence; BP Capital's prior proposal would make literal “someone else's fight” dangerous in new mode.
- **G2:** destructive acquisitions, capital allocation and options-versus-stock incentives, not commodity forecasting alone.
- **G3:** no March 2003 quote/updated debt bridge; cannot turn the earlier $50 proposed value into a current IRR.
- **G4:** P2 public incentive and capital-allocation demands.
- **Prediction:** prompt 08 ESCALATE rather than HOLD, consistent with the follow-up. It may miss that increased ownership and public action coexist.

## 4. InterCept: supporting JANA is the mechanism

**Source:** `compendium/trades/2004_intercept.md`, May 27 letter; June 14 issuer settlement is subsequent and excluded from the hypothetical decision.

Technology-to-banks business, ~$104m loss on unrelated iBill/EPX investments according to Loeb, proposed management buyout conflicts, preferred-holder conversion repricing, and JANA's competing slate. Third Point's $22m stake was below 2% of stated firm assets, not 7.1% NAV.

- **G1 P/F/P/U/P/U:** E1 sale; another activist triggers the literal edge veto; JANA supplies a concrete route; no current valuation bridge.
- **Discipline:** collaboration veto is directly contradicted by Loeb's statement: “Third Point will support Jana's efforts to control the Company's Board and effect a sale.”
- **G2:** genuine edge is security-holder conflict analysis and independent auction, but likely NO_EDGE under the shipped negative rule.
- **G3:** cannot estimate skew from stake market value; preferred rights must be included before common proceeds.
- **G4:** supportive P2/P4 alliance, not Third Point personal seats.
- **Prediction:** new-mode PASS, existing-mode ESCALATE. **Not an exact-action test until routing is fixed.** The actual public intervention is evidence against the categorical rule regardless of ultimate investment outcome.

## 5. Nabi: monetize before cash is consumed

**Source:** `compendium/trades/2006_nabi.md`, April 27 letter; later issuer rebuttal and November settlement are excluded from initial evidence.

Product/development/biologics assets and approximately $25m first-quarter cash burn. Existing stake 8.4% in the first filing; exact entry price unknown. Loeb wanted assets monetized, not proceeds recycled into the failed strategy.

- **G1 P/P/P/U/P/U:** named E1 fix, ownership capable of obtaining a hearing, asset value unmeasured, management disputed but replaceable.
- **Discipline:** a failed R&D program does not establish that separable assets are melting. Whole-company-sale exception requires credible buyers; the dossier does not supply authenticated bids.
- **G2:** asset value endangered by reinvestment and poor incentives; fresh independent adviser/process.
- **G3:** **unquantified** without cash, liabilities, realizable proceeds and burn horizon. This is the case where the observed chain may manufacture a target to complete the template.
- **G4:** P2 sale/strategic review; eventual P3 seats not assumed.
- **Prediction:** low-conviction BUY, 1–2% illustrative research-size range only, not a recovered fund allocation; WATCH is equally defensible under strict valuation discipline. The predicted buy is low confidence precisely because the chain alternates between permissive early activism and rigid floor tests.

## 6. Huntsman: a real holding, not a recoverable pre-entry experiment

**Source:** `compendium/trades/2008_huntsman_merger_arb.md`, July 25, 2008 letter.

The source verifies a Q2 arbitrage holding and loss, not entry date, spread, merger agreement, funding commitment, borrow or hedges. The eventual -51.4% stock move is **not** an input or fund return.

- **G1 P/U/U/U/U/U:** E2 signed-deal situation as described retrospectively; no recovered execution model.
- **Discipline:** financing stress and buyer incentives require deal-specific underwriting, not a blanket leveraged-company rejection.
- **G2:** possible completion-risk premium; unknown whether spread compensates for buyer optionality.
- **G3:** cannot compute annualized spread or break-price loss from this dossier. Required equation is completion probability × consideration plus failure probability × break value, less hedge/funding costs.
- **G4:** P0 monitor contract/financing; no governance control.
- **Prediction:** WATCH/PASS on a faithfully sparse pack, low confidence. Actual ownership verifies the opportunity class but **does not provide a valid exact-date key**. A confident BUY forecast would just import the later fact of ownership.

## 7. Bank of America preferred exchange

**Source:** `compendium/trades/2009_bank_preferreds.md`, July 31 letter describing May execution.

Purchased preferred at average 57 cents, exchanged into common valued at $10 in mid-May. The $11.66 June tangible book and $3 normalized EPS scenario belong to the later retention thesis and must not be backdated.

- **G1 P/P/P/U/U/P:** E2/E3 exchange and capital-repair process; expertise in the claim/terms; full recovery/position capacity not documented.
- **Discipline:** deposit or liability uncertainty is not eliminated by government involvement, but applying the equity veto indiscriminately would reject the actual preferred route.
- **G2:** forced capital restructuring / discounted claim exchange, distinct from generic bank recovery.
- **G3:** 57 cents is an entry price, **not** proof of 75% arbitrage upside to par; exchange ratio, common hedge and borrow determine realized economics.
- **G4:** P5 official/capital process, then P0 retained common.
- **Prediction:** BUY preferred, medium conviction, no defensible exact NAV size. It should identify the transition of instruments. The Citigroup contrast in the same source shows why “direction right” with missing borrow expense can still lose money.

## 8. CIT: create equity through the right senior claim

**Source:** `compendium/trades/2009_cit.md`, reproduction of Q4 2009 letter dated March 8, 2010.

November 1 prearranged bankruptcy creates a senior-unsecured purchase opportunity. Investor-estimated price was >25% below orderly wind-down asset value; debt bought creates equity at about 0.4x plan book. December confirmation and 0.7x trading are excluded as outcomes.

- **G1 P/P/P/P/U/P:** E3 claim coverage, prearranged process, asset modeling; size/liquidity unknown but not equity-volume FAIL.
- **Discipline:** old common and purchased senior bonds are different claims. A blanket bank-liability veto would be wrong; the asset-recovery estimate itself still needs stress.
- **G2:** forced-sale/capital-structure complexity.
- **G3:** if price is 0.75 of credible wind-down value, recovery to that value means ~33% upside, not a 25% investment return. Additional downside is unknown without asset haircuts. A model that uses eventual 0.7/0.4 appreciation has leaked.
- **G4:** P5 court/creditor process.
- **Prediction:** BUY bonds, medium conviction. The 77% equity allocation belongs to the creditor class, not Third Point; the runner must not turn it into position size.

## What these cases predict about generalization

The chain should work best when a dated event, independently valuable claim and identifiable process are all explicit: CIT and post-announcement separations. It is vulnerable where the edge is collaboration, persistent high-quality allocation, timing of capital release, or replacement of a successful position with something better. More case-name exceptions would increase apparent calibration fidelity while leaving those structural ambiguities unresolved. The correct next experiment is to preregister eligible action windows and neutral packs for these mechanisms, not score this hindsight-informed walkthrough as an additional backtest.
