## Recovered evidence

Second-quarter revenue was $756.0 million against $738.4 million a year earlier. Gross billings were $1.49 billion, net loss $51.7 million and adjusted EBITDA $34.0 million. Revenue is therefore not equivalent to billings, and EBITDA is not net income or distributable cash. The financial disclosure added nearly 1.1 million customers; customer additions need to be evaluated against acquisition cost and repeat behavior. [1]


## Underwriting analysis — conditional, not management guidance

Build a bridge from the reported period to the security’s cash claim. Separate revenue recognition, gross profit, recurring operating expense, working capital, maintenance capital expenditure, interest and taxes. The facts above are the recovered figures; absence of a line in this packet does not imply its economic amount is zero. Pay special attention to the denominator: quarterly figures, annual results, operating profit, net income and adjusted earnings are not interchangeable. A percentage should retain the period and accounting definition attached to its source.

For Groupon, the primary operating checks are North American Local billings, repeat cohorts and contribution after marketing. The potential asset support is merchant/customer network and available cash less operating liabilities, without an assumed sale. Neither phrase substitutes for a cash-flow or recovery calculation. Debt at a subsidiary, restricted cash, customer advances and collateral pledges can prevent apparently available assets from reaching the listed parent. Likewise, a company’s adjusted metric may exclude an expense that still requires cash or compensates employees through dilution. Where the necessary reconciliation has not been recovered, no artificial precision is introduced.

The financial test of a local-commerce turnaround under Rich Williams is incremental cash earned after all incremental investment, not merely an improvement in one reported ratio. Compare the same perimeter across periods; acquired and disposed businesses, changes in share count and accounting presentation can otherwise create spurious growth. Treat management forecasts as contingent statements, not balance-sheet assets. Before assigning a target size, stress a weaker operating period, higher financing cost and slower catalyst timing separately. These are analyst sensitivities rather than additional company disclosures.
