The public common offering priced 423,372,629 shares at $9.50. A separate offering covered 14,545,455 equity units at $100 each. Estimated proceeds before expenses were approximately $3.968 billion and $1.186 billion, respectively. The company funded the Fire Victim Trust with $5.4 billion cash and shares representing 22.19% ownership; additional cash obligations follow the plan. Replacing expensive debt is expected to save customers about $250 million annually, not automatically create that amount in shareholder earnings. The new share denominator and trust overhang are central to any per-share valuation. [S1–S2]

Accounting treatment. The figures above retain their original period, currency and definition. Reported and adjusted earnings are not interchangeable, and segment profit is not automatically consolidated distributable cash. Reconcile recurring operating performance with financing costs, taxes, exceptional charges, working capital and necessary reinvestment. An acquisition, divestiture or change in reporting perimeter can change growth and share counts without representing organic improvement. Do not sum overlapping segment or geographic disclosures.

Financial underwriting should use a bridge, not a headline ratio. Start from recurring revenue and operating profit, charge maintenance investment and financing obligations, and test cash conversion under weaker demand or higher costs. A non-cash charge may not consume cash this quarter but can reveal that prior capital allocation failed. A working-capital release may be real cash without being repeatable earnings. Debt maturities, restricted cash, leases and off-balance-sheet obligations require separate modelling beyond the annual balances. D04 supplies an explicitly approximate post-plan diluted denominator; legacy annual shares must not be used as if the capital structure were unchanged.

Analyst cross-check: For any apparent earnings improvement, test three reconciliations: operating income to operating cash flow, operating cash flow to free cash flow after capital spending, and enterprise earnings to the actual security's claim after interest, preferred distributions and dilution. Missing inputs prevent a precise return calculation.
