Title: Star Gas Partners L.P. — Financial record and accounting bridge
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1002590/000115752305001259/a4818531ex991.txt; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2005-02-13

The December-quarter release reports sales of $350.694 million versus $316.070 million. Continuing operations lost $74.648 million; a $153.644 million gain on the propane disposal produced consolidated net income of $74.444 million. The disposal gain is not recurring earnings. Delivery and branch costs rose to $65.480 million from $60.678 million, while product costs reached $222.903 million. [1]

## Economic cross-check

Retail heating-oil volume fell 14.9%, to 142.3 million gallons from 167.2 million. Management attributes 10.5 million gallons of the decline to net customer attrition and 6.0 million to conservation. Annual attrition is approximately 6.5% versus 1.3% in fiscal 2003. Wholesale oil rose 65% to $1.42 per gallon, hurting both margins and retention. Management acknowledges service-centralization problems. [1]

The $475 million propane sale does not represent cash freely available to common units: $311 million financed debt repayment, premiums, expenses and interest. A new $260 million secured revolver supports working capital subject to borrowing-base constraints. The 10.25% notes have an asset-sale reinvestment/debt-reduction covenant. Exact last-close price, current fully diluted capitalization and normalized enterprise-value multiple are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered here. [1]

## Underwriting interpretation

Accounting treatment for the desk: keep reported results, adjusted measures, cash movement and forecasts in separate columns. A disposal gain, impairment or reserve adjustment can materially change net income without changing recurring cash generation by the same amount. Consolidated earnings are not automatically distributable to the security under consideration. Check debt, taxes, pensions, minority interests and restrictions before describing an asset as surplus. A stale balance-sheet figure is not a verified current cash balance.

The critical bridge is from operating activity to cash available after maintenance investment and senior obligations. A peer margin or normalized-profit scenario is an assumption until an executor, cost base and implementation timetable support it. A fund's ownership percentage of an issuer is not its percentage of NAV. Missing fully diluted shares, current debt or cash prevents a precise current enterprise value; the pack does not fill those gaps using a later filing. Ratios should retain the period and accounting scope of their numerator and denominator.
