Title: Star Gas Partners L.P. — Latest operating and disclosure update
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1002590/000115752305001259/a4818531ex991.txt; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2005-02-13

Retail heating-oil volume fell 14.9%, to 142.3 million gallons from 167.2 million. Management attributes 10.5 million gallons of the decline to net customer attrition and 6.0 million to conservation. Annual attrition is approximately 6.5% versus 1.3% in fiscal 2003. Wholesale oil rose 65% to $1.42 per gallon, hurting both margins and retention. Management acknowledges service-centralization problems. [1]

## Economic cross-check

The December-quarter release reports sales of $350.694 million versus $316.070 million. Continuing operations lost $74.648 million; a $153.644 million gain on the propane disposal produced consolidated net income of $74.444 million. The disposal gain is not recurring earnings. Delivery and branch costs rose to $65.480 million from $60.678 million, while product costs reached $222.903 million. [1]

The common distribution was suspended in October. The propane disposal completed in December. The February 9 release is the current operating update; it acknowledges that centralizing service and dispatch damaged customer relationships. There is no completed leadership remedy in the available issuer release. The immediate calendar is the peak January–March heating season and the asset-sale covenant's 360-day use-of-proceeds period. [1]

## Underwriting interpretation

Reading the update: changes in reported earnings must be attributed among volumes, prices, mix, costs, accounting and changes in the asset perimeter. A favorable headline does not establish an inflection, and an adverse headline does not prove every asset has become worthless. Management guidance is a statement of expectation, not an achieved result. The important evidence is the part of the operating bridge that can be checked at the next reporting date.

For a position depending on an event, compare operational progress with the legal or governance process rather than treating the two as interchangeable. A board review, adviser appointment, hearing or strategy announcement is not a completed transaction. Delay can consume cash or reduce expected return even when a long-term value argument survives. The absence of a recovered current metric must remain explicit: no estimate here is quietly reconstructed from a future report. The agent can reason conditionally on the available information but should not claim a fully measured operating trend when comparable data are missing.
