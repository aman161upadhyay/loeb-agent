Title: Star Gas Partners L.P. — Peer and industry comparability

Date: 2005-02-13

The relevant benchmark is heating-oil distribution, not the price multiple of a diversified propane partnership. Gross profit per gallon, accounts retained, route costs and service losses distinguish a viable franchise from merely higher nominal sales. Weather and customer mix influence comparisons; residential customers have different economics from commercial accounts. No independently verified contemporaneous peer enterprise-value table was recovered. [1]

## Economic cross-check

The partnership distributes home heating oil and provides installation and service. Approximately 30% of annual oil volume is normally delivered in October–December and 45% in January–March. Dispatch, telephone handling, route density and customer retention matter independently of wholesale oil prices. The propane operation has been sold, leaving a more concentrated heating-oil business. [1]

The issuer itself warns that high customer losses can persist through the winter and beyond. A smaller customer base damages future earnings even if attrition stops. Price-sensitive households can conserve fuel or leave; fixed delivery costs cannot necessarily shrink at the same rate. Service indicators have improved, but management offers no assurance about timing or efficacy. Secured creditors can consume the apparent asset cushion before common unitholders receive cash. [1]

## Underwriting interpretation

Comparison discipline: align the unit being valued before applying a peer benchmark. Revenue recognition, geographic exposure, product mix, capital intensity, legal seniority and ownership of assets can all make a superficially similar multiple inappropriate. Use a segment or claim-level comparison when the group combines distinct economics. Higher margins elsewhere may identify a question worth asking; they do not prove the entire difference can be eliminated.

A credible normalization case identifies which costs change, what investment is required and whether the company has the ability to sustain the proposed earnings. Cyclical peaks should not be capitalized as perpetual growth. Equally, depressed earnings can understate an asset's potential if a specific operating or legal reset is under way. The benchmark should describe the same accounting measure and period, not a convenient headline from another date. Missing numerical comparables remain missing rather than being filled from a later investor presentation. Competitive response and transaction costs belong in the comparison.
