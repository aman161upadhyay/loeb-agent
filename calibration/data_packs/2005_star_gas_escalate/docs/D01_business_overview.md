Title: Star Gas Partners L.P. — Business, assets and economic claim
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1002590/000115752305001259/a4818531ex991.txt; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2005-02-13

The partnership distributes home heating oil and provides installation and service. Approximately 30% of annual oil volume is normally delivered in October–December and 45% in January–March. Dispatch, telephone handling, route density and customer retention matter independently of wholesale oil prices. The propane operation has been sold, leaving a more concentrated heating-oil business. [1]

## Economic cross-check

The December-quarter release reports sales of $350.694 million versus $316.070 million. Continuing operations lost $74.648 million; a $153.644 million gain on the propane disposal produced consolidated net income of $74.444 million. The disposal gain is not recurring earnings. Delivery and branch costs rose to $65.480 million from $60.678 million, while product costs reached $222.903 million. [1]

The relevant benchmark is heating-oil distribution, not the price multiple of a diversified propane partnership. Gross profit per gallon, accounts retained, route costs and service losses distinguish a viable franchise from merely higher nominal sales. Weather and customer mix influence comparisons; residential customers have different economics from commercial accounts. No independently verified contemporaneous peer enterprise-value table was recovered. [1]

## Underwriting interpretation

Analyst distinctions, not additional reported facts: identify which assets actually generate cash, which require reinvestment, and which can be transferred independently. Contractual ownership, economic exposure and voting control need not coincide. A recognizable product or attractive asset is not sufficient evidence of attractive common-equity economics. Separate a going-concern franchise from its financing before assigning a floor. Consider what remains if the proposed event does not occur: recurring cash, realizable assets, enforceable priority or only optionality. These distinctions prevent the business description from silently turning into a recommendation.

The observable tests are customer or counterparty retention, unit profitability, required maintenance investment and the ability to fund obligations without diluting the claim being evaluated. Where consolidated reporting combines unlike activities, a segment bridge is needed before comparing margins. Missing segment or legal information is not equivalent to a zero liability. No assumption about a future transaction, governance concession or recovery is embedded in this description.
