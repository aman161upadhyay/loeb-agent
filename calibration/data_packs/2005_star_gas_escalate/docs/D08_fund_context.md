Title: Star Gas Partners L.P. — Fund mandate, prior state and constraints
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1002590/000115752305001259/a4818531ex991.txt; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2005-02-13

Desk mandate: an existing distressed common-unit position, approximately 6% of the common units; the original case was recovery after the distribution suspension, not ownership purchased at the old peak. The holding size is an internal position-state input. Current firm cash, gross/net exposure and exact AUM are not available in the recovered pre-cutoff documents. Earlier attempts at management dialogue are part of the existing relationship, not evidence of an agreed remedy.

## Economic cross-check

The partnership distributes home heating oil and provides installation and service. Approximately 30% of annual oil volume is normally delivered in October–December and 45% in January–March. Dispatch, telephone handling, route density and customer retention matter independently of wholesale oil prices. The propane operation has been sold, leaving a more concentrated heating-oil business. [1]

The common distribution was suspended in October. The propane disposal completed in December. The February 9 release is the current operating update; it acknowledges that centralizing service and dispatch damaged customer relationships. There is no completed leadership remedy in the available issuer release. The immediate calendar is the peak January–March heating season and the asset-sale covenant's 360-day use-of-proceeds period. [1]

## Underwriting interpretation

Portfolio accounting for this desk: use current market exposure, not the issuer ownership percentage, to calculate capital at risk. Gross and net can conceal concentrated sector, currency, liquidity or event dependencies. A credit position and an equity position may respond to the same business shock despite occupying different strategy buckets. Derivative economic exposure, voting shares, collateral and counterparty obligations should be tracked separately. An absent exposure report is not evidence that no such risk exists.

The available record does not justify inventing current cash, top holdings or risk limits. Size must remain conditional where those inputs are missing. For an existing holding, separate the original thesis from new facts and sunk cost; for a fresh idea, do not assume ownership merely because the company has appeared in historical commentary. The fund's ability to wait for value depends on portfolio liquidity and obligations as well as the company's assets. No eventual trade, public campaign choice or realized return is encoded in the mandate above.
