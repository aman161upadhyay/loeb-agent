The $475 million propane sale does not represent cash freely available to common units: $311 million financed debt repayment, premiums, expenses and interest. A new $260 million secured revolver supports working capital subject to borrowing-base constraints. The 10.25% notes have an asset-sale reinvestment/debt-reduction covenant. Exact last-close price, current fully diluted capitalization and normalized enterprise-value multiple are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered here. [1]

## Economic cross-check

The December-quarter release reports sales of $350.694 million versus $316.070 million. Continuing operations lost $74.648 million; a $153.644 million gain on the propane disposal produced consolidated net income of $74.444 million. The disposal gain is not recurring earnings. Delivery and branch costs rose to $65.480 million from $60.678 million, while product costs reached $222.903 million. [1]

The issuer itself warns that high customer losses can persist through the winter and beyond. A smaller customer base damages future earnings even if attrition stops. Price-sensitive households can conserve fuel or leave; fixed delivery costs cannot necessarily shrink at the same rate. Service indicators have improved, but management offers no assurance about timing or efficacy. Secured creditors can consume the apparent asset cushion before common unitholders receive cash. [1]

## Underwriting interpretation

Valuation discipline for this desk: purchase price, scenario value, realizable proceeds and total investment profit are four different quantities. A prospective return needs both an entry price and an achievable exit/recovery value, including taxes, fees, dilution and time. When an input is absent, an exact multiple, upside percentage or annualized return is not justified. A low nominal price is not itself a margin of safety; the claim's priority and the business's remaining cash requirements determine the floor.

The base case should not depend on perfect execution and the adverse case should not assume a favorable transaction. Component values need a consistent date, accounting scope and treatment of debt. Avoid double-counting cost savings in both higher earnings and a separate asset value. Similarly, excess cash cannot be added without checking access and associated obligations. A peer multiple is a reference, not a contractual takeout price. No later market quotation or realized outcome is substituted for a missing cutoff input.
