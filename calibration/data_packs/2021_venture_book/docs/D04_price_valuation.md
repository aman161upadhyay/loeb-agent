The IPO bridge is not a January balance sheet: it excludes intervening cash flows and option exercises. It includes loan-funding borrowings and must not be mistaken for a clean unlevered software-company EV. The very high earnings multiple reflects a small, volatile positive trailing denominator, not a normalized forecast.

The January 29 last trading close is $62.19, versus the $20 IPO price; this is a market observation, not the fund's weighted cost. The public price is about 3.1 times the IPO price, calculated, before any dividends or transaction costs. That price change alone does not establish overvaluation or justify increasing exposure. A valuation should estimate fee revenue, sustainable approval/conversion, funding availability, stock compensation and credit-cycle resilience. Complete contemporary forward consensus and fully diluted valuation were not independently recovered. No later billion-dollar stake valuation is used as a point-in-time input. Valuation construction. All calculated multiples must match numerator, denominator, currency and share basis. A historical close is an observation rather than an entry cost. A guidance multiple is not consensus, a trailing ratio is not a forward ratio, and an activist target is not independently verified intrinsic value. No exact target is manufactured when a necessary price or earnings input is missing.

Build a base case without the most ambitious event, then a separately costed event case. Sum-of-parts values require corporate costs, debt, minority claims, taxes and transaction friction; earnings-power values require necessary reinvestment and a credible margin bridge. Cash should be counted only if it can actually be distributed. A repayment, buyback or divestiture must not be counted again after its effect is already reflected in shares, interest or earnings. Test sensitivity to both the operating outcome and the exit multiple. A margin of safety cannot be inferred from a low headline P/E or a high-quality brand alone. Exact upside/downside skew and IRR are not asserted without recoverable valuation endpoints and a realistic timetable.

Analyst cross-check: A useful sensitivity table varies the earnings base and the exit multiple independently, then subtracts financing and implementation costs. Do not assign the same probability to upside, base and stress cases merely because three columns appear in a spreadsheet. Uncertain inputs warrant ranges, not spurious precision.
