Title: Yahoo! Inc. — Fund mandate, prior state and constraints
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1011006/000115752313003338/a50670602-ex991.htm; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2013-07-22

Existing owner state: Third Point participates in governance through directors and holds a substantial common-stock position. The original Asian-asset/core-stewardship thesis has evolved after board and leadership changes. The desk can consider a partial block transaction without assuming a full exit. Insider restrictions, confidentiality and board-related obligations matter. Current fund AUM, cash, exact NAV weight and complete lot-level basis are not recovered.

## Economic cross-check

Yahoo combines a mature internet advertising business with Asian affiliate value. The existing investment began with a large gap between realizable parts and the core implied valuation, plus a governance intervention. The current business question is whether new products and mobile engagement can translate into sustainable core growth rather than whether the old leadership still controls the company. Asian assets remain a distinct source of upside. [1; desk history]

The original governance campaign has produced board access and a different CEO. The decision desk now has issuer block-liquidity terms and a board-service transition to consider. The transaction remains subject to completion and related legal conditions; neither cash receipt nor a future effective resignation is treated as already completed. Quarterly results were released July 16. No subsequent closing-date confirmation appears in the evidence. [1; internal deal terms]

## Underwriting interpretation

Portfolio accounting for this desk: use current market exposure, not the issuer ownership percentage, to calculate capital at risk. Gross and net can conceal concentrated sector, currency, liquidity or event dependencies. A credit position and an equity position may respond to the same business shock despite occupying different strategy buckets. Derivative economic exposure, voting shares, collateral and counterparty obligations should be tracked separately. An absent exposure report is not evidence that no such risk exists.

The available record does not justify inventing current cash, top holdings or risk limits. Size must remain conditional where those inputs are missing. For an existing holding, separate the original thesis from new facts and sunk cost; for a fresh idea, do not assume ownership merely because the company has appeared in historical commentary. The fund's ability to wait for value depends on portfolio liquidity and obligations as well as the company's assets. No eventual trade, public campaign choice or realized return is encoded in the mandate above.
