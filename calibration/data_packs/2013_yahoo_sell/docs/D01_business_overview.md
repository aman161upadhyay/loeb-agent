Title: Yahoo! Inc. — Business, assets and economic claim
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1011006/000115752313003338/a50670602-ex991.htm; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2013-07-22

Yahoo combines a mature internet advertising business with Asian affiliate value. The existing investment began with a large gap between realizable parts and the core implied valuation, plus a governance intervention. The current business question is whether new products and mobile engagement can translate into sustainable core growth rather than whether the old leadership still controls the company. Asian assets remain a distinct source of upside. [1; desk history]

## Economic cross-check

Second-quarter GAAP revenue was $1.135bn, down 7%; revenue ex-TAC was $1.071bn, down 1%. Adjusted EBITDA was $369m, down 7%. GAAP operating income rose to $137m from $55m, but the prior period included $129m restructuring costs; non-GAAP operating income fell 13% to $209m. Diluted GAAP EPS rose to $0.30 and non-GAAP EPS to $0.35. [1]

A core internet earnings comparison should use revenue ex-TAC, normalized operating earnings and the appropriate growth rate. Asian e-commerce optionality is not directly comparable with mature domestic display advertising. The buyer of the equity still owns both exposures unless separately hedged. No contemporaneous independent peer-multiple table or after-tax affiliate mark was recovered, so the pack does not claim a precise current conglomerate discount. [1; analyst framework]

## Underwriting interpretation

Analyst distinctions, not additional reported facts: identify which assets actually generate cash, which require reinvestment, and which can be transferred independently. Contractual ownership, economic exposure and voting control need not coincide. A recognizable product or attractive asset is not sufficient evidence of attractive common-equity economics. Separate a going-concern franchise from its financing before assigning a floor. Consider what remains if the proposed event does not occur: recurring cash, realizable assets, enforceable priority or only optionality. These distinctions prevent the business description from silently turning into a recommendation.

The observable tests are customer or counterparty retention, unit profitability, required maintenance investment and the ability to fund obligations without diluting the claim being evaluated. Where consolidated reporting combines unlike activities, a segment bridge is needed before comparing margins. Missing segment or legal information is not equivalent to a zero liability. No assumption about a future transaction, governance concession or recovery is embedded in this description.
