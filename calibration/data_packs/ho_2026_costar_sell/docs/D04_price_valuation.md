## Recovered evidence

The Q3 13F, disclosed November 14, reports 3.017 million common shares worth $254.544 million. That implies approximately $84.37 at September 30, not December 31. The cutoff price, guidance multiple and latest-published-quarter capital bridge are recovered above; unpublished year-end balances are not assumed known. A valuation must recognize the commercial cash franchise and deduct residential spending, not capitalize core earnings as if the board could automatically stop all losses. [1]


## Underwriting analysis — conditional, not management guidance

No valuation input should silently move between dates. A historical mark inferred by dividing a filing’s market value by shares is not an execution price; a repurchase average is not the cutoff close; an announced exchange ratio is not a fixed cash consideration. Options’ reported underlying value is not cash invested. When a cutoff close or current estimate is missing, a precise entry multiple, target and prospective return cannot be claimed. Arithmetic may be performed on recovered figures only with the original period and units preserved.

For CoStar Group, build the valuation around assessment of the board-settlement remedy against residential investment and core growth. Potential asset support comes from commercial recurring cash flows net of continuing residential spending and group liabilities. Use separate base, upside and downside scenarios, deduct senior claims and incorporate the cash required while waiting for the event. Do not count the same operating earnings in both a capitalized segment and a separately valued asset. A higher peer multiple is justified only if differences in durability, capital intensity and growth survive the comparison.

The key monitoring measures are net new bookings, segment cash earnings and spending accountability. A scenario in which they improve is not the same as a market forecast or a manager’s stated target. Required return must compensate for execution time, interim mark-to-market loss, financing and the chance that the event does not occur. No unsupported probability weights are supplied. Where the data cannot establish a two-sided numerical skew, describe the unresolved inputs rather than manufacture an attractive asymmetry. A missing valuation is a limitation on conviction, not evidence of either overvaluation or undervaluation.
