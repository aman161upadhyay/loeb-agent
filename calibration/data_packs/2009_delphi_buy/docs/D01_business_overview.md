Title: Delphi Corporation — Business, assets and economic claim

Date: 2009-06-01

Delphi supplies automotive components and systems and is operating in Chapter 11. Its manufacturing footprint, customer programs and engineering relationships can have going-concern value despite an impaired old capital structure. GM is a critical customer and restructuring counterparty. Old common stock, prepetition debt and debtor-in-possession claims are different securities; the existence of a valuable industrial business does not establish a recovery for every layer. [1,2]

## Economic cross-check

The first-quarter filing covers the period ended March 31 and includes 564,637,307 old common shares outstanding. That count is not a prospective reorganized-equity denominator. The current analysis requires operating cash burn, secured and administrative claims, pension/labor obligations and facility-specific collateral. A fully reconciled entry-date recovery waterfall and executable DIP-loan quotation are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this pack. [1]

The comparison set is reorganizing auto suppliers and secured industrial claims, not debt-free global component manufacturers. Customer concentration, labor obligations, geographic mix and plant costs determine post-restructuring earning power. A normalized industry-volume assumption needs a bridge from current cash burn to sustainable margins. The pack does not assume an auto recovery already occurred or that every supplier receives the same government support. [1,2]

## Underwriting interpretation

Analyst distinctions, not additional reported facts: identify which assets actually generate cash, which require reinvestment, and which can be transferred independently. Contractual ownership, economic exposure and voting control need not coincide. A recognizable product or attractive asset is not sufficient evidence of attractive common-equity economics. Separate a going-concern franchise from its financing before assigning a floor. Consider what remains if the proposed event does not occur: recurring cash, realizable assets, enforceable priority or only optionality. These distinctions prevent the business description from silently turning into a recommendation.

The observable tests are customer or counterparty retention, unit profitability, required maintenance investment and the ability to fund obligations without diluting the claim being evaluated. Where consolidated reporting combines unlike activities, a segment bridge is needed before comparing margins. Missing segment or legal information is not equivalent to a zero liability. No assumption about a future transaction, governance concession or recovery is embedded in this description.
