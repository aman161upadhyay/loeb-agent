These bonds should be compared with distressed sovereign claims rather than ordinary euro-area investment-grade debt. Governing law, creditor ranking, collective-action provisions and official-sector seniority can dominate headline yield. A single historical recovery average is not sufficient for a country facing currency redenomination risk. Reuters reports many hedge funds running only 10–30% of permitted risky-asset exposure and fund-manager cash balances of 5.3%, illustrating risk aversion rather than safety. [2]

## Economic cross-check

The investment is the newly issued sovereign bond strip following the private-sector debt exchange, not old par-value claims before the haircut. Repayment depends on government revenues, spending discipline, official funding and continued monetary membership rather than corporate EBITDA. The debt documentation and the composition of creditor claims matter because the official sector's policy incentives can differ from private investors' priorities. [1,2]

The election could produce a government unwilling or unable to meet bailout conditions. Deposit flight, a shortage of funding and political deadlock could force capital controls, redenomination or another restructuring. Legal protections help define bargaining rights but cannot create euros when the sovereign lacks funding. Trading liquidity may be least available when an investor wishes to change its political-risk assessment. The imminent binary event argues for special caution on size. [1,2]

## Underwriting interpretation

Comparison discipline: align the unit being valued before applying a peer benchmark. Revenue recognition, geographic exposure, product mix, capital intensity, legal seniority and ownership of assets can all make a superficially similar multiple inappropriate. Use a segment or claim-level comparison when the group combines distinct economics. Higher margins elsewhere may identify a question worth asking; they do not prove the entire difference can be eliminated.

A credible normalization case identifies which costs change, what investment is required and whether the company has the ability to sustain the proposed earnings. Cyclical peaks should not be capitalized as perpetual growth. Equally, depressed earnings can understate an asset's potential if a specific operating or legal reset is under way. The benchmark should describe the same accounting measure and period, not a convenient headline from another date. Missing numerical comparables remain missing rather than being filled from a later investor presentation. Competitive response and transaction costs belong in the comparison.
