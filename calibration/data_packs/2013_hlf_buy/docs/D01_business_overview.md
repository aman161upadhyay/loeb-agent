Title: Herbalife Ltd. — Business, assets and economic claim
Source: https://ir.herbalife.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001157523-12-005483/0001157523-12-005483.pdf; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2013-01-08

Herbalife sells nutritional and weight-management products through an independent-distributor network. The crucial distinction is genuine retail/end-user consumption versus sales driven primarily by recruitment incentives. Accounting revenue and company profitability do not, by themselves, settle that legal/economic question. Distributor retention, purchasing behavior, inventory and the demand for nutrition products must be analyzed independently of the public controversy. [1; analyst framework]

## Economic cross-check

Third-quarter net sales were approximately $1bn, up 14%, while volume points increased 17%. Net income was $117.8m and diluted EPS $1.04, versus $108.0m and $0.87 a year earlier. Operating cash flow was $142.4m, capital expenditures $20.0m and dividends $32.4m. The company repurchased $181.9m of stock during the quarter. These figures establish a cash-generating reported business, not regulatory clearance. [1]

Other direct sellers offer operating comparisons but do not establish Herbalife's legality. Product consumption, compensation design and the economics of the typical distributor differ by company and country. A consumer-staples multiple would be inappropriate without resolving those differences. A large advertised short can create a technical catalyst, but crowded positioning is not evidence about end-user demand. Independently verified cutoff peer multiples and distributor-cohort data are unavailable. [1; analyst framework]

## Underwriting interpretation

Analyst distinctions, not additional reported facts: identify which assets actually generate cash, which require reinvestment, and which can be transferred independently. Contractual ownership, economic exposure and voting control need not coincide. A recognizable product or attractive asset is not sufficient evidence of attractive common-equity economics. Separate a going-concern franchise from its financing before assigning a floor. Consider what remains if the proposed event does not occur: recurring cash, realizable assets, enforceable priority or only optionality. These distinctions prevent the business description from silently turning into a recommendation.

The observable tests are customer or counterparty retention, unit profitability, required maintenance investment and the ability to fund obligations without diluting the claim being evaluated. Where consolidated reporting combines unlike activities, a segment bridge is needed before comparing margins. Missing segment or legal information is not equivalent to a zero liability. No assumption about a future transaction, governance concession or recovery is embedded in this description.
