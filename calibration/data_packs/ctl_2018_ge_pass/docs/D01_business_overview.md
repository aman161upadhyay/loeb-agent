Title: General Electric: Business economics and the investable event
Source: https://www.ge.com/news/press-releases/h-lawrence-culp-jr-named-chairman-and-ceo-ge ; https://www.ge.com/news/reports/ge-demonstrates-continued-progress-second-quarter-earnings
Date: 2018-10-01

## Recovered evidence

GE combines major aviation, healthcare, power and financial exposures. On October 1 the board appointed H. Lawrence Culp Jr. chairman and CEO, replacing John Flannery. A genuine bull case joins a fresh management mandate with separable franchises and a possible capital-allocation reset. The investment question is whether the value of healthy operations exceeds the claims and repair costs carried by the group. [1]


## Underwriting analysis — conditional, not management guidance

For General Electric, distinguish the operating asset from the security through which the asset is owned. The investable mechanism is Larry Culp’s appointment and a portfolio/balance-sheet reset. The existence of that mechanism does not establish a numerical expected return. Identify which customer payment reaches the enterprise, what direct expense is required to earn it, what central costs remain and which capital commitments must be funded before common shareholders receive cash. The candidate operating indicators are industrial free cash flow and segment earnings after separation costs. A rising revenue line without the associated cash and capital bridge is insufficient evidence of improving business quality.

The proposed asset support is aviation and healthcare franchises net of industrial debt, financial obligations and restructuring costs. This is a description of the potential source of recovery, not an appraisal or guaranteed minimum value. The claim hierarchy can matter as much as operating performance. Customer demand, supplier obligations, debt service and working capital must be considered together. An event-driven interpretation should explain why the mechanism changes cash flows or the market’s recognition of them, rather than relying on a favorable narrative alone.

The relevant actors are Larry Culp, GE’s board, creditors and business-unit management. Establish what each can approve, delay or veto before assigning value to a management change, operating reset or possible transaction. Any operating improvement should be measured against a stated baseline and the resources needed to obtain it. Unrecovered financial inputs remain missing; they are not assigned a zero value. An attractive franchise can still be an unattractive common security if senior claims consume its value, while a difficult operating situation can have a better expression elsewhere in the capital structure.
