## Recovered evidence

The April monthly report, published May 5, states firm assets of $19.3 billion. This is Third Point LLC firm-wide AUM, not Offshore NAV or the listed feeder’s assets. At April 30 total equity exposure was 98.1% long, 51.7% short and 46.4% net; total credit net was 34.6%. PG&E, CoStar, U.S. Steel, TSMC and Siemens Energy were top gross equity longs. These exposures are stale historical context, not a cutoff portfolio estimate. The Q2 13F disclosed August 14 provides a public baseline of 5,171,676 Soho shares. It cannot establish swaps, intraday transactions or a realized selling price. Prior public engagement can create legal and process constraints; a voting or support agreement must be read before assuming unrestricted trading. [1]


## Underwriting analysis — conditional, not management guidance

Separate the investment manager, the trading fund and any listed feeder. Firm-wide assets, Offshore net assets, reported 13F security value and a feeder’s capitalization measure different things. A reported securities table excludes much credit, foreign ordinary shares, shorts, swaps and private assets. It therefore cannot establish current cash, gross exposure, net exposure or the amount of liquidity available for a new idea. Historical exposure tables remain dated rather than rolled forward mechanically.

The opportunity under review is an independent and fair sale process for minority shareholders. Its business and financing sensitivities must be compared with the existing book, not judged in isolation. The relevant operating measures are net proceeds per minority share, process independence and competing offers. A small position in a binary or illiquid security can require more risk capital than its market value suggests. Short exposure, collateral requirements and private commitments can consume liquidity even where headline net equity exposure appears modest. Common industry labels can conceal a shared factor or refinancing sensitivity.

The relevant engagement actors are the board and special committee, controlling shareholders and outside bidders. Access to management, existing agreements, information barriers and standstills can constrain a position independently of the valuation. Do not infer authority from another shareholder’s board seat or treat a disclosed common holding as the investor’s entire economic exposure. The figures supplied above describe only what was publicly recovered by the cutoff. No subsequent trade, manager explanation or result is part of this context. Where cash, exposure, nomination windows or a precise firm AUM were not recovered, those remain open inputs rather than silently assumed capacity.
