Search, display advertising and affiliate e-commerce have different growth and capital needs. The correct comparison is net revenue and normalized operating cash flow rather than gross billings across incompatible revenue recognition. Yahoo reports nine globally leading properties and significant video engagement; those franchise assets are a counterweight to a purely terminal-decline narrative. No verified point-in-time numerical peer multiple table was recovered. [1]

## Economic cross-check

Yahoo operates a large advertising-supported internet audience across mail, news, finance, sports, search and other media. Its economic interests include Asian affiliates as well as the core operating platform. The Microsoft search agreement changes revenue presentation and shares search economics. Audience scale alone does not establish monetization strength, and affiliate value must be separated from the core advertising franchise. [1,2]

Bartz's departure may merely begin another expensive transition. Google and other online platforms can erode advertising share while core revenue stagnates. A strategic review can produce value-destructive acquisitions or fail to monetize Asian assets efficiently. Affiliate transactions can have tax and control constraints; they are not equivalent to cash already held by the parent. Replacing a CEO without fixing board accountability may leave the original problem intact. [1,2; risk analysis]

## Underwriting interpretation

Comparison discipline: align the unit being valued before applying a peer benchmark. Revenue recognition, geographic exposure, product mix, capital intensity, legal seniority and ownership of assets can all make a superficially similar multiple inappropriate. Use a segment or claim-level comparison when the group combines distinct economics. Higher margins elsewhere may identify a question worth asking; they do not prove the entire difference can be eliminated.

A credible normalization case identifies which costs change, what investment is required and whether the company has the ability to sustain the proposed earnings. Cyclical peaks should not be capitalized as perpetual growth. Equally, depressed earnings can understate an asset's potential if a specific operating or legal reset is under way. The benchmark should describe the same accounting measure and period, not a convenient headline from another date. Missing numerical comparables remain missing rather than being filled from a later investor presentation. Competitive response and transaction costs belong in the comparison.
