Title: Yahoo! Inc. — Business, assets and economic claim
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1011006/000115752311004050/a6797052-ex991.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1011006/000119312511242088/dex991.htm; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2011-09-07

Yahoo operates a large advertising-supported internet audience across mail, news, finance, sports, search and other media. Its economic interests include Asian affiliates as well as the core operating platform. The Microsoft search agreement changes revenue presentation and shares search economics. Audience scale alone does not establish monetization strength, and affiliate value must be separated from the core advertising franchise. [1,2]

## Economic cross-check

Second-quarter revenue ex-TAC was $1.076bn, down 5%; GAAP revenue was $1.229bn, down 23%, materially affected by search-agreement presentation. Operating income increased 9% to $191m. Net income was $237m and diluted EPS $0.18 versus $0.15. Excluding stated agreement and special items, revenue ex-TAC rose approximately 1%. Reported headline revenue decline is not an apples-to-apples measure of audience collapse. [1]

Search, display advertising and affiliate e-commerce have different growth and capital needs. The correct comparison is net revenue and normalized operating cash flow rather than gross billings across incompatible revenue recognition. Yahoo reports nine globally leading properties and significant video engagement; those franchise assets are a counterweight to a purely terminal-decline narrative. No verified point-in-time numerical peer multiple table was recovered. [1]

## Underwriting interpretation

Analyst distinctions, not additional reported facts: identify which assets actually generate cash, which require reinvestment, and which can be transferred independently. Contractual ownership, economic exposure and voting control need not coincide. A recognizable product or attractive asset is not sufficient evidence of attractive common-equity economics. Separate a going-concern franchise from its financing before assigning a floor. Consider what remains if the proposed event does not occur: recurring cash, realizable assets, enforceable priority or only optionality. These distinctions prevent the business description from silently turning into a recommendation.

The observable tests are customer or counterparty retention, unit profitability, required maintenance investment and the ability to fund obligations without diluting the claim being evaluated. Where consolidated reporting combines unlike activities, a segment bridge is needed before comparing margins. Missing segment or legal information is not equivalent to a zero liability. No assumption about a future transaction, governance concession or recovery is embedded in this description.
