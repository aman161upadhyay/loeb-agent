Title: FANUC Corporation — Business, assets and economic claim
Source: https://www.fanuc.co.jp/en/ir/announce/pdf/2015/financialresult201412_e.pdf; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2015-02-10

Fanuc supplies factory-automation controls, servo motors, robots and small machining centers. Installed controls, customer processes and service capability can create substantial switching costs. Manufacturing demand is cyclical even when automation penetration has a secular tailwind. Robotics, control systems and machining centers have different order patterns and should not be treated as one undifferentiated technology-growth business. [1; analyst framework]

## Economic cross-check

Nine-month sales were ¥526.406bn, up 60.9%; operating income was ¥211.910bn, up 85.4%, a calculated 40.3% sales margin. Net income was ¥149.453bn, up 93.9%, and basic EPS ¥763.82. Cash/deposits were ¥808.703bn plus ¥120bn securities, chiefly negotiable certificates of deposit. Total liabilities were ¥187.608bn, including ¥42.847bn retirement-benefit liability; the balance sheet shows no separate borrowing line. Do not describe all liabilities as debt. [1]

The comparison set includes industrial controls, robotics and machine-tool suppliers, adjusted for installed-base service and product mix. A high-quality automation franchise can deserve a premium to commoditized machinery, but not an unlimited growth multiple. Matching cash, treasury-stock treatment and cyclically normalized margins is essential. No independently verified February 10 numerical peer table is supplied in the recovered company source. [1; analyst framework]

## Underwriting interpretation

Analyst distinctions, not additional reported facts: identify which assets actually generate cash, which require reinvestment, and which can be transferred independently. Contractual ownership, economic exposure and voting control need not coincide. A recognizable product or attractive asset is not sufficient evidence of attractive common-equity economics. Separate a going-concern franchise from its financing before assigning a floor. Consider what remains if the proposed event does not occur: recurring cash, realizable assets, enforceable priority or only optionality. These distinctions prevent the business description from silently turning into a recommendation.

The observable tests are customer or counterparty retention, unit profitability, required maintenance investment and the ability to fund obligations without diluting the claim being evaluated. Where consolidated reporting combines unlike activities, a segment bridge is needed before comparing margins. Missing segment or legal information is not equivalent to a zero liability. No assumption about a future transaction, governance concession or recovery is embedded in this description.
