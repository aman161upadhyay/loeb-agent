Title: Pogo Producing Company — Business, assets and economic claim
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/230463/000110465906068244/a06-21362_1ex99d1.htm; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2007-01-31

Pogo is an exploration and production company with US and international oil and gas interests, including Northrock's western Canadian properties. Its asset base has changed through the sale of Thailand interests and acquisition of Northrock. Reported volume growth across those changes is not necessarily organic growth. Production decline, reinvestment needs and commodity mix determine reserve value more reliably than a single quarter's accounting income. [1]

## Economic cross-check

Third-quarter revenue was $353.769 million and net income $33.338 million, or $0.58 per share. Prior-year net income of $473.528 million included Thailand disposal income; excluding that sale, the comparative income was $61.903 million. Nine-month operating cash flow was $462.333 million versus $638.940 million. Accounting profit on asset sales should not be confused with sustainable operating returns. [1]

The relevant comparators are independent producers with similar reserve life, oil/gas mix and reinvestment intensity. The issuer highlights higher oil prices and lower natural gas prices; a peer whose mix differs is not an exact control. Return on acquisition capital, normalized free cash flow and organic production should accompany market returns. A reliable cutoff peer valuation table was not recovered. [1]

## Underwriting interpretation

Analyst distinctions, not additional reported facts: identify which assets actually generate cash, which require reinvestment, and which can be transferred independently. Contractual ownership, economic exposure and voting control need not coincide. A recognizable product or attractive asset is not sufficient evidence of attractive common-equity economics. Separate a going-concern franchise from its financing before assigning a floor. Consider what remains if the proposed event does not occur: recurring cash, realizable assets, enforceable priority or only optionality. These distinctions prevent the business description from silently turning into a recommendation.

The observable tests are customer or counterparty retention, unit profitability, required maintenance investment and the ability to fund obligations without diluting the claim being evaluated. Where consolidated reporting combines unlike activities, a segment bridge is needed before comparing margins. Missing segment or legal information is not equivalent to a zero liability. No assumption about a future transaction, governance concession or recovery is embedded in this description.
