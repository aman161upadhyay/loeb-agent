Title: Massey Energy Company — Business, assets and economic claim
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/37748/000119312505210164/dex991.htm; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2005-11-15

Massey produces steam, industrial and metallurgical coal in Central Appalachia using underground, surface and highwall operations. Earnings depend on contracted realizations, mine productivity, diesel and labor costs, infrastructure availability and geological conditions. A valuable reserve base can coexist with poor cash returns if production plans disappoint. The company is committing forward tonnage while industry conditions remain strong, creating a measurable operating calendar. [1]

## Economic cross-check

Third-quarter produced-coal revenue was $418.3 million, up 14% from $366.6 million. Net income was $22.5 million, or $0.28 diluted EPS, versus $2.0 million a year earlier. EBITDA rose 49% to $99.4 million from $66.6 million. However, the quarter included a $38.2 million pretax noncash reserve-exchange gain and $4.1 million favorable legal-reserve adjustment; headline improvement is not equivalent to recurring cash generation. [1]

The issuer says domestic and worldwide demand is high but supply growth is constrained by labor, infrastructure and shipping. These are industry conditions, not proof of superior company execution. A peer comparison should hold coal mix, contract roll-off and mining method constant. Steam and metallurgical prices are different exposures; a blended multiple can overstate diversification. An independently recovered numerical peer valuation table is unavailable. [1]

## Underwriting interpretation

Analyst distinctions, not additional reported facts: identify which assets actually generate cash, which require reinvestment, and which can be transferred independently. Contractual ownership, economic exposure and voting control need not coincide. A recognizable product or attractive asset is not sufficient evidence of attractive common-equity economics. Separate a going-concern franchise from its financing before assigning a floor. Consider what remains if the proposed event does not occur: recurring cash, realizable assets, enforceable priority or only optionality. These distinctions prevent the business description from silently turning into a recommendation.

The observable tests are customer or counterparty retention, unit profitability, required maintenance investment and the ability to fund obligations without diluting the claim being evaluated. Where consolidated reporting combines unlike activities, a segment bridge is needed before comparing margins. Missing segment or legal information is not equivalent to a zero liability. No assumption about a future transaction, governance concession or recovery is embedded in this description.
