Title: Massey Energy Company — Price, valuation and unfilled inputs
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/37748/000119312505210164/dex991.htm; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2005-11-15

An earnings model should separate coal-price benefit from production execution and noncash gains. No reliable November 15 close or current enterprise-value multiple was recovered; a target return therefore cannot be calculated honestly. The October quarter's $99.4 million EBITDA cannot simply be multiplied by four without removing reserve exchanges and testing production recovery. Buyback capacity is conditional on cash conversion, debt, reclamation obligations and required mine investment. [1; analyst analysis]

## Economic cross-check

Third-quarter produced-coal revenue was $418.3 million, up 14% from $366.6 million. Net income was $22.5 million, or $0.28 diluted EPS, versus $2.0 million a year earlier. EBITDA rose 49% to $99.4 million from $66.6 million. However, the quarter included a $38.2 million pretax noncash reserve-exchange gain and $4.1 million favorable legal-reserve adjustment; headline improvement is not equivalent to recurring cash generation. [1]

Management's favorable pricing outlook does not eliminate operational risk: production has already fallen despite a strong demand environment. Labor shortages, infrastructure constraints, fuel costs, legal liabilities and mine reinvestment can consume the price windfall. Borrowing to repurchase shares could amplify losses if prices normalize before productivity improves. A large coal reserve is not a liquid cash floor after reclamation and other liabilities. [1; risk analysis]

## Underwriting interpretation

Valuation discipline for this desk: purchase price, scenario value, realizable proceeds and total investment profit are four different quantities. A prospective return needs both an entry price and an achievable exit/recovery value, including taxes, fees, dilution and time. When an input is absent, an exact multiple, upside percentage or annualized return is not justified. A low nominal price is not itself a margin of safety; the claim's priority and the business's remaining cash requirements determine the floor.

The base case should not depend on perfect execution and the adverse case should not assume a favorable transaction. Component values need a consistent date, accounting scope and treatment of debt. Avoid double-counting cost savings in both higher earnings and a separate asset value. Similarly, excess cash cannot be added without checking access and associated obligations. A peer multiple is a reference, not a contractual takeout price. No later market quotation or realized outcome is substituted for a missing cutoff input.
