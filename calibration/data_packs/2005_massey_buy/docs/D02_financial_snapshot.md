Third-quarter produced-coal revenue was $418.3 million, up 14% from $366.6 million. Net income was $22.5 million, or $0.28 diluted EPS, versus $2.0 million a year earlier. EBITDA rose 49% to $99.4 million from $66.6 million. However, the quarter included a $38.2 million pretax noncash reserve-exchange gain and $4.1 million favorable legal-reserve adjustment; headline improvement is not equivalent to recurring cash generation. [1]

## Economic cross-check

Produced tons sold were 10.0 million versus 10.1 million a year earlier and 11.6 million sequentially. Revenue per ton was $41.75 versus $36.33 a year earlier. Underground production fell to about 4.7 million tons from 6 million sequentially. The issuer specifically identifies underground production as a negative earnings factor, notwithstanding strong market prices. Full-year production was estimated at 43.5 million tons. [1]

An earnings model should separate coal-price benefit from production execution and noncash gains. D04 now supplies the November 15 close and a dated valuation bridge. The October quarter's $99.4 million EBITDA cannot simply be multiplied by four without removing reserve exchanges and testing production recovery. Buyback capacity is conditional on cash conversion, debt, reclamation obligations and required mine investment. [1; analyst analysis]

## Underwriting interpretation

Accounting treatment for the desk: keep reported results, adjusted measures, cash movement and forecasts in separate columns. A disposal gain, impairment or reserve adjustment can materially change net income without changing recurring cash generation by the same amount. Consolidated earnings are not automatically distributable to the security under consideration. Check debt, taxes, pensions, minority interests and restrictions before describing an asset as surplus. A stale balance-sheet figure is not a verified current cash balance.

The critical bridge is from operating activity to cash available after maintenance investment and senior obligations. A peer margin or normalized-profit scenario is an assumption until an executor, cost base and implementation timetable support it. A fund's ownership percentage of an issuer is not its percentage of NAV. Basic-share enterprise value and diluted per-share earnings have different denominators, particularly with convertible debt; the dated calculation in D04 is not a fully diluted takeover value.
