Total revenue increased 22.2%, appreciably faster than produced-coal revenue because the revenue perimeter also includes purchased coal and other items. The $38.2m reserve-exchange gain and $4.1m legal-reserve benefit prevent treating the entire operating-margin expansion as recurring productivity improvement. These figures concern the September quarter, not financial performance after the November desk date.

Produced tons sold were 10.0 million versus 10.1 million a year earlier and 11.6 million sequentially. Revenue per ton was $41.75 versus $36.33 a year earlier. Underground production fell to about 4.7 million tons from 6 million sequentially. The issuer specifically identifies underground production as a negative earnings factor, notwithstanding strong market prices. Full-year production was estimated at 43.5 million tons. [1]

## Economic cross-check

Third-quarter produced-coal revenue was $418.3 million, up 14% from $366.6 million. Net income was $22.5 million, or $0.28 diluted EPS, versus $2.0 million a year earlier. EBITDA rose 49% to $99.4 million from $66.6 million. However, the quarter included a $38.2 million pretax noncash reserve-exchange gain and $4.1 million favorable legal-reserve adjustment; headline improvement is not equivalent to recurring cash generation. [1]

The October 27 results provide the latest operating checkpoint for this November desk date. Management is committing 2007 and 2008 tonnage at strong prices, but those deliveries and collections are still prospective. The board controls distributions, leverage and advisor appointments. An annual shareholder vote offers an accountability route, subject to nomination and governance requirements; no proxy-contest result is part of this information set. [1]

## Underwriting interpretation

Reading the update: changes in reported earnings must be attributed among volumes, prices, mix, costs, accounting and changes in the asset perimeter. A favorable headline does not establish an inflection, and an adverse headline does not prove every asset has become worthless. Management guidance is a statement of expectation, not an achieved result. The important evidence is the part of the operating bridge that can be checked at the next reporting date.

For a position depending on an event, compare operational progress with the legal or governance process rather than treating the two as interchangeable. A board review, adviser appointment, hearing or strategy announcement is not a completed transaction. Delay can consume cash or reduce expected return even when a long-term value argument survives. The absence of a recovered current metric must remain explicit: no estimate here is quietly reconstructed from a future report. The agent can reason conditionally on the available information but should not claim a fully measured operating trend when comparable data are missing.
