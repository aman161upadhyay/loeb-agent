## Recovered evidence

January 13: annual financial results. March 16: regulators welcome the banking consortium’s support; this is the desk cutoff. Next milestones are deposit stability, disclosure of funding cost and a durable balance-sheet solution. No later quarter results, government resolution, purchase agreement or depositor-loss figure is included. The rescue supports a genuine recovery scenario but is not a contractual equity catalyst. ## Underwriting analysis — conditional, not management guidance

Map each stage of the mechanism to a decision maker and an observable deliverable. The relevant actors are First Republic management, the eleven depositing banks, Treasury, Federal Reserve, FDIC and OCC. Ask which approvals are binding, which commitments are aspirational and which actions depend on financing, a regulator, a customer or a controlling shareholder. A public plan is not a completed transaction; a completed transaction is not proof that every shareholder received the same price. Distinguish operating catalysts from contractual cash-payment dates.

The event being evaluated is a $30 billion deposit placement by eleven banks to stabilize funding. A timetable should allow for implementation expenditure, delay, disclosure lag and the possibility of no resolution. The next evidence should illuminate deposit retention, funding cost, liquidity and tangible common equity under fair-value stress. Treat a future year or target date explicitly described as guidance in a pre-cutoff source as a forecast, not a subsequent fact. No actual event after this desk date is admissible merely because it fits the expected calendar.

Construct a base-case route and a failure route. In the latter, identify whether the investor can change the outcome through engagement, must rely on counterparties, or can only change the instrument and size. The adverse mechanism is that liquidity assistance protects depositors but can leave common equity exposed to asset marks and expensive replacement funding. Governance access, board representation and voting agreements can affect both leverage over management and trading freedom; they are not interchangeable. Exact legal restrictions and nomination dates must be checked in source documents rather than invented. This chronology reports only recovered evidence and does not convert a quarter-end observation into an exact trading day.
