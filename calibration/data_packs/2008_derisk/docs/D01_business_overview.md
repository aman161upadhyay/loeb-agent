Title: Third Point portfolio — Business, assets and economic claim

The object is an event-driven, multi-asset portfolio, not a single operating company. Equity longs, equity shorts, credit and merger situations can share liquidity, funding and counterparty risks despite different fundamental stories. The midyear letter describes substantial energy exposure and financial shorts. A portfolio decision must evaluate those joint exposures rather than infer safety from the nominal number of positions. [1]

## Economic cross-check

The July letter reports Offshore +6.2% in the second quarter and +1.6% year to date, with approximately $5.6bn firm AUM at June. Those observations are stale at this September cutoff. Current daily NAV, September gross and net, counterparty haircuts, investor redemption notices and a complete credit marks file are not available in the recovered contemporaneous source. Missing exposure data is a limitation, not evidence of low leverage. [1]

The relevant peer comparison is liquidity and factor risk rather than a league table of fund returns. An energy-long/financial-short book has sector-relative exposure even if dollar net is modest. Credit and risk arbitrage can add liquidity-sensitive beta. Matching portfolio exit horizons with investor withdrawal terms is an analytical requirement; a particular later risk guideline is not assumed to be in effect at this cutoff. [1; analyst analysis]

## Underwriting interpretation

Analyst distinctions, not additional reported facts: identify which assets actually generate cash, which require reinvestment, and which can be transferred independently. Contractual ownership, economic exposure and voting control need not coincide. A recognizable product or attractive asset is not sufficient evidence of attractive common-equity economics. Separate a going-concern franchise from its financing before assigning a floor. Consider what remains if the proposed event does not occur: recurring cash, realizable assets, enforceable priority or only optionality. These distinctions prevent the business description from silently turning into a recommendation.

The observable tests are customer or counterparty retention, unit profitability, required maintenance investment and the ability to fund obligations without diluting the claim being evaluated. Where consolidated reporting combines unlike activities, a segment bridge is needed before comparing margins. Missing segment or legal information is not equivalent to a zero liability. No assumption about a future transaction, governance concession or recovery is embedded in this description.
