Title: The Dow Chemical Company — Business, assets and economic claim
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/29915/000002991513000031/ex991dow09302013er.htm; internal desk state where labeled
Date: 2014-01-21

Dow spans petrochemicals, plastics, specialty materials, agricultural sciences, coatings and other end markets. The petrochemical value chain benefits from North American feedstock economics but is cyclical and capital intensive. Specialty activities have different competitive and valuation characteristics. Integration can create operating benefits, while opaque internal transfers can make it difficult for investors to assess returns by business. [1; analyst framework]

## Economic cross-check

Third-quarter sales were $13.7bn, up 1%; adjusted EPS was $0.50 versus reported EPS $0.49. EBITDA was $1.8bn and operating cash flow exceeded $1.4bn. Year-to-date operating cash flow reached $5.6bn. Gross debt fell $2.4bn year to date, and net debt/total capitalization was 34.7%. Equity earnings of $322m included meaningful joint-venture contributions and are not all wholly owned operating earnings. [1]

Integrated petrochemical peers and specialty peers are separate comparison groups. Each petrochemical chain should preserve economically important upstream/downstream relationships in a model. The current release shows different growth in agriculture, plastics and other segments; that supports analysis by business rather than proof every segment deserves the same multiple. No verified point-in-time peer margin and enterprise-value table was recovered. [1; analyst framework]

## Underwriting interpretation

Analyst distinctions, not additional reported facts: identify which assets actually generate cash, which require reinvestment, and which can be transferred independently. Contractual ownership, economic exposure and voting control need not coincide. A recognizable product or attractive asset is not sufficient evidence of attractive common-equity economics. Separate a going-concern franchise from its financing before assigning a floor. Consider what remains if the proposed event does not occur: recurring cash, realizable assets, enforceable priority or only optionality. These distinctions prevent the business description from silently turning into a recommendation.

The observable tests are customer or counterparty retention, unit profitability, required maintenance investment and the ability to fund obligations without diluting the claim being evaluated. Where consolidated reporting combines unlike activities, a segment bridge is needed before comparing margins. Missing segment or legal information is not equivalent to a zero liability. No assumption about a future transaction, governance concession or recovery is embedded in this description.
