## Recovered evidence

The April 2025 monthly report, published May 5, states firm assets of $19.3 billion. This is Third Point LLC firm-wide AUM, not Offshore NAV or the listed feeder’s assets. At April 30, 2025 total equity exposure was 98.1% long, 51.7% short and 46.4% net; total credit net was 34.6%. PG&E, CoStar, U.S. Steel, TSMC and Siemens Energy were top gross equity longs. These exposures are stale historical context, not a cutoff portfolio estimate. A new consumer-finance position would add credit and regulatory sensitivities as well as technology exposure. Latest precise NAV, gross/net and available risk capital were not recovered. [1]


## Underwriting analysis — conditional, not management guidance

Separate the investment manager, the trading fund and any listed feeder. Firm-wide assets, Offshore net assets, reported 13F security value and a feeder’s capitalization measure different things. A reported securities table excludes much credit, foreign ordinary shares, shorts, swaps and private assets. It therefore cannot establish current cash, gross exposure, net exposure or the amount of liquidity available for a new idea. Historical exposure tables remain dated rather than rolled forward mechanically.

The opportunity under review is monetization of a scaled consumer network and an organizational cost reset. Its business and financing sensitivities must be compared with the existing book, not judged in isolation. The relevant operating measures are gross profit, transacting actives, lending losses and operating leverage. A small position in a binary or illiquid security can require more risk capital than its market value suggests. Short exposure, collateral requirements and private commitments can consume liquidity even where headline net equity exposure appears modest. Common industry labels can conceal a shared factor or refinancing sensitivity.

The relevant engagement actors are Jack Dorsey, Amrita Ahuja, the board, banking regulators, merchants and consumers. Access to management, existing agreements, information barriers and standstills can constrain a position independently of the valuation. Do not infer authority from another shareholder’s board seat or treat a disclosed common holding as the investor’s entire economic exposure. The figures supplied above describe only what was publicly recovered by the cutoff. No subsequent trade, manager explanation or result is part of this context. Where cash, exposure, nomination windows or a precise firm AUM were not recovered, those remain open inputs rather than silently assumed capacity.
